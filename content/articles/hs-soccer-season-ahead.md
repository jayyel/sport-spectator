If you have been looking for Miami-Dade high school soccer standings this week, there are none, and there is a straightforward reason: the FHSAA soccer season does not begin until November. Regular season play runs through January, districts follow, and the state finals land in February.

That timing is unusual nationally and completely normal here. Florida plays soccer in the one stretch of the year when the weather cooperates, which means the county's best players spend September and October in club competition rather than in school colors.

So rather than invent a table that does not exist, here is what is actually worth tracking between now and the first whistle.

## The calendar

Practice opens in the fall, matches begin in November, and the compressed schedule means teams often play two or three times a week through December and January. District tournaments arrive in late January, regionals immediately after, and the state championships are held in February. From first match to final, it is roughly a hundred days.

For families new to Florida, that compression matters. There is very little margin for a slow start. Programs that lose two of their first four are frequently playing for seeding rather than position by the time the calendar turns.

## What Miami-Dade soccer actually is

The talent level in this county is not a secret to anyone who follows the sport. Miami-Dade produces professional players, and the high school game sits in a complicated relationship with the club and academy system that develops most of them. Some of the county's best 17-year-olds will not play a high school match at all. Others will, and the difference in any given district often comes down to which programs kept their club players.

That is the honest frame for the season ahead: high school soccer in South Florida is played at a genuinely high standard, and it is played by a roster that is partly determined by decisions made elsewhere.
