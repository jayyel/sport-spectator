Lionel Messi announced on Monday that he has retired from the Argentina national team, ending 21 years of international football that began when he was 18 years old and ended in a stadium in North America at 39.

The detail that matters most is buried in the announcement itself: he wrote it two days after Argentina's 1-0 extra-time loss to Spain in the World Cup final on July 19. He did not post it. In the weeks that followed, his father and agent, Jorge Messi, died. When he finally published the message on Monday, he said the intervening time had left him more certain than ever.

He described the decision as one that hurt and still hurts, but said he understood the moment had arrived.

## The career, without the argument

For most of it, Messi was the greatest player alive and the man who could not win with his country. He lost the 2014 World Cup final to Germany and Copa América finals in 2007, 2015 and 2016. After the 2016 defeat on penalties to Chile he announced his international retirement on the spot, in an emotional interview in New Jersey, and reversed it weeks later.

Then everything arrived at once: Copa América in 2021, the World Cup in 2022, another Copa América in 2024, and a run to the final again this summer. He leaves with six World Cups played, one won, and two runners-up medals separated by twelve years.

Argentina's federation has said the No. 10 shirt will be retired in his honor.

## What it means here

Nothing about Monday changes Messi's club situation, and that is precisely why it matters in Miami.

He is 39. He has just removed the single largest competing demand on his body — the international windows, the transatlantic flights, the tournaments that consumed his summers. Everything that remains goes to Inter Miami.

He is currently leading MLS in scoring with 18 goals. Inter Miami sit second in the Eastern Conference. Chase Stadium is now the only place in the world where you can watch him play a competitive match.

There is a version of this story that is only about loss, and Argentine fans are entitled to tell it that way. From here it reads differently. The last chapter of the best career in the sport's history is going to be written entirely in South Florida, and we get to cover it.
