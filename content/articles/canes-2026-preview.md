There is no version of the 2026 Miami Hurricanes season that gets graded on wins alone. The program went 13-3 last year and lost the national championship game to Indiana. It enters this season ranked seventh and picked to win the ACC. Anything short of a return trip to the playoff will feel like regression, which is a strange and privileged problem for a program that spent most of two decades trying to become relevant again.

Mario Cristobal has spent four years assembling exactly this: a roster with no obvious hole and a schedule that gives it room to prove the point.

## The offense is the strength, and the reason is new

Darian Mensah takes over at quarterback, and the receiving room he inherits is the most talented Miami has had in years. Malachi Toney is a genuine problem for defensive coordinators — he is dangerous on punt returns and impossible to leave in single coverage — and Cooper Barkate gives the offense a second player who can win on his own. Charmar Brown carries the run game.

The question mark is up front. Miami lost first-round talent from the offensive line and the group has to prove it can create movement against ACC fronts that will load the box specifically to make this a one-dimensional offense. If the Hurricanes can run it in November, they are a playoff team. If they cannot, they are a very good team that loses one it should not have.

## The defense reloads rather than rebuilds

Rueben Bain Jr. and Akheem Mesidor are in the NFL. That is real attrition off a unit that carried Miami through stretches of last season. What returns is still formidable: Ahmad Moten Sr. anchoring the interior, Damon Wilson II rushing off the edge, Xavier Lucas at corner and Bryce Fitzgerald at safety.

The bet here is that Miami's recruiting has reached the point where losing two stars costs you a step rather than a level. Most of the country would take that trade.

## Where the season turns

The schedule opens gently — at Stanford, then Florida A&M at home — before Wake Forest on the road and Central Michigan. October is where it gets decided. Clemson at Memorial Stadium on October 3, Florida State at Hard Rock on the 17th, Pittsburgh the following week, then North Carolina on the road on Halloween.

That Clemson trip is the one to circle. Miami has lost games exactly like it in seasons exactly like this, and the difference between a program that is good and one that finishes is what happens on a hostile Saturday in early October when the offense is not clicking.

Everything else about this team suggests it is ready. The last step is the one nobody can preview.
