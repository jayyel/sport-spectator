Miami has never been ranked No. 1 in the College Football Playoff era. That changes nothing about tonight's assignment: go into Notre Dame Stadium at 7:30 and beat a team that cannot afford to lose.

The Hurricanes are 8-0, 6-0 in the ACC, and sit atop the first CFP rankings of the season — a first for the program since the committee started releasing rankings in 2014. It's also the first time Miami has been No. 1 in any major poll since December 8, 2002, a drought that covers nearly a quarter-century of mediocrity, coaching changes and bowl-game afterthoughts. The team that once treated the top spot as a birthright hasn't sniffed it since the Big East days.

Notre Dame, at 7-1, comes in ranked 11th — a startling gap between the committee's view of the Irish and where most keep them in the AP poll. The reason is simple: Notre Dame <cite index="22-9">doesn't have a victory against a team that is currently ranked, and none of its final three opponents after Miami is in the committee's top 25</cite>. That makes tonight close to an elimination game for the Irish. A loss likely ends their playoff hopes for a second straight year.

## The Mensah Problem, Without His Top Target

The matchup inside the matchup is Darian Mensah against a Notre Dame defense that ranks fifth nationally in pass defense. Mensah leads the nation in a stat line that's hard to argue with: he's completing <cite index="23-10">80.7% of his passes with 202.7 pass efficiency, tied for the national lead with 25 touchdown passes against one interception</cite>. He's also doing it as a co-favorite for the Heisman Trophy.

What he won't have is Malachi Toney, the sophomore receiver responsible for 13 of those 25 touchdown throws. Toney needs ankle surgery and is out for tonight's game, a loss that reshapes Miami's passing attack on its biggest stage of the year. Notre Dame will also be shorthanded on defense — linebacker Drayk Bowen, the team's leading tackler, is out along with the green-dot helmet that relays defensive calls, according to CBS Sports' matchup breakdown.

The absences matter because of how lopsided this Miami offense has otherwise been. Running back Mark Fletcher Jr. became the program's all-time leader in combined rushing and receiving touchdowns during last week's 38-35 win over North Carolina, scoring three times to push his career total to 38. If Toney's absence slows the passing game, Fletcher becomes the pressure valve.

## A Program That Knows What Happens Next

Miami fans have reason for caution that goes beyond this specific roster. The Hurricanes' last trip to the No. 1 ranking, in 2002, ended in a national championship game loss to Ohio State in double overtime. The program's other brushes with the top spot — 1984, 1986 — both ended with losses that live on in Miami lore for the wrong reasons. The history isn't encouraging for teams that get there and then have to defend it on the road.

It also matters that this exact matchup already happened once this year. Miami beat Notre Dame 27-24 in the 2025 season opener, and the Irish still finished ahead of the Hurricanes in five of six CFP rankings last season before the committee flipped them in the final week. That flip helped push Miami into the 12-team bracket and knocked Notre Dame out. The two programs have unfinished business, and the committee's early positioning of Notre Dame at 11 — well below where most other polls have them — suggests this game will do more to settle the argument than the rankings have so far.

Vegas numbers aside, the practical stakes are plain: a Miami win keeps the Hurricanes undefeated with three winnable games left on the schedule and all but locks a playoff bid. A loss puts Miami right back in the pack it spent a month trying to escape, with Georgia, Utah and others waiting to pounce on the vacancy at the top.
