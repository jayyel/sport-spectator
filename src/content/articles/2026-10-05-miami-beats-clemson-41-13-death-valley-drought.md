Miami hadn't won at Clemson's Memorial Stadium since 2010. That ended Saturday night, and it wasn't close. <cite index="20-6">Prior to Saturday night's game, Miami had not won at Memorial Stadium since 2010.</cite> The Hurricanes left Death Valley with a 41-13 win that was never in doubt after the first quarter.

The scar tissue here is real. <cite index="25-1">For Miami coach Mario Cristobal, the win was especially meaningful because the last time the Hurricanes played at Clemson four years ago, they lost 40-10.</cite> Cristobal didn't dress it up afterward. <cite index="25-2">"We got our faces kicked in," Cristobal said of the result in 2022.</cite> Clemson came into Saturday as a sizable home underdog — <cite index="30-18">a 17 1/2-point home underdog, the largest point spread in the Dabo Swinney era</cite> — and still Miami's margin felt generous to the Tigers.

## Mensah's Heisman case keeps building

Darian Mensah didn't throw for huge numbers, but he was efficient and decisive. <cite index="30-10,30-11">Darian Mensah threw for 227 yards and a touchdown and had a 34-yard scoring run as No. 4 Miami routed Clemson 41-13 on Saturday night to seize sole possession of first place in the Atlantic Coast Conference.</cite> Cristobal was blunt about what he saw from his transfer quarterback. <cite index="30-12">"I thought he had awesome numbers, both with his arms and legs," Miami coach Mario Cristobal said about Mensah.</cite>

The bigger number is the streak. <cite index="30-15">Mensah has quickly climbed into the Heisman Trophy conversation after a fast start to the season with 15 touchdown passes in his first five games.</cite> He extended his run of games with a touchdown pass to 23 straight dating back through his time at Duke and Tulane, a streak that keeps his name in the national conversation even on a night his arm numbers were modest. Mark Fletcher Jr. did the heavy lifting on the ground, picking up 77 yards and two touchdowns on 16 carries, and Cooper Barkate led the receivers with five catches for 98 yards.

## The defense carried a banged-up roster

If Mensah is the headline, the defense was the actual story. Clemson's run game never got going. <cite index="34-10,34-11,34-12">Clemson had 55 yards rushing on 31 attempts, for an average of 1.8 yards per rush, and Miami's rush defense continues to be the best in the country. The Canes also forced two turnovers, with one being a pick-six.</cite> That pick-six came from a freshman who had no business being on the field healthy. Cristobal pointed to linebacker JJ Dunnigan's touchdown return as the kind of play that defines the season Miami is having — a banged-up roster finding ways to make winning plays anyway.

That's not spin. <cite index="21-1">The Miami Hurricanes enter their bye week undefeated but dealing with several injuries after beating Clemson 41-13.</cite> Cristobal spent much of his Monday session crediting the offensive line's fourth-quarter control and Damon Wilson's pass rush while <cite index="21-4">emphasizing that Miami remains far from its desired standard before facing Florida State</cite> after the bye.

## Why this one is different

Miami has now beaten Clemson three straight times under Cristobal, a stretch that includes a double-overtime classic at Hard Rock Stadium in 2023. This one didn't need overtime. It didn't need a signature moment, either — just a defense that strangled the run and a quarterback who protected the ball when it mattered.

The standings reflect it. <cite index="27-5">The Hurricanes remain undefeated on the season at 5-0 — one of just 13 power conference teams (plus Notre Dame) with that distinction — and unblemished at 3-0 in ACC play.</cite> It's also <cite index="20-3">the Hurricanes' third straight season with a 5-0 start</cite>, which says less about any single opponent and more about where this program now sits week to week. Three years ago, a 5-0 start was a surprise. Now it's the baseline, and beating Clemson on the road isn't the exception — it's the proof that the baseline is real.

On the other sideline, the loss adds to a different kind of pressure. <cite index="20-7">Under Cristobal the "U" is back on the national stage and Swinney is faced with questions about his job security.</cite> That's not Miami's problem to solve. The Hurricanes have a bye week to get healthy, and Florida State is next. Given how this month has gone, that's a matchup Miami should be favored to handle too.
