The Heat beat the Pelicans 128-118 at Kaseya Center on Thursday night to improve to 2-0 in the preseason, and Giannis Antetokounmpo did it in 18 minutes. He finished with 18 points on 8-of-13 shooting, four rebounds and four assists, sitting the entire second half because it's October and none of this counts yet.

Afterward, he said he's still not comfortable.

"I'm a little bit more comfortable, but I'm not there yet. I've played with the Bucks 900+ games, played with the Heat two…I think it takes a little bit more time," <cite index="32-7,32-8">Antetokounmpo told reporters, adding that he's still slowly figuring out his spots offensively.</cite> That's two preseason games into a partnership the Heat gave up two former first-round picks, a Sixth Man of the Year runner-up and a former All-Star guard to build.

## A Lopsided Win That Wasn't the Point

The box score made Thursday look like a laugher. <cite index="54-4,54-5">Antetokounmpo finished with a game-high 18 points as the Heat beat a young but healthy Pelicans team 128-118, holding New Orleans to 44% shooting while shooting 47% themselves.</cite> <cite index="54-6,54-7">Miami's offense was surprisingly coherent for a team still learning each other, turning the ball over just 11 times to New Orleans' 22 while racking up 36 assists as a group.</cite> Andrew Wiggins added 13 points and Tim Hardaway Jr. chipped in 11, and <cite index="55-11">Davion Mitchell ran the attack with nine points and seven assists against a single turnover as Miami scored 73 points in the first 24 minutes.</cite>

None of that is supposed to matter in preseason. But it's the context around the numbers that matters here: Miami gave up a historic haul to get this player, and it needs the fit to work fast given how much the roster changed to make room for him.

The trade itself, completed in June, was one of the biggest in league history. <cite index="41-5,41-6,41-7">The Bucks received Tyler Herro, Kel'el Ware, Jaime Jaquez Jr., Kasparas Jakučionis and three first-round picks, including the No. 13 overall pick, while the Heat also received veteran forward Bobby Portis from Milwaukee.</cite> <cite index="42-6">The last time any Heat player finished higher than 10th in MVP voting was 2013-14, when LeBron James was the runner-up</cite> — which is to say, this is the first time since the LeBron era that Miami has had a player of this caliber walking into the building.

## Bam Is Already Sold, Even If the Fit Isn't Finished

Bam Adebayo, who shares an agent with Antetokounmpo and will be asked to coexist with him in the frontcourt for years, isn't waiting around for comfort. Asked about the Heat's offensive questions heading into the real season, he pointed to last year's team — the one that didn't have a second superstar.

"I mean, last year, we led the league in 140-point games and that's with no Giannis Antetokounmpo. But we see the potential," <cite index="31-11,31-12">Adebayo said, with a laugh, about the Heat's offensive upside.</cite>

That's the bet Miami is making: that two non-shooting bigs who both want the ball in space can coexist well enough to turn flashes into a finished product. So far, the early returns support it more than the quotes suggest. <cite index="31-6,31-7">Antetokounmpo has looked comfortable in the Heat offense, which is still using cutting and movement to create pace, setting picks early in sets while the team's defense creates the turnovers that let it get out and run.</cite>

Erik Spoelstra has treated these two games as building blocks rather than auditions, capping Antetokounmpo's minutes and resting him for entire halves even in a game Miami controlled. That's standard preseason management for a 31-year-old two-time MVP who logged more than 900 games in Milwaukee. But it also means the league hasn't actually seen what this version of the Heat looks like with Antetokounmpo playing starter's minutes against a team trying to win.

The Heat's actual answer on whether this works arrives October 21, when the games start counting. Until then, the numbers from two blowout wins and the quotes from the two stars involved are telling slightly different stories — one says this is already working, the other says to wait. Both are probably right at once.
