The Miami Dolphins are 0-2, and the easy story is the new quarterback. It's the wrong story. <cite index="87-6,87-7">Miami has allowed nine sacks in two games, the most in the NFL, and quarterback Malik Willis has a 43.1% pressure rate, the fifth-highest in the league, according to Pro Football Focus.</cite>

That's the number that should worry Dolphins fans heading into Sunday's home opener, not anything about Willis himself. <cite index="90-20,90-21">Willis' play hasn't been a problem. He has been solid, throwing for 417 yards, one touchdown and one interception.</cite> <cite index="86-8">He's added 39 scramble yards and a rushing touchdown.</cite> The issue is what's happening around him.

## The Right Tackle Is the Problem, Not the Passer

<cite index="87-15,87-16">Kadyn Proctor and Jonah Savaiinaea have shown flashes, but right tackle Austin Jackson has been one of the league's worst linemen so far. He has surrendered nine pressures and two sacks through the first two weeks, putting his starting job in jeopardy.</cite> The front office already has contingency plans. <cite index="87-17,87-18">The Dolphins could move Proctor to right tackle, which could ultimately be their long-term plan, but they could give Caedan Wallace a chance to be the team's starting right tackle before deciding to move Proctor, who played tackle in college at Alabama.</cite>

Proctor isn't hiding from the criticism. <cite index="90-5,90-6,90-7">"I've never started 0-2, but we're going against some great defenses," Proctor said Monday. "Like I said, it's just the start. It's a long season."</cite>

Coach Jeff Hafley confirmed after Monday's film review that changes are being discussed, though he stopped short of naming names. Speaking on whether lineup changes are coming, <cite index="77-7">Hafley said: "I'd be lying to you if I didn't say there were those conversations."</cite> Asked more broadly about the state of the roster, <cite index="82-6,82-7,82-8">Hafley acknowledged that conversations about personnel changes are indeed happening behind the scenes, saying: "I think we have to make sure that there's constant competition. I think there's certain positions where I'd be lying to you if I didn't say that there was those conversations."</cite>

## Change Has Already Started at Safety

One position group has already turned over. <cite index="82-9,82-10,82-11">The safety room is one place where change has already arrived. Zayne Anderson ceded the vast majority of his snaps in Week 2, and Miami went to a pair of younger options in Michael Taaffe, a fifth-round rookie, and Dante Trader, a second-year pro.</cite> That's the model for how the next round of changes could unfold — not a press-conference announcement, but snaps quietly shifting to whoever's playing better.

The injury report complicates the picture further. <cite index="76-1,76-2">Dolphins receiver Caleb Douglas' status for Sunday's game against the Chiefs is in question because of an ankle injury, and Hafley said it's too early to tell if Douglas will be able to play in the home opener.</cite> <cite index="76-6,76-7,76-8,76-9,76-10,76-11">If Douglas cannot play, that could mean an opportunity for veteran Jalen Tolbert, who has been inactive for the first two games. Hafley said he recently told Tolbert how much he appreciates how he's accepting his role, adding that his time is going to come.</cite> On defense, <cite index="80-8,80-9,80-10">edge rusher Chop Robinson missed the 49ers game after suffering a concussion in practice, but Hafley said Robinson has made progress through the NFL's concussion protocol and is "exactly where we thought he'd be right now."</cite>

## Sunday Is the Real Test

None of this gets easier against Kansas City. <cite index="79-2,79-3,79-4,79-5">The Chiefs are feeling good getting off to an undefeated start after last year's 6-11 debacle, and a loss to the 0-2 Dolphins would be a major momentum halter for them, while Miami will be desperate to get its first win of the season and avoid a dreaded 0-3 start.</cite> <cite index="79-13,79-14">It will be Kansas City's first road game of the season after starting 2-0 with back-to-back home primetime wins.</cite>

If Jackson struggles again behind an offensive line already surrendering pressure at the league's highest clip, the Dolphins won't need three weeks to make a decision — they'll need one half. Willis has shown he can play through contact. What he can't do is create time that isn't there. Whether Miami fixes its line before Sunday, or waits until Jackson gets beaten again on national television, says more about this coaching staff than any quarterback evaluation ever could.
