Inter Miami beat Cruz Azul 2-0 on Wednesday night at Nu Stadium to win the Campeones Cup, and it should have been a clean, feel-good story. New coach wins in his first game. Messi scores his 100th goal for the club. The Herons snap a nasty form slump with their first clean sheet in 12 matches. Instead, the story out of Miami on Thursday morning is Messi hunting down MLS commissioner Don Garber on the trophy stage to give him an earful.

Cameras caught it twice. Garber, all smiles for the ceremony, found himself on the receiving end of an animated Messi pointing fingers and shaking his head, then walking off, then coming back to do it again. <cite index="43-6,43-7">He went on to hug and celebrate with his supporters before returning to repeat the action with Garber, all while speaking animatedly to him, and had to be taken aside by Luis Suarez, as they continued with their celebration.</cite> Neither side has said a word publicly about what was actually said.

## A Game That Deserved Better Headlines

Strip away the Garber moment and this was as good a night as Inter Miami has had all year. <cite index="51-3">Goals from captain Leo Messi, who made history in the process by becoming the first Inter Miami player to reach 100 goals, and midfielder Casemiro secured the trophy for the team this evening at Nu Stadium.</cite> Messi opened the scoring in the 24th minute, <cite index="43-14">heading home a cross from Luis Suarez to give his side the lead,</cite> and then set up the clincher late. <cite index="51-9">In the 81st minute, Messi whipped in a corner from the left, followed by a towering header from Casemiro to double Inter Miami's advantage.</cite>

The win also doubled as a debut party for Cristian "Kily" González, the club's third head coach of the calendar year. <cite index="51-4">Additionally, the championship match also presented a winning debut for new head coach Cristian "Kily" González in his first match at the helm.</cite> González inherited a team that had gone ice cold. <cite index="58-1">The coaching change comes during a five-match winless streak, with the club recording four losses and one draw between MLS regular season and Leagues Cup play.</cite> Before Wednesday, Miami hadn't kept a clean sheet in three months of play. One night in, that problem looks solved, at least for now.

## Why Messi and Garber Aren't Friends

This wasn't a random flare-up. There's a paper trail. <cite index="39-6">Garber revealed in a July 2026 interview that his and Messi's relationship has been strained since Garber handed him a one-game suspension for skipping the 2025 MLS All-Star Game, and that he believes Messi holds him accountable for issues with referees in the MLS.</cite> Wednesday's final gave Messi fresh material on that front. <cite index="39-8">Miami's 2-0 win over Cruz Azul in the 2026 Campeones Cup final had some tense moments, especially as Messi protested multiple decisions in the first half.</cite> Mexican outlets covering the match noted him arguing a non-call near the box in the second half as well.

The context that makes this more than a one-off tantrum: Garber is on his way out. <cite index="66-1,66-2">Berg will succeed longtime commissioner Don Garber, who has been the MLS commissioner since 1999, and per the league, Garber will stay commissioner through the 2026 season and will become league chairman in 2027.</cite> So this is likely one of the last times these two share a stage in these roles, and it still went sideways. That says something about how the league's biggest star actually feels about the league office, independent of results on the field.

## What Actually Matters Here

The soccer version of this story is straightforward: Inter Miami is trending up after a miserable stretch, González looks like he can steady the group, and Messi remains capable of deciding finals by himself at age 39. <cite index="43-11,43-12">Lionel Messi became the fastest player to reach 100 goals for a team in the MLS after he scored once in Inter Miami's 2-0 win over Cruz Azul in the Campeones Cup, needing just 114 games to score a century of goals for the Herons.</cite>

But the Garber moment is the one with legs, because it's not really about one bad handshake. It's a reminder that MLS's relationship with the player who transformed its relevance is still, after three years, an uneasy one — and that Messi isn't shy about making that visible on a stage built for celebration. Miami hosts San Diego FC on Sunday. Nobody will be talking about the lineup.
