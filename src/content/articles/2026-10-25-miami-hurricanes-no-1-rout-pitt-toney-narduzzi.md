Miami hadn't been the No. 1 team in the country in nearly 24 years. In its first game carrying that ranking, it didn't blink. The Hurricanes beat Pittsburgh 52-23 at Hard Rock Stadium on Saturday, and it wasn't competitive after halftime — Miami led 31-7 at the break and never trailed.

The win pushed Miami to 7-0 and 5-0 in the ACC. It also came on a day the program brought back members of its 2001 national championship team, with Ed Reed, Clinton Portis, Jeremy Shockey and Andre Johnson among the alumni on hand to watch the current roster play in throwback uniforms. The symbolism wasn't subtle, and for one afternoon the current group matched it.

## Toney's record-breaking day

Sophomore receiver Malachi Toney caught 16 passes for 194 yards and three touchdowns, breaking the single-game school reception record he had tied against Pitt a year earlier. The previous mark of 13, set by Mike Harley in 2021, had stood until Toney matched it last November and then shattered it Saturday — he caught the first 16 passes thrown his way before finally dropping an incompletion late. By halftime, Toney alone had <cite index="39-9,39-10">159 receiving yards, matching Pitt's total offense through the first two quarters.</cite>

The performance pushed Toney past 2,000 career receiving yards, making him the 11th player in Miami history to reach that mark. Quarterback Darian Mensah finished 31-of-37 for 387 yards and four touchdowns, and running back Javian Mallory added two rushing scores while Mark Fletcher Jr. moved into second place on Miami's career rushing touchdown list. None of it was particularly close. Pitt managed only 403 total yards against a Miami defense that had forced a three-and-out on the game's opening possession.

## A handshake with a backstory

The football was never really in doubt, which is part of why the postgame scene at midfield got as much attention as the game itself. Cristobal and Narduzzi met for the customary handshake, and cameras caught what multiple outlets described as an extended, pointed exchange of backslaps that ended with Cristobal appearing to give Narduzzi an extra shove as they separated. Fans spent the rest of the night arguing over the clip.

The tension didn't come from nowhere. Back in July, at ACC Kickoff, Narduzzi had publicly accused Miami of trying to pull Pitt quarterback Mason Heintschel into the transfer portal in the final hours before it closed. <cite index="54-4">"Miami tried to get Mason Thursday before (the portal closed Friday), and we were able to hang on to him and shut them down," Narduzzi told The Athletic.</cite> He said he'd then warned Duke coach Manny Diaz that Miami would come for Darian Mensah next — which is exactly what happened, with Mensah eventually landing in Coral Gables after a legal dispute between Duke and Miami over his contract got settled.

Narduzzi kept the story alive into fight week, telling reporters this past Monday that if nobody polices tampering in the portal, programs will keep doing what they want. The Hurricanes didn't need to say much back. Heintschel, the player at the center of the whole saga, has missed Pitt's last several games with an injury, and the Panthers have now dropped three straight.

## What actually matters here

The handshake is a sideshow. The real story is that Miami, with its first real shot at a national championship since the Larry Coker era, played like a No. 1 team instead of flinching under the weight of the ranking. Teams that get to the top of the polls and then look over their shoulder don't usually blow out a five-win Pitt team by 29. Miami didn't look over its shoulder.

What's left is the schedule. The Hurricanes go to North Carolina next, then face a Notre Dame team on the road in November before closing with Duke, Virginia Tech and Boston College at home. None of those is an obvious trap game on paper, but neither was Louisville, and Miami needed two overtimes to beat Florida State the week before this one. The margin for error at No. 1 is thinner than the scoreboard from Saturday suggests. For one game, though, Miami looked like exactly what the ranking says it is.
