Miami hadn't won at Clemson's Memorial Stadium since 2010. Saturday night, the Hurricanes didn't just win there — they made it look routine.

No. 4 Miami beat Clemson 41-13 in Death Valley, scoring on the first play from scrimmage and never trailing. <cite index="21-2,21-3">Darian Mensah threw for 227 yards and a touchdown and had a 34-yard scoring run as No. 4 Miami routed Clemson 41-13 to seize sole possession of first place in the Atlantic Coast Conference.</cite> <cite index="21-4">Miami outgained Clemson on the ground 192-55.</cite>

The win pushed Miami to 5-0 and 3-0 in ACC play, <cite index="25-10">and the Hurricanes head into their off week atop the conference standings.</cite> It's also the program's third straight season starting 5-0 under Mario Cristobal — proof the rebuild has stabilized, even if the bigger tests are still ahead.

## A drought that said everything about the last decade

The scoreline matters less than what it ended. <cite index="50-6,50-7">Death Valley hasn't been particularly kind to the Hurricanes in years past — heading into this primetime showdown at Clemson, Miami hadn't won at Memorial Stadium since 2010, its last two visits in 2020 and 2022 ending in disappointment.</cite>

That drought wasn't close. From 2015 to 2022, as Clemson built a run of national championships and Miami searched for an identity, <cite index="56-1">the Tigers outscored Miami 178-30 across four matchups.</cite> The low point came in 2015, when Clemson blew out the Hurricanes 58-0 in Death Valley, and again in the 2017 ACC title game, when Clemson won 38-3. Those were the version of this rivalry Miami fans got used to swallowing.

Saturday flipped it. Mensah found Cooper Barkate for a 61-yard touchdown on Miami's first offensive snap, and the Hurricanes controlled the game from there. Barkate finished with five catches for 98 yards, while Clemson's freshman quarterback Tait Reynolds put up gaudy numbers in garbage time — <cite index="29-2">completing 29-of-44 passes for 295 yards, all career highs</cite> — that didn't change the outcome. Mark Fletcher Jr. added a rushing score, and a JJ Dunnigan interception return for a touchdown put the final margin out of reach.

Cristobal, not known for showing much after wins, didn't hide how much this one meant. Speaking with Joe Rose on WQAM afterward, he pointed to the physical toll the previous trips to Clemson had taken on this program and credited his players for not flinching in a hostile building that's historically eaten Miami alive.

## The bye week before the real exam

Miami won't play again until October 17, against Florida State in the Florida Cup, giving banged-up starters time to heal — right tackle Matthew McCoy and guard Max Buchanan both left Saturday's game with injuries, though McCoy returned. But the schedule is building toward one date everyone in the building already knows by heart: November 7, at Notre Dame.

The Fighting Irish are also 5-0 after beating North Carolina 37-26 on the road, <cite index="42-4">a result that extended Notre Dame's winning streak to 15 games and made it the best start of the Marcus Freeman era.</cite> Both teams have avoided any letdown so far, and the ACC and the sport are circling the Notre Dame Stadium date as the one most likely to carry College Football Playoff weight. It will be <cite index="40-11">the first night meeting between the programs at Notre Dame Stadium since 1984.</cite>

That history runs both directions. Miami's last trip to South Bend as an underdog program produced one of the sport's most famous finishes — the 1988 "Catholics vs. Convicts" game, decided on a batted-down two-point conversion. The stakes this time are different: two unbeaten, ranked teams with real playoff ambitions, not a rivalry renewal.

Before any of that, Miami still has North Carolina, Pittsburgh and a home date with Florida State to navigate. None of those should scare a team that just ended a 16-year losing streak in one of the sport's most hostile stadiums. But Death Valley was supposed to be the hard part. South Bend is shaping up to be harder.
