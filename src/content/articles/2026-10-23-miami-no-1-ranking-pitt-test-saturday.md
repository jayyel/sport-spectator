Miami hosts Pittsburgh at noon Saturday carrying something the program hasn't carried since 2002: the No. 1 ranking in the country. The Hurricanes <cite index="20-2,20-3">are ranked No. 1 in the AP Top 25 Poll for the first time since 2002</cite>, a 24-year gap that covers four head coaches, two stadium names and an entire generation of Miami fans who've never seen this.

It didn't come from Miami blowing teams off the field. It came from <cite index="21-7">five of the top seven teams losing in one week</cite>, something that had never happened in the poll's 90-year history. Miami beat Florida State in double overtime and got out of the way while everyone else collapsed. One writer put it bluntly: the Hurricanes became No. 1 <cite index="21-10,21-11">"by default," a fair description of a 6-0 team that survived while everyone around it stumbled</cite>.

## Cristobal isn't interested in the number

Mario Cristobal spent his Monday press conference trying to drain the ranking of any meaning. Asked about sitting atop the polls, he said <cite index="49-7,49-8,49-9,49-10,49-11,49-12">"We are at the top of what? Last I checked, there has never been a trophy or parade for anybody halfway through the season... We like being the hunter. We like being the alpha. We don't believe in all that BS that comes with predictions and rankings. Let's just shut our mouths and go get better."</cite>

That's partly coach-speak, but it's also accurate history. The last time Miami held this ranking, <cite index="49-5,49-6">the Hurricanes were No. 1 in the 2002 season, before they lost to Ohio State in the national championship game</cite>. The number hasn't meant much to this program in the past — it's what happens in December and January that counts, and Miami knows that better than most.

There's also a cautionary tale sitting right in front of them. <cite index="26-16,26-17,26-18,26-19">The last time the top two teams in the rankings lost in the same week in the regular season was 2017, when No. 1 Alabama lost to Auburn and No. 2 Miami lost to Pittsburgh on Thanksgiving weekend. The last time unranked teams knocked off a top-ranked team in consecutive weeks was 1990.</cite> Pitt is the team standing between Miami and extending that streak of near-misses into a third week.

## Pitt isn't who it was a month ago

The Panthers arrive at 5-2 after consecutive losses, and they'll be without their starting quarterback. Pitt <cite index="48-6">arrives after consecutive losses to North Carolina and Boston College and will play without starting quarterback Mason Heintschel, who is out for the season with a torn ACL</cite>. That's a significant subtraction for a team that still ranks among the ACC's better offenses — Pitt <cite index="48-3">ranks fourth in the ACC in total offense at 450.1 yards per game</cite> even with the shuffle at quarterback.

Miami's own numbers explain why the offense has carried this team to 6-0. Darian Mensah <cite index="48-2">has thrown for 1,764 yards and 18 touchdowns with one interception</cite> through six games, and the Hurricanes <cite index="48-1">have outscored opponents 291-84 through six games</cite>. Mensah's protection issues have been the one real wrinkle, and Pitt presents a specific problem there — the Panthers rank near the bottom nationally against the pass but <cite index="54-2">boast the nation's top run defense but rank 115th against the pass</cite>, which should tell Miami's coaching staff exactly where to attack.

The history between these programs adds a layer, too. This is the 44th meeting in a series Miami dominates overall, but Pitt has had Miami's number recently — <cite index="40-7">the Panthers have won two of their past five visits to Miami, including a 42-16 victory in their most recent trip in 2022</cite>. A repeat Saturday would be unprecedented in the modern era: <cite index="40-9">a win would give the Panthers consecutive road victories over Miami for the first time since 1951 and 1956</cite>.

## Why this game matters more than the ranking does

Strip away the poll position and this is still the most dangerous kind of game for a team on a run — a desperate opponent, a short week of hype, and a defense that's shown it can get after the quarterback. Miami's margin for error got a reality check against Florida State, when it needed overtime to beat an unranked rival. Cristobal's own read on that game was that his offensive line only found its footing in the second half, when <cite index="49-13">the Hurricanes leaned on their running game more in recent weeks, averaging 5.8 yards per carry vs. the Seminoles and totaling 257 on the ground overall</cite>.

That's the actual story here, more than the banner. Miami has built a ranking on survival, not separation. Saturday is the first chance to find out whether this team can look like a No. 1 instead of just getting there by attrition.
