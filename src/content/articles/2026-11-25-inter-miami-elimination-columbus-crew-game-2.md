Inter Miami plays for its season Friday night in Columbus, and the math is not complicated. Lose, and the defending MLS Cup champions are out in the first round. Win, and they get a Game 3 back home at Nu Stadium on Dec. 2. There is no draw option in a sudden-death playoff format — a tie after 90 minutes goes straight to penalties.

That's where five months of warning signs have landed the league's highest-profile team.

## A 3-0 collapse that was about more than the scoreline

Game 1 at Nu Stadium went wrong almost immediately. <cite index="37-7">In the ninth minute, Chilean forward Gonzalo Tapia scored a penalty following a reckless move by goalkeeper Dayne St. Clair, who was brought down by Tapia inside the box.</cite> It got worse from there. <cite index="37-8">In the 36th minute, defender Gonzalo Luján received his second yellow card for a harsh tackle on Steven Moreira, leaving Inter Miami with ten players for over an hour.</cite> Columbus made it count, closing out a 3-0 win that left the No. 2 seed stunned by the No. 7.

The part of that night that's generated more conversation than the scoreline itself is what Miami's bench did with it. <cite index="38-4">Manager Kily González kept Lionel Messi, Germán Berterame, and Casemiro on the pitch for the full 90 minutes despite yellow cards and a 3-0 deficit.</cite> Down three goals and a man, with nothing left to play for in that particular match except pride, Miami's coach left his most important — and most fatigue-prone — players out there. It's the kind of decision that invites a question nobody at the club wants to answer directly: who's actually making these calls.

That question matters because Miami's form has been sliding for a while, not just in one bad half. <cite index="38-6">Miami has earned only one victory over a playoff qualifier in its final 16 league matches since Messi returned from World Cup duty.</cite> Messi himself hasn't stopped scoring — he collected the Golden Boot before Game 1 kicked off — but a team built around one 38-year-old's individual brilliance has looked disorganized for stretches most of the fall.

## Columbus didn't back into this

It's worth saying plainly: Columbus is not a fluke opponent playing over its head for one night. <cite index="20-10,20-11">The Crew won eight of their final nine regular season matches, including six consecutive victories, to record one of the most significant turnarounds in MLS history, outscoring opponents 24-10 over that stretch and earning 24 points out of a possible 27 to close the regular season.</cite> They were in last place in the East as recently as September. Three months later, they're one win from the second round against the best team in the league's regular-season standings.

The head-to-head history actually favors Miami — <cite index="23-12">Inter Miami leads the all-time MLS regular-season series 7-2-3 across 12 meetings, outscoring Columbus 21-17</cite> — but that record is backward-looking and irrelevant to this month. <cite index="23-13">Columbus went unbeaten against Miami in 2026, drawing 2-2 in Miami on August 1 and winning 2-1 at ScottsMiracle-Gro Field on September 27.</cite> This is now the third time this calendar year Columbus has had Miami's number.

## What Friday actually decides

Game 2 kicks off at 8 p.m. ET at ScottsMiracle-Gro Field. <cite index="32-7,32-8">Columbus would advance with a Game 2 victory. Should Miami win, it would force a decisive Game 3 at Nu Stadium on Dec. 2.</cite> Luján, who was sent off in Game 1, won't be available, which only tightens Miami's margin for error in central defense.

This isn't really a story about whether Inter Miami can be upset — plenty of favorites lose in a single elimination format. It's a story about whether a club that reorganized its entire roster and coaching staff around keeping Messi happy and productive can actually function when the plan hits adversity. González's decision to leave his stars on the field in a blowout, and the team's broader inability to beat good opponents down the stretch, point to the same issue: this group hasn't shown it can adjust when things go wrong.

If Miami loses Friday, the defending champions go out in the first round to the No. 7 seed, and every one of those questions follows the franchise into the offseason.
