Miami needed one week to answer its only loss of the season, and it did it with its two biggest offseason additions and one homegrown record-breaker all playing starring roles at once.

The Hurricanes beat Duke 36-25 at Hard Rock Stadium on Saturday, bouncing back from a loss at Notre Dame to push to 9-1 overall and 7-0 in the ACC. That keeps Miami in control of its own path to the conference championship game in Charlotte, with two regular-season games remaining.

## Fletcher rewrites the record book

The game's most significant moment arrived in the second quarter, when Mark Fletcher Jr. punched in a short touchdown run that gave Miami a 10-3 lead. <cite index="23-1">It was Fletcher's 36th career rushing touchdown, a milestone in his Hurricanes career.</cite> <cite index="23-9">The score moved him past Stephen McGuire for the most rushing touchdowns in program history, after he'd already passed McGuire's total touchdown record against North Carolina.</cite>

Fletcher didn't finish the day on the field. <cite index="29-8">He left the game with an apparent injury in the first half and did not return.</cite> That put more of the workload on freshman running back Javian Mallory, who answered with the best game of his season.

## Mallory breaks out as Mensah picks apart his old team

<cite index="21-2">Javian Mallory finished with 150 scrimmage yards and three touchdowns.</cite> <cite index="28-8">He capped a drive with a 63-yard catch-and-run touchdown that made it 22-10, and later added a 39-yard touchdown run that sealed the win.</cite> Those two scores, mixed in with a short rushing touchdown, gave Mallory touchdowns through the air and on the ground in a game where Miami needed answers after losing its lead back.

The day's emotional center, though, was the matchup between Darian Mensah and the program that developed him. <cite index="26-4">After months of anticipation, Mensah and receiver Cooper Barkate finally got a chance to face the Duke team they left in January.</cite> <cite index="26-1">Mensah finished with 382 yards and a touchdown in the victory,</cite> and <cite index="20-9">he went over the 3,000-yard mark for the season while completing 30 of 41 passes.</cite> <cite index="26-10,26-11">The Hurricanes showed patience by hitting Duke with the quick game to build longer drives, and Mensah went 23-of-29 for 300 yards and a score on throws of 5 air yards or fewer.</cite>

Barkate matched him. <cite index="24-4">Cooper Barkate had his first 100-yard game for Miami, catching eight passes for 134 yards.</cite> Mensah didn't dodge the weirdness of playing his former team. <cite index="26-9">"If I'm being honest, it definitely did feel different, a little bit weird," Mensah said after the game.</cite> Barkate was more direct about what the win means going forward, saying <cite index="28-9">"Our goal is definitely to get to Charlotte, and beyond that."</cite>

## Duke's record-setter and a defense that mostly held

Duke didn't go quietly. <cite index="28-4">Nate Sheppard ran for 131 yards and two touchdowns, setting the school's single-season rushing record.</cite> That came against a Miami defense that <cite index="31-7">entered Saturday allowing the fewest rushing yards per game in the country at 64.3.</cite> Sheppard was the one Blue Devil who consistently found room, but Miami got the stop it needed when it mattered most. <cite index="30-12">Xavier Lucas picked off Duke quarterback Walker Eget, and five plays later Mallory scored again to push Miami's lead to 36-17.</cite>

There was also a quieter stat buried in the box score that showed how good Duke's defense had been coming in. <cite index="20-11">Fletcher's touchdown was the first Duke had allowed in its last 210 defensive snaps, spanning 36 possessions over 13 quarters.</cite> Miami snapped that streak and then kept scoring.

The Hurricanes weren't clean doing it. <cite index="22-14">Miami was flagged for 67 penalty yards compared to Duke's 18,</cite> and the offense stalled in stretches before putting together the kind of long, physical drives that wore Duke down in the fourth quarter. <cite index="30-11">Mallory capped a 13-play, 79-yard drive with a 2-yard touchdown run that gave the Hurricanes breathing room</cite> after Duke had cut the lead to single digits.

None of the sloppiness changed the outcome. Miami is 9-1, has won seven straight in conference play, and has its two best transfers and its all-time leading rusher all producing in the same game. That's the version of this team that was supposed to show up all year. After one bad week in South Bend, it showed up again when it mattered.
