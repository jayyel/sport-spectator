The Heat gutted their roster in June for a shot at a championship. Four games into the Giannis Antetokounmpo era, they've won zero of them, and the locker room is already pointing fingers.

Miami fell to 0-4 on Monday night with a 113-109 home loss to Dallas, missing 11 of 25 free throws and shooting 36 percent from the floor in the fourth quarter of a game they led. It's Miami's worst start to a season since 2007-08, when the team finished 15-67. The Heat visit Atlanta on Wednesday night still looking for win No. 1.

## What the Heat Gave Up

This isn't a rebuilding team that stumbled into a bad month. In June, Miami sent Tyler Herro, Kel'el Ware, Jaime Jaquez Jr., Kasparas Jakucionis and a stack of first-round picks to Milwaukee for Antetokounmpo and Bobby Portis — <cite index="63-3,63-4">one of the biggest trades in franchise history, closing out a saga that had been building for more than a year</cite>. The idea was straightforward: pair a two-time MVP with Bam Adebayo and get back to the Finals for the first time since 2023.

Instead, Antetokounmpo is averaging strong numbers in a losing cause — he posted his <cite index="51-8,51-9">first triple-double with the Heat in Monday's loss to Dallas, finishing with 29 points, 14 rebounds and 11 assists</cite> — while the team around him can't close games. Miami's offense ranks <cite index="51-14">27th in the league with an offensive rating of 105.3</cite> entering Wednesday's game, and the defense hasn't been much better.

## Adebayo Pushes Back

The most uncomfortable part of the start isn't the record — it's what's happening to Adebayo's game and how he's responding to questions about it. The Heat captain is averaging <cite index="69-7">11.8 points, 7.3 rebounds and 3.5 assists per game while shooting 40 percent from the field and 16.7 percent from three-point range</cite> through four games, a steep drop from the 20-point, double-digit-rebound season he put up a year ago. On Monday he managed <cite index="68-6">just eight points on 3-of-10 shooting and missed all five of his 3-point attempts</cite>.

Asked about it afterward, Adebayo didn't accept the premise that his slump is tied to adjusting to a new co-star. <cite index="68-6,68-7,68-8">"Can't overthink basketball," he said. "For me, my shot not going in. I'm not going to overthink that."</cite> He then turned the question back on the people asking it: <cite index="68-9,68-10">"it's funny how the outside noise wants to make me the scapegoat. But when the tables turn, just all that energy — when the energy shifts — just be prepared for that."</cite>

It's the kind of answer that reads differently depending on how the next ten games go. Said now, at 0-4, it sounds like a star bristling at blame he thinks is unfair. Adebayo has a point that the criticism has been lopsided — Antetokounmpo put it plainly after the game, saying <cite index="51-16,51-17,51-18">"it's not just Bam. It's everybody. We're trying to figure out where we fit in and what we've got to do."</cite> But Adebayo's shooting numbers are real, and "my shot's not going in" doesn't fully explain a center who's clearly navigating reduced touches and overlapping space with a ball-dominant forward who needs the paint to operate.

## The Actual Problem Isn't Chemistry Jokes

Strip away the soundbites and the issue is simpler than a personality clash: two bigs who both want to work from similar spots on the floor, on a team that traded away shooting and secondary playmaking to get here. Guard Davion Mitchell, who's started hot with 21 and 22 points in recent games, has said as much — acknowledging that he and Antetokounmpo often occupy the same real estate and need time to sort it out. That's a schematic question for Erik Spoelstra, not a referendum on Adebayo's effort.

Spoelstra's response to the start has been measured — <cite index="51-10,51-11">"This is not the start anybody wanted, but it has happened. So we have to address what's in front of us, and we need to continue to get more consistent with our game,"</cite> he said. That's the right tone for four games in October. It will sound very different if Miami is still searching for its identity in December with a roster this thin on shooting after what it surrendered for Antetokounmpo.

Wednesday in Atlanta is as good a measuring stick as any. The Hawks are 2-2 and not exactly imposing, which makes this close to a must-win if Miami wants to put a stop to the outside noise Adebayo is so unhappy about. Lose again, and the scapegoat conversation won't be the only uncomfortable one in that locker room.
