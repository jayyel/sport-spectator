Inter Miami is headed back to the playoffs, and this time the win came with a trophy already in the rearview mirror and a new voice in the locker room. The Herons beat New York City FC 2-0 at Nu Stadium on Wednesday night, a result that clinched a postseason berth with five regular-season matches still unplayed.

Germán Berterame opened the scoring in the 20th minute off a pass from Luis Suárez, snapping a scoreless drought that stretched back to May. <cite index="48-6">Rodrigo De Paul then doubled the score in the first half, connecting with Lionel Messi just outside the box before rocketing the ball into the upper left corner.</cite> <cite index="48-9">NYCFC had a chance at a late goal from the penalty spot but Miami keeper Dayne St. Clair came up with a big save to keep a clean sheet on the night.</cite>

The victory moved Miami to second in the Eastern Conference with 52 points through 29 games, and locked in a return trip to the postseason for a third straight year. <cite index="48-11">Miami missed the playoffs in 2023 in Messi's first year, before going on to qualify for the postseason in each of the past three seasons.</cite> This is also the fifth playoff appearance in club history, and the first to be played at Nu Stadium.

## A season that nearly went sideways

What makes the clinch notable isn't just the arithmetic. It's the path to get here. Javier Mascherano, who had coached Miami to its first-ever MLS Cup title in 2025, stepped down in April for personal reasons. Assistant Guillermo Hoyos ran the team on an interim basis through the summer, but results slipped — Miami won just one of eight games during one stretch, conceding 23 goals, and fan frustration with the coaching situation became loud enough that players publicly defended the staff.

The club's answer was unusual: promote Hoyos's former boss. Cristian "Kily" González, a 52-year-old Argentine who played alongside Messi on the national team back in 2005, took over in mid-September after a short run as Miami's sporting director. He inherited a team that had gone winless in five matches across competitions and was trailing Nashville SC by nine points in the Supporters' Shield race.

Wednesday's win was just González's second in MLS play. <cite index="48-12">Wednesday's victory came after Miami routed DC United 4-0 last Saturday on a pair of goals from Messi and Suárez, and it was the second league win for new coach González, who took over Sept. 16.</cite> Two wins in five days, both coming with the playoff math already in motion, is as clean a start as the club could have asked for from a coach who had never managed outside Argentina before this job.

## What's actually at stake now

Clinching with games to spare shifts the conversation from "will they make it" to "how high can they finish." <cite index="47-6,47-7">With five regular-season matches left, the reigning MLS Cup presented by Audi champions are second in the Eastern Conference standings at 52 points, and have their sights set on becoming the first back-to-back winners since the 2011-12 LA Galaxy.</cite> That's the real stakes of the next month: not just getting into the bracket, but building seeding and form heading into a title defense.

Messi remains the engine. He leads the Inter Miami attack and set up the clinching goal rather than scoring it himself Wednesday — a reminder that this version of the team spreads its production more than it used to, with De Paul, Suárez and Berterame all factoring into the scoring column this month. <cite index="48-8">Miami coach Cristian "Kily" González said after the game that qualifying for the playoffs was one of the club's initial goals for the season, and that they're there, but still need to keep improving as a team.</cite>

The schedule doesn't get easier before it gets meaningful. <cite index="48-13">NYCFC hosts LAFC in its next game while Miami plays away at Atlanta United.</cite> Neither result changes Miami's playoff status now. What it will determine is whether González's group arrives at the postseason with momentum or merely a ticket punched early — and whether a coaching change that looked like a gamble in September ends up looking like the right call in December.
