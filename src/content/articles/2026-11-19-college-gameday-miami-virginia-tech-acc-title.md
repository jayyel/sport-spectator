ESPN's College GameDay is setting up on Lakeside Patio again, and this time a win actually puts Miami exactly where it wants to be. The Hurricanes (9-1, 7-0 ACC) host Virginia Tech Saturday at 7 p.m. at Hard Rock Stadium, and a victory locks up a spot in the ACC Championship Game. The pregame show broadcasts live from campus from 9 a.m. to noon, and Dwayne "The Rock" Johnson — a Miami alum — is the guest picker.

This marks the <cite index="25-11">tenth time Miami has served as the host team for College GameDay and the third time the show has broadcast from the university's campus.</cite> The last time the show visited Coral Gables, Miami beat Florida. The program has had a good run on these occasions: <cite index="25-12">the Hurricanes hold an 8-1 record in their previous nine home games featured on the show.</cite>

## The stakes are real this time

Miami isn't just chasing a nice home atmosphere. The Hurricanes <cite index="20-4,20-5,20-6,20-7">enter the weekend 9-1 and have established themselves as one of the top teams in the country, sitting atop the ACC standings at 7-0 in conference play. A victory over Virginia Tech would secure Miami a spot in the ACC Championship Game.</cite> That's the actual headline here, not the TV circus around it.

Getting to this point took a bounce-back. Miami lost at Notre Dame on November 7, its first defeat of the season, then responded with a 36-25 win over Duke in a game the program described as a "crucial ACC matchup." That win kept the conference race in Miami's hands heading into the final stretch.

Virginia Tech, at 6-4, is a program trying to salvage its season against a team that has dominated the series. Miami leads the all-time series 27-15 and has won five straight meetings, including a 34-17 win in Blacksburg last season. The Hokies have also struggled on defense recently — Virginia Tech has allowed 91 points combined across its last two losses, a bad number to carry into a game against Miami's offense.

## Mallory filling a very real void

The games have meant more attrition for Miami. Malachi Toney, the Hurricanes' best receiver, is out for the rest of the regular season after having surgery. Miami is also without wide receiver Joshua Moore, running back CharMar Brown, two cornerbacks, an offensive lineman and a tight end heading into Saturday.

What's kept the offense moving despite those losses is a freshman nobody circled in August. Javian Mallory leads Miami with 580 rushing yards and nine touchdowns on 84 carries, and he had his best game yet against Duke — <cite index="29-9">150 yards from scrimmage and three total touchdowns after Mark Fletcher Jr. left with an injury.</cite> That performance is a big reason Miami didn't miss a beat after Toney went down, and it's the storyline worth watching if Virginia Tech's defense tries to load up against the pass.

At quarterback, Darian Mensah keeps piling up hardware. He was named a Davey O'Brien Award semifinalist this week and already picked up ACC Quarterback of the Week honors for the Duke game. Virginia Tech's defense, which has been gashed through the air lately, is exactly the kind of opponent a quarterback in that form wants to see.

## Why this one matters beyond Saturday

Strip away the GameDay bus and the celebrity picker, and the stakes are simple: win, and Miami plays for the ACC title. Lose, and the conference race opens back up with two weeks left in the regular season. The Hurricanes have the better roster, the better record and the recent history against this opponent. What they don't have is any more margin for a letdown like the one that cost them at Notre Dame. A trip to the ACC Championship Game — and, by extension, a strong positioning for the College Football Playoff — is sitting in front of them. Saturday is the test of whether they can just go take it.
