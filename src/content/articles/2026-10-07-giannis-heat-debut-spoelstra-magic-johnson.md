The box score said nine points. The story was the seven assists.

Giannis Antetokounmpo played 14 minutes in his first game as a member of the Miami Heat last Saturday, a 129-105 preseason win over Toronto in Quebec City, and he did it without taking a single three-pointer. <cite index="53-8">His box-score line was 9 points on 3-for-3 shooting from the field, 4 rebounds, 7 assists and 2 turnovers.</cite> <cite index="53-9">He also went 3-for-4 from the free-throw line.</cite> What mattered more than any of that was what happened on the other end of his passes. <cite index="74-7,74-8">The seven assisted shots generated 21 points, and added to Antetokounmpo's nine points, he either scored or supplied the credited assist on 30 Miami points.</cite>

That's the stat that got Erik Spoelstra talking.

## A Magic Johnson comparison, from the guy who'd know

After the game, Spoelstra reached for a name that carries real weight in that locker room, given who's running the organization. <cite index="69-5,69-6">Spoelstra compared the two-time MVP to Los Angeles Lakers legend Magic Johnson, noting that his size and court vision evoke memories of the iconic point guard who once played under Heat president Pat Riley in LA.</cite> The quote itself was specific about where the comparison came from: <cite index="67-1">"The way he's played with the ball in his hands and delivering the ball, (Giannis) reminds all of us of a great big point guard that played for Pat (Riley) back in the 80s," Spoelstra said.</cite>

Antetokounmpo heard about it and pushed back, gently. <cite index="70-4,70-5">"It's a great compliment," Antetokounmpo said when asked about the comparison made by Spoelstra and Heat president Pat Riley. "I think I'm far away from Magic Johnson, one of the – if not the best point guard to ever play this game."</cite> He added that it's become a running theme with every new coaching staff. <cite index="71-4">"I think every coach that been around me say the same thing … they're like 'oh I actually didn't know Giannis can pass the ball.'"</cite>

The numbers put some guardrails on how far to take it. <cite index="73-9,73-10">Johnson averaged 11.2 assists per game over 13 seasons, while Antetokounmpo's career high is 6.5 assists per game, and he left Milwaukee after 13 seasons as the Bucks' all-time assists leader.</cite> One half of one preseason game isn't a sample size. But Spoelstra's larger point wasn't about per-game averages — it was about what a 6-foot-11 player who draws two defenders creates for everyone standing around him.

## Why Miami gave up almost nothing to get here

It's worth remembering what this cost, because it clarifies why Miami is treating 14 preseason minutes like a data point worth discussing. <cite index="42-5,42-6,42-7">The Bucks received Tyler Herro, Kel'el Ware, Jaime Jaquez Jr., Kasparas Jakucionis and three first-round picks, including the No. 13 overall pick in that year's draft, while the Heat also received veteran forward Bobby Portis from Milwaukee in the trade.</cite> It was a lot of draft capital and a lot of promising young talent. It was not, notably, Bam Adebayo, and reporting at the time made clear Miami had drawn that line. <cite index="45-10">Miami had long coveted Giannis and had been willing to offer any assets other than All-Star center Bam Adebayo to make a deal happen.</cite>

Then, weeks later, Miami added the other piece that makes this roster interesting: shooting around its new frontcourt pairing. <cite index="61-6,61-7">Klay Thompson paired with Stephen Curry to be part of Golden State's famed "Splash Brothers" duo, and he's a five-time All-Star who holds the league record with 14 made 3s in a game.</cite> Thompson signed a two-year deal worth roughly $11.5 million, taking what was left of Miami's mid-level exception. He joins Tim Hardaway Jr., who also arrived this offseason and who flashed exactly the kind of shooting the Heat built around Antetokounmpo for — <cite index="30-2">Hardaway finished with 14 points on 4-4 shooting from the field, 3-3 from three, in 11 minutes off the bench during the preseason win over Toronto.</cite>

That's the bet: put enough shooters around a point-forward who commands two defenders in the paint, and the math takes care of itself. Seven assists leading to seven made threes in his first appearance is either a fluke or a preview. Spoelstra clearly thinks it's the latter.

## What's actually still unknown

None of this settles anything. The Raptors game was one half with the starters, at a neutral site, against a team that rested its best perimeter defender. Andrew Wiggins, who played alongside Antetokounmpo and scored 12 points, described the effect in blunter terms than his coach did: <cite index="74-12">"Giannis, his gravitational pull in that paint, it's crazy," Wiggins said.</cite> That's the scouting report in six words, but it's still a scouting report based on 14 minutes.

The bigger test starts Thursday, when Miami hosts New Orleans in its home preseason opener at Kaseya Center — <cite index="48-5">the Heat's home preseason opener is on Thursday against the New Orleans Pelicans, ahead of the first game of the regular season on Oct. 21 against the Minnesota Timberwolves.</cite> Three more exhibitions follow before the games count. Somewhere in that stretch, Miami needs to find out whether a 31-year-old former MVP who's never averaged more than 6.5 assists a game can actually run an offense built to feed shooters, or whether one highlight-reel half in Quebec was just a very good half.

Either way, the Heat didn't trade four players and a pile of picks to find out if Antetokounmpo could still dunk. They already knew that. They wanted to know if he could pass out of a crowd and make Klay Thompson's catch-and-shoot threes easier. Through one game, the answer looks like yes. Through one game is also not very long.
