The top-ranked Hurricanes travel to Notre Dame Stadium Saturday missing the receiver who has carried their offense all season — and the timing could not be worse.

Malachi Toney rolled his ankle during practice Wednesday, and by Thursday he was in surgery. Miami's star wide receiver underwent tightrope surgery on his ankle Thursday and will miss Saturday's matchup at Notre Dame, with his return ruled out indefinitely. The sophomore has been nothing short of the engine of this offense. Toney has seven touchdowns in his last three games and has already hit the 1,000-yard mark through just eight games. He leads the Hurricanes with 1,027 yards this season.

The injury itself is a high ankle sprain, the kind that typically costs a player a month or more. Toney is out not only for the Notre Dame matchup, but for the next four weeks, per ESPN's Pete Thamel. The program's own statement gave almost nothing away — Miami said only that "Malachi Toney sustained an injury during Wednesday's practice and is currently undergoing further evaluation by the team's medical staff." But the belief around the sport is that this isn't season-ending. The belief is that it is not a season-ending injury, and the four-week timeline to recovery would allow Toney to return at the beginning of December — right in time for the College Football Playoff, if the undefeated Hurricanes can continue to cement their status as a worthy playoff team.

That's the best-case version. The worst-case version is that Miami has to navigate the heart of its schedule — starting Saturday night — without the one player defenses have had to game-plan around all year.

## Why This Game Was Already Massive

Strip away the injury news and this was already shaping up as the signature game of the season. Current No. 1 Miami heads to South Bend for a showdown with Notre Dame, in a rivalry that's one of the most significant of the past half-century in college football, dating to the Catholics vs. Convicts. There's bad blood from last year too. A year ago, Miami knocked off Notre Dame in the opener, and though the Irish spent all of November ranked well ahead of the Canes and in line for a playoff berth, the committee bumped up Miami and dropped the Domers in its final rankings despite neither team playing a game.

Notre Dame enters with its own playoff anxiety. With the Fighting Irish ranked 11th, they're right back where they were a year ago — they can't lose a second game, to Miami or anybody else, and expect to make the 12-team playoff field. History isn't on Miami's side in South Bend, either. It's a chance to beat No. 1 in South Bend, which the Irish have done twice. The Hurricanes have managed just one win in 11 trips there, back in 1984.

## An Offense Built Around One Guy

What makes the Toney injury so damaging is how central he's been to a historically efficient attack. With quarterback Darian Mensah and Toney, Miami's offense currently ranks No. 2 in the FBS, averaging 534.9 yards per game, and the Hurricanes also lead the nation in scoring with 47.6 points per game. Take away the receiver averaging 128.4 yards per game and 13 touchdowns in just eight games, and that machine suddenly looks different.

Notre Dame also loses a key piece on defense. Notre Dame lost a key defensive player this week when green dot linebacker Drayk Bowen was ruled out with an ankle injury. But Miami's absence is the one that changes the entire offensive script. The matchup everyone wanted to see — Toney against Notre Dame's top cover man — simply won't happen. Toney would have been matched up against star Notre Dame cornerback Leonard Moore on Saturday, had he been able to go.

So who fills the void? With Toney sidelined, the Hurricanes could hope for senior wideout Cooper Barkate to step up as a top target for Mensah while Javian Mallory and Mark Fletcher Jr. lead the team's run game. On3 floated two other names who could slide inside. If Toney cannot go against Notre Dame, the Hurricanes could move Daylyn Upshaw or Vandrevius Jacobs to the slot.

Notre Dame isn't assuming anything. Coach Marcus Freeman made clear his staff prepared for both scenarios up until kickoff, and said he's not expecting one person to replace Malachi Toney, but noted the Hurricanes have a lot of great players and will find ways to get the best players on the field. That's the real story here — not whether Miami stays undefeated, but whether an offense this reliant on one receiver can function at the level that got it to No. 1 in the first place. The answer starts Saturday night in South Bend.
