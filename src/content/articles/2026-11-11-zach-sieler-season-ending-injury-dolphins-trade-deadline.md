Zach Sieler ruptured his biceps on the opening play of Sunday's loss to Detroit. He played the rest of the game anyway, finishing with a tackle and a quarterback hurry while helping a Dolphins front that spent the afternoon harassing Jared Goff. Two days later, on the same Tuesday as the NFL's trade deadline, word got out that his season is over.

That double timing says something about where Miami is five years into whatever this is. The team's best interior defender, a captain, a 31-year-old who hadn't missed a game from 2020 through last season, tore through a muscle and kept playing because that's who he is. Then the front office let the deadline pass without adding a single piece to a 1-7 roster, and without moving Sieler either, even though reports had floated him as trade bait all week.

## A captain's injury nobody saw coming

Sieler was dealing with a listed shoulder issue last week, limited in practice Wednesday and Thursday before going full-speed Friday. Nobody flagged the biceps. He showed up Sunday with a brace on his right arm and tore it on a two-yard Jahmyr Gibbs run on the first snap. He finished the game. The injury didn't become public until ESPN's Adam Schefter reported it Tuesday, and <cite index="28-5">the expected recovery timeline for the injury is five to six months, meaning Sieler will miss the rest of the Dolphins' season</cite>.

The numbers on his year were modest but real: <cite index="20-6,20-7">Sieler started the first eight games of the season for the Dolphins. He had 24 tackles and two sacks in those appearances</cite>. Since joining as a waiver claim off Baltimore in 2019, <cite index="20-8">he has 377 tackles, 37.5 sacks, two interceptions, five forced fumbles and six fumble recoveries during his time in Miami</cite>. He signed a three-year extension before last season that <cite index="20-9,20-10">ties him to the team through the 2029 season with cap hits of more than $20 million each year</cite>.

Head coach Jeff Hafley talked about what Sieler means to a roster that skews young in blunt terms before any of this happened. <cite index="28-7,28-8,28-9,28-10">"It's valuable to me. It's valuable to our young players," Hafley said of Sieler's presence, adding "there's not a much better person out there than Zach Sieler."</cite> That's the kind of thing coaches say about players they're about to lose for a season, and in this case it happened to be true before the injury even leaked.

## No moves, no direction, no urgency

What makes the timing worse is what didn't happen Tuesday. The Dolphins went into the deadline widely expected to sell off veterans on an expiring roster, and <cite index="4-5">the Miami Dolphins were projected to be sellers at the NFL's 2026 trade deadline, but with the 4 p.m. Eastern deadline now come and gone, Miami stood pat with their roster</cite>. Linebacker Jordyn Brooks was the most obvious trade chip on the roster, with teams like New England, Philadelphia, Chicago and Dallas reportedly interested, and Miami moved him nowhere.

Sieler's injury took any question of trading him off the table entirely, though reports indicated the front office never intended to move him regardless of health. The front office did make smaller moves this week — claiming defensive lineman Keith Cooper Jr. back off Carolina's practice squad, a depth signing that barely qualifies as a story. Nothing addressed the hole at the position Sieler leaves vacant.

That hole now falls to a rotation of Jordan Phillips, Zeek Biggers and Kenneth Grant, none of whom have Sieler's track record of three-down snaps against the run and the pass. For a defensive line that was already playing from behind against a quality Detroit offensive front, losing its most experienced piece for the stretch run of a lost season isn't a crisis so much as a confirmation. Miami sits at 1-7, a game out of the AFC East basement and a game off the top of the 2027 draft order, and that math was always going to matter more than the won-loss column from here to January.

The real decision on Sieler isn't about this season anyway. It's about whether a team rebuilding around young talent wants to keep paying a 31-year-old interior lineman cap hits of $20.9 million, $23.7 million and $23 million over the next three years, or whether a restructure gets worked out that keeps him around on different terms. That conversation can wait until the surgery is done and the recovery starts. The current season, for all practical purposes, is just playing itself out.
