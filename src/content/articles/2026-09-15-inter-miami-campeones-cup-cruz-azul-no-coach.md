Inter Miami hosts Cruz Azul on Wednesday night for the Campeones Cup, the first shot this club has had at silverware inside Nu Stadium. It's also a match the Herons will play without a real head coach — because the guy they hired three weeks ago still doesn't have the paperwork to stand on the sideline.

## A Trophy Game With No Real Coach

Inter Miami <cite index="24-1,24-3">appointed Cristian "Kily" González as new head coach on Aug. 28, with the club announcing it had reached an agreement for him to become the first team's new head coach.</cite> That was nearly three weeks ago. As of Saturday's game against Nashville, <cite index="35-1">the club was still being run by Guillermo Hoyos while waiting for González to take over as coach, since he still hasn't been able to debut because of administrative delays with his work visa.</cite>

That means González will watch Wednesday's Campeones Cup — his club's first crack at a trophy in its new building — from somewhere other than the touchline, same as he's watched every match since he was hired. Hoyos, still technically the interim, will run the show against a Cruz Azul team that just won a Liga MX title.

The hire itself was already a strange one. <cite index="21-4">González replaces interim boss Hoyos and becomes Inter Miami's third head coach of the year.</cite> He arrives with a thin résumé for a club with these ambitions — <cite index="23-2">a former Argentina international with a career win percentage of 32.5% in management</cite>, having only run three domestic Argentine clubs before this. Hoyos himself only had the job because <cite index="23-12">Javier Mascherano resigned in April following the club's exit from the Concacaf Champions Cup.</cite>

## The Opponent Isn't the Only Problem

Cruz Azul is not an easy team to break in a new coach against. <cite index="57-6,57-7">Cruz Azul earned the LIGA MX Clausura 2026 title after a 2-1 victory over Pumas in the final, and prior to its playoff run, Cruz Azul held the best overall record in the 2025-26 season, winning 30 out of 34 matches.</cite> This will be their first meeting with Inter Miami since Messi and Sergio Busquets made their club debuts against La Máquina back in 2023.

Miami arrives banged up. <cite index="37-8">Inter Miami has five players listed as unavailable — Allende, Luján, Micael, Sergio Reguilón and Silvetti — all reported out with injuries.</cite> Tadeo Allende's absence is the worst of it: he's <cite index="58-11">out for the rest of the season, with the forward listed with a "Season Ending Injury" status on the club's official website</cite> after knee surgery in Barcelona. Mateo Silvetti, who was supposed to be the insurance policy next to Messi, keeps re-aggravating a shoulder he first dislocated in early August.

And the results on the field match the chaos off it. Miami hasn't won since before the World Cup break. Saturday's home match against Nashville ended in a draw after <cite index="36-5">Nashville struck back in stoppage time to earn a 2-2 draw and maintain its nine-point lead at the top of the Eastern Conference.</cite> Messi is still doing his part — <cite index="36-2">he scored his MLS-leading 19th goal of the season</cite> in that game — but he's carrying a club that keeps finding new ways to not win.

## Why Wednesday Actually Matters

It would be easy to write off the Campeones Cup as preseason theater dressed up with a trophy. It isn't, not for this club. <cite index="52-4,52-5">The Michelob ULTRA Campeones Cup is a one-match, winner-takes-all clash between the reigning MLS Cup champion and the reigning LIGA MX champion, and the 2026 edition is the eighth in the series.</cite> It's also the first time Inter Miami will play for a trophy in the building it just moved into, with a fan base that's been asked to buy in on a $1 billion stadium and hasn't seen a home game decided on a trophy since.

The larger issue for Miami isn't Wednesday's opponent. It's that the front office spent three weeks selling a coaching change as the fix for a five-game winless run, and the fix still hasn't shown up to work. Hoyos will coach Wednesday the same way he's coached every match since Mascherano left — as a placeholder, waiting on someone else's visa. Whatever happens against Cruz Azul, the more revealing story is the one happening in an immigration office rather than on the touchline.
