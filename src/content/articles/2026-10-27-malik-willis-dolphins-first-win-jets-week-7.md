The Dolphins are 1-5. That sentence should not read as good news. On Sunday at MetLife Stadium, it did.

Miami beat the Jets 28-25, snapping a five-game losing streak and avoiding its first 0-6 start since 2019. It was Jeff Hafley's first win as an NFL head coach. And it happened because Malik Willis — the quarterback Hafley was being asked just two weeks ago whether he'd bench — played the best game of his career.

Willis finished 14-of-21 for 208 yards and two passing touchdowns, adding a rushing score and 37 yards on the ground. Through his first five starts of the season, he'd completed <cite index="28-11,28-12">73 of 130 passes for 849 yards, with two touchdowns and four interceptions, with a passer rating of 68.4 that put him last among qualifiers.</cite> Against the Jets, he looked like a different player entirely — a strong first half saw him finish <cite index="17-11">10/13 with 128 passing yards and 2 total touchdowns</cite>, and <cite index="43-8">the Jets defense had no answer for him, whether he was scrambling, using his legs to move the chains, or pushing the ball downfield.</cite>

## A Bye Week That Actually Worked

Miami used its Week 6 bye to retool, and it showed immediately. The Dolphins jumped out to a 21-7 lead through <cite index="17-10">methodical drives and timely defensive stops</cite>, controlling the ball for <cite index="16-7">over 21 minutes in the first half alone.</cite> Rookie receiver Caleb Douglas, back in the lineup, caught two passes for 70 yards and two touchdowns and gave Willis a legitimate outside threat the offense had been missing. Tight end Greg Dulcich was his primary target early, and the ground game controlled tempo enough that New York never got comfortable.

It wasn't clean. Willis cooled off after halftime, and the Jets clawed back within a field goal before Miami sealed it with a gutsy fourth-and-one conversion late. But a team that had looked, through six weeks, like <cite index="25-3">the league's least-talented squad</cite> finally put together a complete performance.

Hafley wasn't interested in overselling it afterward. Asked how the win changes things going forward, he said simply that <cite index="12-7,12-8">everything the team does, every day, is about trying to go win a game, and he hopes the players are happy they finally did.</cite> Guard Kadyn Proctor framed it as proof of concept more than a turning point, saying the Dolphins have <cite index="11-7,11-8">hung with some pretty good teams and played a lot of undefeated opponents in close games</cite>, and now just need to stack that performance.

## Why One Win Doesn't Settle Anything

Here's the uncomfortable part. Willis is 27 years old, in his first season as a full-time starter after four years as a backup in Tennessee and Green Bay, and signed to a three-year, $67.5 million contract that was always a bet rather than a sure thing. One strong game against a Jets defense that had been bleeding points doesn't erase five weeks of a 44.0 QBR, 24th in the league. It also doesn't erase the fact that general manager Jon-Eric Sullivan has said the Dolphins will likely draft a quarterback again next spring regardless of who's under center now.

The schedule ahead is the real test. Miami still has to face <cite index="25-17">the New England Patriots, Detroit Lions and Buffalo Bills before Thanksgiving</cite> — three playoff-caliber defenses that will tell the real story of whether Sunday was a breakthrough or an outlier against a bad opponent. The Dolphins are still looking at a high draft pick in the spring regardless; the Jets loss keeps Miami in position to pick third overall if the season ended today.

What matters about this win isn't the standings. It's that for one afternoon, the pieces Miami brought in during the offseason — the coach, the quarterback, the young receivers — looked like they belonged on the same field together. That's a low bar after an 0-5 start. But it's the first evidence all season that the rebuild has a floor.
