The Dolphins are 0-5 for the first time since 2007, and the problem isn't really the record. It's that the tape from Sunday's 31-24 loss to Cincinnati makes it hard to see how the record gets better anytime soon with Malik Willis at quarterback.

Willis finished 15 of 27 for 137 yards with one touchdown and two interceptions, and he lost a fumble late in the fourth quarter. That's three turnovers in a one-score loss to a Bengals team that came in 2-2. It was also <cite index="27-11">the third straight game in which Willis lost a fumble</cite>. Bengals safety Jordan Battle picked him off twice in the second quarter alone, once on a tipped pass and once on a throw across his body that never should have left his hand.

Jeff Hafley, in his first season as an NFL head coach, didn't dodge it afterward. "You're never going to win a game turning the ball over three times," <cite index="30-3,30-4">Hafley said. "And then when we get down to the one-yard line, we got to score."</cite> Miami drove to the Cincinnati 1-yard line in the third quarter and came away with nothing — a loss on first down, an eight-yard sack on third, then a field goal.

## The numbers are getting hard to explain away

This wasn't a one-game slump. Willis has <cite index="25-15">not thrown for 200 yards in three of his five starts</cite>, and <cite index="25-15">no Dolphins receiver has topped 100 yards in a game all season</cite>. Against the Bengals, Willis didn't complete his first pass of the game until four minutes into the second quarter. He's shown real juice as a runner — 74 rushing yards and a touchdown Sunday, including a 39-yard scramble — but an NFL offense can't run through its quarterback's legs every week and survive multiple turnovers on top of it.

Hafley was careful not to bury his quarterback, noting Willis made "splash plays with his feet" and insisting the passing game needs more consistency "with all 11 players." But he also wouldn't offer a quick vote of confidence when asked directly about Willis's ball security, saying the staff would take <cite index="30-4">"a hard look at it during the bye week."</cite> That's not nothing. Teams don't usually say that about a quarterback they're fully committed to.

## What the bye week actually has to answer

Miami signed Willis to a three-year, $67.5 million deal in March, betting that the traits that flashed behind Jordan Love in Green Bay would translate into a full-time job. Nine games in — five of them losses to start this season — the bet hasn't paid off, and the Dolphins have a legitimate alternative sitting on the bench in Kyle McCord, the rookie they traded for in August when they also dealt away Quinn Ewers. McCord has never taken an NFL regular-season snap. Hafley has given no indication a change is coming during the bye.

That's the real decision Miami has to make over these next nine days, before the team returns to face the Jets on October 25. The offensive line has given Willis time to extend plays. Ollie Gordon II has looked like a real NFL back, running for 88 yards on 18 carries in his second career start, and the Dolphins actually out-rushed Cincinnati by a wide margin. The defense held a Joe Burrow-led offense to field position battles for stretches. None of it matters if the quarterback hands the ball back three times a game.

Hafley framed the whole night as shared failure — plan and execution both lacking, coaches and players both accountable. That's the right instinct for a first-year coach trying to hold a losing locker room together in October. But at some point blame-sharing stops being useful information, and the Dolphins are approaching that point. They have a bye week to figure out whether this season is about developing Willis or finding out what they actually have at quarterback before 2027 roster-building begins. Those are two different plans, and right now it looks like Miami hasn't picked one.
