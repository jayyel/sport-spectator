Miami is 3-0, ranked No. 6, and 2-0 in the ACC. None of that tells you what's actually going on with this team right now. What tells you is the injury report Mario Cristobal read off Monday morning on WQAM, and it wasn't short.

Running back CharMar Brown and wide receiver Joshua Moore are both done for the season. That news came three days after a 33-20 win at Wake Forest that looked, on the scoreboard, like business as usual for a team that opened the year by outscoring Stanford and Florida A&M by a combined 122-13.

## Two more starters, gone for the year

<cite index="49-1">No. 6 Miami lost running back CharMar Brown and wide receiver Joshua Moore for the rest of the season because of injuries, coach Mario Cristobal said Monday, adding that several other key players also got hurt in last weekend's win over Wake Forest.</cite> Brown went down on the kind of play that made the loss easy to see coming in real time. <cite index="51-9">The redshirt junior had just ripped off a 32-yard run when he went down with what appeared to be a right knee injury.</cite> He was Miami's second-leading rusher a year ago during the Hurricanes' run to the College Football Playoff title game, and <cite index="49-10">had 97 yards on 13 carries this season</cite> before the injury.

Moore's exit is the stranger one. <cite index="49-9">Moore, who caught a touchdown pass to open the scoring, played into the fourth quarter but will be out with an upper body injury, Cristobal said.</cite> There was no cart, no obvious limp, nothing that signaled a season-ender in the moment. <cite index="51-14">The sophomore had seven catches for 107 yards and two touchdowns through three games and was beginning to earn a bigger role in Miami's receiver rotation.</cite>

Linebacker Ezekiel Marcelin also got hurt against Wake Forest and will miss several weeks, not the season, but he was a player on the rise. <cite index="49-6">Marcelin Jr., who had been rising on the depth chart, was expected to miss several weeks with a lower body issue.</cite>

## The secondary was already stretched thin

This isn't a new problem so much as a compounding one. Miami's cornerback room was supposed to be a strength this year. It didn't survive the opener. <cite index="59-2">Miami lost starting cornerback OJ Frederique on the first series of the game with an injury against Stanford.</cite> Then it got worse: <cite index="59-9">OJ Frederique is a little bit more extensive than that, and we lost Jaboree Antoine in the preseason,</cite> Cristobal said at the time, referring to a third corner who never got on the field to begin with. Xavier Lucas, the other outside corner from the season opener, went down too and was expected to miss about a month.

That means Miami played its win over Wake Forest, a team that made things uncomfortable in the second half, with all three of its top outside cornerbacks unavailable. <cite index="57-7">Miami entered with a weakened secondary, with cornerbacks OJ Frederique and Xavier Lucas out with injury.</cite> Safety Bryce Fitzgerald has been playing corner. True freshmen are taking snaps that were never supposed to be theirs in September.

Put it together and the accounting is stark: <cite index="48-1">the Hurricanes are already down three starting-level corners, and now with Brown and Moore out for the year, the injury bug is turning into a virus.</cite>

## Why this matters more in October than September

Miami has gotten away with it so far because Stanford and Florida A&M weren't built to punish a shorthanded secondary, and because the offense had enough firepower to cover for a hobbled backfield. That margin shrinks fast. Central Michigan comes to Hard Rock Stadium Saturday, but the schedule that follows — at Clemson, a home date with Florida State in the Florida Cup, Pittsburgh, at North Carolina, at Notre Dame — is not going to be as forgiving of a defense playing its fourth- and fifth-string corners or an offense missing its No. 2 back and a rotational receiver who was starting to matter.

Cristobal's program has built its identity over the past two seasons on depth and physicality up front. That depth is now the actual test. The record says Miami is one of six unbeaten teams left with something to prove. The training room says the next six weeks will decide whether this roster was ever as deep as it looked in August.
