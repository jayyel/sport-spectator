Miami is the No. 1 team in college football for the first time since December 8, 2002. It happened because a Georgia team that had been No. 1 for exactly one week lost at home to unranked Auburn on a last-second field goal, because Notre Dame lost on the final play to BYU, and because the Hurricanes needed double overtime and two disputed calls to beat an unranked Florida State team by eight.

That's the whole story of Week 7. Five of the AP's top seven teams lost on the same day, something that had never happened in the poll's 90-year history. Miami was simply the undefeated team left standing closest to the top when the dust settled, receiving 40 of 69 first-place votes and 1,697 points in Sunday's poll.

## A win that needed help

Miami trailed Florida State 21-14 with under eight minutes left in regulation — the first time all season the Hurricanes had trailed anyone. Darian Mensah threw for 326 yards and three touchdowns, but he also threw an interception, and it took a 75-yard touchdown from FSU's Duce Robinson with less than three minutes left to even force overtime. <cite index="24-8,24-9">Mensah's last touchdown throw, a 3-yarder to Malachi Toney in the second overtime, held up, and No. 3 Miami beat archrival Florida State 43-35</cite> in what both sides called a classic.

Then came the ending that will follow this game around. <cite index="56-11,56-12">Quarterback Ashton Daniels was sacked on first down, and a false start contributed to a fourth-and-21 situation at the Miami 36-yard line, before Daniels connected with Micahi Danzy in the end zone on a 36-yard pass that appeared to give the Seminoles a chance to tie.</cite> Officials flagged left tackle Xavier Chaplin for holding, wiping out the score. On the very next snap, facing fourth-and-31, Daniels launched another prayer toward Robinson in the end zone. <cite index="49-3,49-4">A holding call negated the play, and officials called the play an interception on the field, and after a brief replay review, the call was upheld</cite> and Miami's sideline emptied in celebration.

Florida State coach Mike Norvell wasn't having it two days later. <cite index="56-8">He called the holding penalty that erased the touchdown an "absolute joke."</cite> He also pointed out something that added fuel to the fire: <cite index="56-18">the same officiating crew worked a game Miami won over Florida State last year, 28-22, calling it "pretty unique"</cite> to land in that spot twice in back-to-back seasons. Whether the holding call was correct is a legitimate debate — plenty of neutral observers online thought it was a stretch — but it's also true Florida State let a 21-14 lead evaporate in the fourth quarter against an offense that had barely needed to show up to that point.

## Cristobal isn't biting on the ranking

Mario Cristobal has been through this whiplash before, watching Miami teams open hot in each of his first four seasons before fading. Asked about sitting atop the country for the first time in nearly a quarter-century, he kept it clinical. <cite index="39-14,39-15">"I think we have a good enough sample size of 2026 to know that rankings are absolutely insignificant and irrelevant with the changing of the guard from week to week," he said. "I'll focus on the real one that's important, 1-0."</cite>

That's the right instinct, and not just coach-speak. Miami is one of six remaining unbeaten FBS teams, but the No. 1 ranking this early has historically been a trap more than a trophy — ask Georgia, which held it for exactly seven days. The Hurricanes host Pittsburgh on October 24 as a favorite of more than three touchdowns by most accounts, then go on the road to North Carolina and Notre Dame in consecutive weeks. Neither is ranked right now, but neither was Florida State.

What actually matters for Miami isn't the number next to its name this week. It's whether an offense that leaned on two gift-wrapped fourth-down stops to escape an unranked rival can be relied on when the margin for officiating controversy runs out.
