Allen Bailey's wife found him unresponsive Tuesday at their Florida home. The family believes he died of natural causes, according to his brother, Alford Bailey Jr., though no official cause of death had been released as of Wednesday. He was 37.

The former Miami Hurricanes defensive lineman and 10-year NFL veteran spent four seasons at Miami before an eight-year run with the Kansas City Chiefs and two more with the Atlanta Falcons. News of his death first circulated Tuesday night, and <cite index="32-21,32-22">many felt it was a hoax before it was confirmed Wednesday morning by most outlets.</cite> The Hurricanes posted their own statement, writing that they were heartbroken over the loss of their brother and sending thoughts and prayers to his family.

## From Sapelo Island to the U

Bailey's path to Coral Gables is one of the more unusual stories in program history. <cite index="32-13,32-14,32-16">He grew up on an island off the coast of Georgia and didn't play football until high school, yet he was a heavily recruited player</cite> out of McIntosh County Academy in Darien. <cite index="32-17,32-18">College coaches either had to fly directly to the island or take a ferry for his recruiting visits, and he chose the Hurricanes over several top programs.</cite>

He was part of Randy Shannon's 2007 recruiting class, <cite index="37-4,37-5">one of Shannon's best classes, a group that included wide receiver Leonard Hankerson and defensive back Demarcus Van Dyke.</cite> Over four seasons at Miami, Bailey became known by the nickname "The Hulk." <cite index="31-8,31-9">He amassed 107 total tackles, 31 tackles for loss and 19 sacks, earning first-team All-ACC honors in 2009 and second-team All-ACC in 2010.</cite> <cite index="40-15">His best college game came in a 2010 matchup against North Carolina, when he recorded 3.5 sacks against future NFL quarterback T.J. Yates.</cite>

He talked about his upbringing with Southern newspapers during his playing days, once explaining to the Atlanta Journal-Constitution how tight-knit Sapelo Island was, saying <cite index="42-12,42-13">"We have always lived there and I'm pretty much kin to everybody. It's natural that everyone knows everyone."</cite> He grew up in the island's Hog Hammock community, a roughly 20-minute ferry ride from the Georgia mainland, and later described the isolation fondly to a Kansas City columnist, recalling the freedom of a childhood without city traffic or crowds.

## A Decade in Kansas City, and a Short Stop in Atlanta

The Chiefs made Bailey a third-round pick, No. 86 overall, in the 2011 draft. <cite index="34-10,34-11">He spent eight seasons in Kansas City and played two more years with the Atlanta Falcons, finishing his career with 22 sacks and 248 tackles in 133 regular-season games across 10 seasons.</cite> His individual peak came in the middle of that run, <cite index="37-10">when he had 41 tackles with five sacks and 10 tackles for loss in 2014 and added another 38 tackles, 4.5 sacks and seven tackles for loss in 2015.</cite> <cite index="37-11,37-12">He signed a four-year, $25 million deal to stay in Kansas City before injuries slowed his production in 2016 and 2017, then bounced back with six sacks in 2018 and signed with Atlanta as a free agent in 2019, retiring after his release in 2021.</cite>

Former Chiefs coach Andy Reid, who coached Bailey from 2013 to 2018, addressed his death before Wednesday's weekly press conference. <cite index="40-3,40-4,40-5,40-6,40-7">"Our hearts go out to his family. Prayers are with them," Reid said. "Way too young, obviously. One of my favorite players. Great kid. Just sorry to see that happened to him."</cite> The Chiefs' organization called him <cite index="34-12">"an excellent teammate, generous man, and kind soul"</cite> in a statement, while the Falcons offered their own condolences on social media.

Quarterback Patrick Mahomes, who overlapped with Bailey for two of his rookie seasons in Kansas City, recalled that <cite index="34-16">Bailey was the first person he saw in the locker room during his rookie year.</cite> Former Chiefs center Mitch Morse remembered him as "a brilliant spirit" who was kind and generous, and former offensive lineman Jeff Allen wrote on social media that he had lost <cite index="43-9,43-10,43-11">"a teammate, a friend, a brother. God needed another angel. Rest easy, my brother."</cite>

## Miami Remembers Its Own

The tributes from Bailey's Miami years carried a different, more personal register. Former Hurricanes linebacker Colin McCarthy, who played alongside Bailey in Coral Gables, posted a message late Tuesday night that read, in part, <cite index="43-5,43-6,43-7">"Lost a fellow brother today… prayers to AB family! Love you Big Cuzzo!"</cite> Former Canes receiver Travis Benjamin and tight end Jimmy Graham, both teammates during Bailey's playing days, also posted tributes. Former Chiefs defensive back Husain Abdullah added his own message of grief and gratitude for having played alongside him.

Bailey leaves behind his wife, Lauren, and a large extended family; <cite index="35-17">he was born and raised on Sapelo Island, Georgia, in a community of less than 70 people, the second-youngest of seven children.</cite> For a Miami program now riding its first No. 1 ranking since 2002, the week carried both ends of what makes college football linger in people's lives — the rare high of a current roster chasing history, and the sudden loss of a player who helped build the foundation decades before them.

No funeral arrangements had been made public as of Wednesday.
