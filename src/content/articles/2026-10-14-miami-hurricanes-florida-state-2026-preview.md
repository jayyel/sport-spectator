No. 3 Miami hosts Florida State on Saturday night at Hard Rock Stadium, the 71st meeting in a series that dates back to 1951 and now carries the Florida Cup trophy. The Hurricanes are 5-0, ranked No. 3 in both major polls, and riding a two-game win streak over their oldest rival. Florida State arrives at 3-3 having just lost 44-20 at Louisville, and on Tuesday the program dismissed its top-ranked 2026 recruit, freshman cornerback Chuck Kennon, for what the school called conduct detrimental to the team.

On paper, this should be a laugher. It's a rivalry game instead, and Mario Cristobal keeps treating it like one.

## A Florida State team that doesn't look like a contender right now

<cite index="39-7,39-8">FSU is coming off a 44-20 loss at Louisville, and the Seminoles also lost to SMU and Alabama this season.</cite> <cite index="39-9">Miami will host a Florida State team that has not won a true road contest since beating Florida in Gainesville in November 2023</cite> — though that stat matters less with the Seminoles traveling to Miami Gardens, a road trip in name only given the rivalry's geography.

Still, Cristobal isn't selling his team on a cakewalk. He pointed to Florida State nearly pulling off an upset earlier this season, noting the Seminoles <cite index="39-17">were up 21-6 against Alabama</cite> before it slipped away. He's also framed Florida State as a roster loaded with NFL-caliber players regardless of record, saying <cite index="39-16">"They have NFL talent all over the place with challenging schemes."</cite>

That's the needle Cristobal has threaded all week: respect the opponent publicly, remind his locker room that records don't win rivalry games. He put it bluntly when discussing how fast upsets are happening across the sport this season, warning his team not to assume anything. <cite index="37-14,37-17">He framed it as an unranked team against the No. 1 team in the country, and said anyone assuming they're entitled to winning "are out of their minds."</cite>

## Miami's defense has been the best in the country

The numbers back up why Miami is favored heavily regardless of the rivalry noise. <cite index="34-11">The Hurricanes' defense has allowed an ACC-low average of 9.8 points per game</cite> through five weeks, and that unit gets healthier this week. <cite index="34-12">Cornerback Xavier Lucas is expected to return, as is linebacker Ezekiel Marcelin,</cite> plugging two injury gaps that had thinned the secondary and linebacker corps during Miami's unbeaten start.

Offensively, Miami and Florida State present a rare matchup of two run-heavy ACC attacks. <cite index="34-8">Miami and Florida State rank third and fourth, respectively, in rushing offense in the ACC,</cite> which sets up as a ground-game identity clash more than a shootout, despite FSU's uneven defense. Cristobal has specifically flagged Florida State's offensive front as the game's central challenge, saying the Seminoles make it difficult to diagnose their scheme because <cite index="34-2">"the guys they have up front are really, really explosive football players."</cite>

## What's actually at stake

This isn't a playoff elimination game for Miami — the Hurricanes are built for a longer run and this is Game 6 of 12. But the rivalry carries its own weight independent of seeding. The Florida Cup has stayed in Coral Gables the last two meetings, and a third straight win would be the program's longest active streak over Florida State since the 2010s. Cristobal has called this matchup the best rivalry in sports going back to his own childhood, pointing to history that includes mascot skirmishes and combined national titles between the two programs.

For Florida State, this is about proving the Louisville loss was an aberration and not a preview of a lost season under continued pressure on Mike Norvell. Quarterback Ashton Daniels, the Auburn and Stanford transfer brought in to fix FSU's passing game, hasn't turned the offense around yet, and the Kennon dismissal this week is one more sign of a program trying to stop bleeding internally while also trying to beat its biggest rival on national television.

Kickoff is 7 p.m. Saturday on ESPN. Miami has won five straight games this season by a combined margin that has made the Hurricanes look like the most complete team in the country. Florida State is the one program built to make Miami's record irrelevant for three hours, win or lose.
