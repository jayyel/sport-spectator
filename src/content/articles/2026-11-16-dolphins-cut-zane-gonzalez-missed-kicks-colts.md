The Dolphins waived kicker Zane Gonzalez on Monday morning, a day after he missed two field goals and an extra point in a 24-22 loss at Indianapolis that Miami had no business losing. The team is now 1-8, has dropped three in a row, and spent its Monday press conferences talking about a kicking search and a defensive line that's down to bare bones.

Start with the math, because Jeff Hafley did. <cite index="23-5">The Dolphins head coach pointed out that the missed kicks accounted for seven potential points, including an extra point that forced Miami to attempt a two-point conversion later in the game.</cite> Miami lost by two. Either missed field goal — <cite index="21-3">either of the two missed field goals would have secured a victory for Miami</cite>, according to reporting on the game — would have flipped the result against a Colts team that came in at 3-7.

<cite index="20-5">Indianapolis became the first team in the Super Bowl era to win a game despite being outgained 293-276, losing the turnover margin 2-1, and committing 10 more penalties than its opponent, 13 to three.</cite> That's not a team Miami should have lost to. It's exactly the kind of game a rebuilding roster needs to find a way to win, and the kicking operation made sure it didn't.

## The Kicker Search Starts Over

Gonzalez had only been on the roster a few weeks. <cite index="22-10">Gonzalez had gone 6 for 10 on field goals since joining the Dolphins on Oct 20.</cite> That's not a sample size that inspires confidence, and Sunday's performance ended it. Hafley didn't hedge afterward. <cite index="20-7,20-8,20-9">"We got to make the extra point. We have to make the field goal," Hafley said. "I'm not going to ever BS you."</cite>

<cite index="22-11">Hafley said a decision on a new kicker was still pending</cite> as of Monday. For a team playing out a lost season, special teams competence shouldn't be the variable deciding whether Miami's young players get to see a close game through to the finish. It's the second time this year the Dolphins have had to go searching at the position, and it's becoming its own storyline inside a 1-8 season.

## Down to Two Defensive Tackles

The bigger problem might be the one Hafley can't fix with a roster move off the street. Zach Sieler is already out for the season. On Sunday, Miami lost both Zeek Biggers and Jordan Phillips to injuries in the same game. <cite index="31-4,31-5">"Zeek hurt his leg, Jordan hurt his arm, and all of a sudden we are down to two defensive tackles, which is hard against a team that runs the ball," Hafley said. "We had KG [Kenneth Grant] and Keith Cooper [taking all the snaps late]."</cite>

Cooper only arrived last week. Linebacker Willie Gay Jr. put it plainly when asked how the group kept its focus after watching two starters go down in the same afternoon, saying the injuries pile up and the team has to lock in and finish the game, dealing with the fallout afterward. By Monday, Hafley still didn't have clarity on either player. <cite index="28-4,28-5,28-6,28-7">Jordan and Zeek are both obviously guys you're going to ask about. Both guys I've asked medical about, I will have more information later today. There is nothing definitive yet.</cite> Phillips left the stadium with his arm in a sling, though Hafley said surgery isn't currently on the table.

## McCord's First Start Wasn't the Problem

Lost in the kicking and the defensive line attrition was a quarterback debut that actually went fine. Kyle McCord started in place of the injured Malik Willis and went 17 of 32 for 209 yards, taking the loss in stride afterward. <cite index="20-10,20-11">"It was a great learning experience," said McCord. "Some good, some plays I wish I could've had back, some throws and reads I wish I could've had back."</cite> He lost a fumble that Indianapolis turned into a touchdown, but Hafley came away encouraged by how he handled the pressure.

That's the frustrating part of Sunday for Miami. The quarterback they're auditioning held up. The defense, even playing down two linemen, kept it a two-score game against a run-heavy opponent. The special teams unit is what turned a winnable afternoon into loss number eight, and now the Dolphins have to replace a kicker again while hoping Biggers and Phillips aren't looking at extended time away.
