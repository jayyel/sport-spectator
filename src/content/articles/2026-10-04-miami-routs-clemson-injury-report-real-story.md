No. 4 Miami went into Death Valley on Saturday night and left with its most lopsided road win of the season, beating Clemson 41-13 to improve to 5-0 and take sole possession of first place in the ACC. The final score wasn't close. The health report from Memorial Stadium is why Sunday morning feels more complicated than the scoreboard suggests.

Start with what went right, because plenty did. <cite index="20-10,20-11">Darian Mensah threw for 227 yards and a touchdown and had a 34-yard scoring run as No. 4 Miami routed Clemson 41-13 on Saturday night to seize sole possession of first place in the Atlantic Coast Conference.</cite> He opened the scoring almost immediately — <cite index="24-8">D. Mensah pass to C. Barkate for 61 yards, for a touchdown, on a single play covering 76 yards in just 14 seconds</cite> off the first offensive snap of the game. Barkate finished with <cite index="20-12">five catches for 98 yards, including a 66-yard touchdown reception, for the Hurricanes, who started 5-0 for the second straight season.</cite>

Mensah's rushing score was its own kind of absurd. He <cite index="20-15">scampered 34 yards for a score, fumbling just before he got to the end zone and then recovering it himself</cite> — a Duke and Tulane transfer making his case as the best quarterback in the country look effortless even when it almost wasn't. It extended a streak that's becoming the defining stat of his first season in Coral Gables: <cite index="20-14">it marked the 23rd straight game Mensah has thrown a TD pass dating to his time at Duke, where he led the Blue Devils to the ACC championship last season.</cite>

## Mensah's Heisman case keeps building

Through four games before Clemson, <cite index="35-4">Mensah became the first FBS passer in the last 30 years to have more touchdown passes (14) than incompletions (12) through four games, according to CBS Sports research.</cite> That stat line, paired with a nation-leading completion percentage, has made him an early Heisman name — not just a good story for a transfer who had to sue his way out of a contract dispute with Duke to get to Miami in the first place.

The gap between these two programs was supposed to be close on paper, Clemson's name recognition still carrying weight. The oddsmakers didn't see it that way. Miami arrived as a <cite index="31-4">16.5-point favorite at Clemson, the largest spread Dabo Swinney has faced in 245 games and 18-plus seasons as coach</cite>. The final margin covered it. Miami wore the Tigers down on the ground in the second half, <cite index="20-17,20-20">wearing down Clemson with the run game and outgaining the Tigers on the ground 192-55.</cite> Mark Fletcher Jr. did the dirty work, <cite index="20-12">running for 77 yards and two touchdowns</cite>, including the score that effectively ended things in the fourth quarter.

## The casualty list is getting long

None of that changes what happened on the sideline. Miami's offensive line, already playing without multiple starters from a year ago, lost two more bodies in the first half. <cite index="47-5,47-6">Miami's injury issues deepened in the first half at Death Valley, with four Hurricanes leaving the field and the status of starting right tackle Matthew McCoy looking especially concerning, as McCoy, a game captain, went down in clear distress after a Miami possession late in the first half.</cite> Guard Max Buchanan went down too, with <cite index="39-7">a left leg injury</cite>, and while McCoy returned with a shoulder harness, he didn't look fully right according to the broadcast.

On defense, the Hurricanes lost their most productive pass rusher. <cite index="39-1,39-2">Edge rusher Marquise Lightfoot left the game with an injury after being Miami's biggest pass-rushing threat since Rueben Bain and Akheem Mesidor left for the NFL.</cite> He'd already <cite index="46-9">added a fifth sack of the season before leaving</cite>, continuing a breakout year, but Miami now has to figure out its front seven without him for an unknown stretch.

This all comes on top of a secondary that's been thin all season. Miami has been playing without three top cornerbacks for most of 2026, and while Xavier Lucas was listed as questionable to return against Clemson, the Hurricanes needed reserves at multiple spots again Saturday.

Miami is 5-0, ranked No. 4, and has the look of the best team left in the ACC after that win. But October is long, Notre Dame and Pittsburgh are still on the schedule, and a team that keeps losing linemen and pass rushers one game at a time doesn't get to just enjoy the scoreboard. The next step for this program isn't winning Saturday night. It's getting through the next month with enough bodies to keep doing it.
