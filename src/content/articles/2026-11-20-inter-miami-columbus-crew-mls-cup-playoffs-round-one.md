Inter Miami opens defense of its MLS Cup title Saturday night at Nu Stadium, and the opponent arrives with more momentum than a No. 7 seed usually carries into the postseason.

Columbus Crew finished the regular season 14-15-5, a mediocre record on paper that masks how the Crew actually got there. <cite index="69-3,69-4,69-5">Despite starting September in last place in the Eastern Conference, the Columbus Crew ended the MLS regular season comfortably into the playoffs, winning eight of its last nine games, including six straight, to secure the No. 7 seed in the East.</cite> <cite index="73-4">They clinched their playoff spot on the final day, thanks to a 93rd-minute winner from Brais Méndez against the Chicago Fire.</cite> Interim coach Laurent Courtois has the Crew believing they can win from any position in a game, telling reporters this week <cite index="69-10">"We are aware and comfortable in each scenario and the point is to not panic in either of them."</cite>

Miami's recent form points the other direction. <cite index="69-11">Miami ended the season with just four wins in its last nine games, including losses to the Crew and Red Bull New York.</cite> The two clubs have already met twice this season, and <cite index="70-4">Columbus went unbeaten against Miami this year at 1-0-1, and memorably ousted them en route to their 2024 Leagues Cup title.</cite> This is not a team that has had Miami's number by accident.

## Messi's numbers are historic. The team around him is leakier.

None of that changes what Lionel Messi has done individually. He enters the postseason having just collected his second straight Golden Boot, <cite index="65-5">topping the league with 26 goals and 13 assists in 31 appearances.</cite> <cite index="60-9">Largely because of Messi's contributions, and those of his 39-year-old friend Luis Suarez, no team this season has scored more goals than Inter Miami's 80.</cite>

But scoring has covered for problems elsewhere. A newsletter breakdown of the matchup framed it bluntly as a question of whether a team built to outscore everyone can also get stops when it needs them, noting <cite index="60-10">Miami finished the regular season 17 points behind Nashville in the East and is not looking like the dominant team that won eight of its final nine games in 2025.</cite> That version of Miami broke a playoff scoring record a year ago. This version has been treading water since the schedule flipped to November.

## Nu Stadium hasn't been the fortress it was supposed to be

Home-field advantage is normally the safety net for a two-seed. For Miami, it might not be one. <cite index="66-1">Miami's record at the new Nu Stadium is the softest of any team in the playoff field: six wins, two losses, nine draws across the regular season.</cite> That's a stadium that has produced more stalemates than statement wins in its first year of existence, and two of the three possible games in this series are scheduled there.

There's recent precedent for a lower seed making a run like this count. Atlanta United, the No. 9 seed in 2024, knocked out a Miami team that had just set the all-time MLS points record, a first-round defeat with <cite index="34-2">a 34-point difference between the two teams, the largest in playoffs history</cite> for that series. Seeding has not meant safety for this franchise in the postseason before.

## What's actually at stake

A win doesn't just extend Miami's season — it keeps alive a shot at something that hasn't happened in nearly a decade and a half. <cite index="62-3">Inter Miami is looking to become the first team in nearly 15 years to win back-to-back MLS Cup championships,</cite> a feat last managed by an LA Galaxy team that had David Beckham on the roster instead of in the owner's box.

The series format gives Miami a cushion if Saturday goes wrong. Game 2 shifts to Columbus on Nov. 27, with a potential Game 3 back at Nu Stadium in early December. But a loss at home to open the series would put the pressure squarely on a Miami defense that has spent all year being bailed out by its attack, on the road, against the one team in this bracket that has already proven it isn't afraid of the name on the other bench.

Kickoff Saturday is set for 6 p.m. at Nu Stadium.
