## Back on Top, and Built Differently Than You'd Expect

Miami hasn't been the top-ranked team in the Associated Press poll since December 8, 2002. The Hurricanes carry that distinction into Kenan Stadium on Saturday night, a 7-0 team facing a North Carolina program that hasn't played Miami since 2023 but has won the last five meetings in the series dating back to 2019.

This isn't the Miami of two decades ago, loaded with future first-round picks on defense. This team scores. The Hurricanes lead the nation in total offense at 551.9 yards per game and scoring at 49.0 points per contest. Quarterback Darian Mensah, who transferred to Miami after stops at Tulane and Duke, has been the engine: he has thrown for 2,151 yards and 22 touchdowns while leading the nation with an 81.4 completion percentage. His top target, Malachi Toney, set a new Miami record with 16 catches in last week's 52-23 win over Pittsburgh and ranks third nationally with 952 receiving yards.

The ascent to No. 1 came the way most chaotic college football seasons go — not by Miami blowing everyone out, but by everyone above it losing. Then-No. 1 Georgia, No. 2 Notre Dame, No. 5 Indiana, No. 6 Alabama and No. 7 Texas all fell in the same weekend, and Miami's survival of a narrow 43-35 double-overtime win over Florida State, in which the Hurricanes survived two Hail Marys, was enough to push them to the top. A week later, after handling Pittsburgh, Miami arrived at this weekend as the only undefeated team on the board at 7-0, 5-0 in the ACC, and the AP No. 1 with 59 first-place votes.

This isn't just a one-week fluke of circumstance, either. Miami allows 15.3 points per game, 11th nationally, and averages 551.9 yards per game while surrendering just 60.0 rushing yards per game. The résumé includes wins at Wake Forest, at Clemson, over Florida State and over Pittsburgh.

## The Belichick Variable

North Carolina enters at 4-3 and coming off a gut-punch, having had its two-game winning streak snapped with a 31-30 overtime home loss to Syracuse. The Tar Heels are also the most scrutinized 4-3 team in the country, because they're coached by Bill Belichick, in his first season trying to build a college program after decades in the NFL.

Belichick has clearly done his homework on Mensah, warning this week that "Mensah is dangerous in the scramble." Mensah returned the respect, calling North Carolina's defense a "physical group that flies around" with "some good players in the back end as well," adding, "We'll have to be ready."

Cristobal, who has steered this program to its best season in over two decades, isn't selling his team on comfort. He pointed out that North Carolina's three losses have all been close, saying the Tar Heels are "a couple of plays away from either being a one-loss team or an undefeated team." He's also been blunt about the cost of letting up for even a possession, telling reporters this week, "If not, you're putting a win at risk."

## Why This Is the Game That Matters More Than the Record Suggests

The obvious trap here isn't North Carolina's record — it's what comes after. Miami's trip to Notre Dame is one week away, on November 8, and the Hurricanes control their own path to the ACC title game if they get through Chapel Hill first. That's the kind of schedule spot that has undone ranked teams in October for as long as the sport has existed: a quality opponent sandwiched between a signature win and a marquee rematch, with a desperate home crowd and a coach who built his reputation on taking away exactly what an opponent does best.

For Miami, the stakes are about more than style points. The program hasn't finished a season ranked this high since the Larry Coker era, and the last time the Hurricanes held the AP's top spot, Ken Dorsey was still the quarterback. A loss doesn't end Miami's season or its College Football Playoff hopes by itself, but it would hand the committee an easy excuse to drop the Hurricanes behind one-loss teams with flashier losses-of-the-week.

What happens in Chapel Hill will say more about this team than six blowouts of overmatched opponents did. Mensah has not faced a defense from a coach with Belichick's track record of taking away what quarterbacks do best. How Miami responds to that pressure — not just whether it wins — is the actual story here.
