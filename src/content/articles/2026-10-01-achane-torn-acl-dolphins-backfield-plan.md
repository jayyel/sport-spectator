De'Von Achane tore his ACL on an 11-yard run in the first quarter against Kansas City. He got up, stood on the sideline between two teammates, then didn't come back. By Monday, testing had confirmed what everyone in the building already feared.

The timing is brutal on every level. The Dolphins extended Achane in May for four years and $64 million, with $32 million guaranteed, betting that his dual-threat speed would anchor whatever offense Jeff Hafley and offensive coordinator built around him. Four months later, he's on injured reserve, done for the year, and Miami is 0-3 with no established answer behind him.

## A Backfield With No Clear No. 1

<cite index="24-11">Behind Achane, Ollie Gordon II was the only other active running back for Sunday's 24-10 loss that dropped the Dolphins to 0-3.</cite> Gordon took over and <cite index="41-6,41-7,41-8">had a good day before ending up sidelined with heavy cramping, though he should be good to go in Week 4, which is concerning to some degree given he cramped up with less than a full game played.</cite>

Jaylen Wright, who'd been Achane's primary backup entering the season, wasn't even available Sunday. <cite index="39-7">According to Dolphins coach Jeff Hafley, Wright is "day-to-day" with a stinger and foot injuries.</cite> That left Miami's depth chart in a strange spot — a veteran-ish No. 2 back unavailable, a rookie sixth-round pick cramping up in relief duty, and a practice-squad trade acquisition in Jarquez Hunter waiting for his number to get called. <cite index="39-8,39-9">Receiver Malik Washington was even listed as the No. 3 running back on the depth chart.</cite>

This isn't a new problem for Hafley's staff — it's just the first time it's been forced into the open. <cite index="41-12">Every key player on offense except for quarterback Malik Willis and tight end Greg Dulcich are in their first three seasons in the NFL.</cite> That's the rebuild Sullivan and Hafley signed up for. It's just happening without the one proven playmaker they thought they had locked up for years.

## Why This One Hurts More Than the Record

Miami's offense was already miserable before Achane went down. <cite index="20-12,20-13">The Dolphins, alongside New England, were averaging a league-low 12 points per game, and Miami was generating the seventh-fewest offensive yards per game, the ninth-fewest passing yards per game and the 13th-fewest rushing yards per contest.</cite> Even accounting for that, Achane hadn't been himself through three weeks — <cite index="20-10,20-11">just 34 rushes for 127 yards and zero touchdowns, with seven catches for 49 yards and no scores.</cite> But the numbers undersold what he represented. <cite index="21-6">Miami said goodbye to quarterback Tua Tagovailoa and wide receivers Jaylen Waddle and Tyreek Hill over the offseason, leaving Achane as the lone offensive standout on the roster.</cite>

Hafley didn't try to spin it. <cite index="23-2,23-3,23-4,23-5">"My heart breaks for the guy," he said Monday. "Since I've gotten to know him, watching him grow and develop, earning the captain role, becoming a leader of the team, becoming such great energy, such a great teammate. I mean, I just love his energy. I love his enthusiasm."</cite>

The coach insists the approach won't change. <cite index="30-7,30-8,30-9,30-10">"I want to run the football. That's important, and we have the offensive line to do it," he said. "I have a lot of confidence in those backs, so this isn't like, 'We can't run the ball anymore. These are NFL players.'"</cite> That's the right thing to say. It's also a harder thing to execute with a committee nobody drafted to be a committee.

## What Comes Next

Miami travels to Minnesota in Week 4 to face a Vikings team that's 3-0 and already favored by a wide margin. The one bright spot through three games has been rookie linebacker Jacob Rodriguez, whose diving interception of Patrick Mahomes and strong overall play have put him in early Defensive Rookie of the Year conversations. That's a nice building block on a roster otherwise searching for answers.

But building blocks don't fix an immediate problem. Whoever starts at running back Sunday — Gordon if the cramping clears up, Wright if the stinger does, or Hunter if neither does — will be doing it without a true lead back for the first time all season, against a defense that just held one of the league's best rushers under 100 yards. The Dolphins didn't just lose a game Sunday. They lost the one player they could point to and say, unambiguously, this guy is good. Now they have to find out if anyone else on that depth chart is.
