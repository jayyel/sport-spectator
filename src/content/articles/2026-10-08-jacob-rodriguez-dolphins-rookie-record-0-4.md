## A 30-Year-Old Record Falls

In Miami's 15-10 loss at Minnesota on Sunday, the second-round rookie linebacker racked up <cite index="26-6,26-7">a team-high 18 tackles, including nine solo stops, setting a new franchise rookie record and surpassing the previous mark of 16 tackles set by Zach Thomas against the Arizona Cardinals in Week 2 of the 1996 season.</cite> It wasn't just a franchise mark. <cite index="26-10">Rodriguez's 18 tackles were also the most logged by any player in a single game this season and the highest by a Dolphins rookie since tackles became officially tracked in 1994.</cite>

It's the second time in four weeks Rodriguez has rewritten the franchise rookie record book. <cite index="26-8,26-9">He also recorded 14 tackles in Miami's Week 1 game versus the Las Vegas Raiders, meaning he has already produced two of the franchise's top three rookie tackle performances through his first four NFL games.</cite>

The résumé Rodriguez brought with him made this almost predictable. <cite index="22-2">He arrived in Miami as the 43rd overall pick in the 2026 draft after winning the Bronko Nagurski Trophy, Butkus Award, Lombardi Award and Chuck Bednarik Award in 2025, along with unanimous All-American honors and Big 12 Defensive Player of the Year.</cite> There's a poetic thread to where he landed, too. <cite index="25-2">Rodriguez was mentored throughout his collegiate career at Texas Tech by Dolphins legend Zach Thomas, so it was quite serendipitous that he wound up in Miami.</cite> The guy who inherited the record just so happens to have been coached by the guy who used to hold it.

Rodriguez isn't just piling up tackles against the run. In Week 3 against Kansas City, <cite index="25-11">he became the first NFL rookie to record at least five tackles, three tackles for loss and an interception in a single game since Tampa Bay's Lavonte David in 2012</cite> — and the pick came off Patrick Mahomes. Through four games he's at or near the top of the entire league in tackles, not just among rookies, a number few expected from a defense that was supposed to be rebuilding from scratch.

What stands out beyond the stat sheet is the responsibility he's already carrying. <cite index="28-10">He's the only rookie in the league who has worn the green dot on his helmet in every game this season</cite>, meaning he's making the defensive calls as a 24-year-old in his first NFL season. Teammates have noticed the poise that comes with it. Edge rusher Josh Uche described him as <cite index="21-13,21-14,21-15">"the same guy every play... very steady. Isn't a volatile person"</cite> — the kind of praise coaches usually reserve for veterans, not a rookie calling signals on a losing defense.

## The Bright Spot on a 0-4 Team

None of it has translated to wins. <cite index="18-7,18-8">At 0-4, Miami is still searching for its first victory under first-year coach Jeff Hafley, coming off a 15-10 loss at Minnesota in which the offense managed only seven first downs.</cite> The numbers around Rodriguez tell the story of just how lopsided this team is right now: <cite index="18-9">through four games, the Dolphins are averaging 11.5 points while allowing 24.2 per game.</cite>

That gap is exactly why Rodriguez matters more than a typical rookie highlight. Miami isn't losing games because its defense can't get stops — it's losing because the offense can't finish drives. A defense anchored by a 21-year-old who's already outplaying his draft slot buys the front office time to fix the rest.

## What's Next

There's reinforcement on the way. <cite index="18-12,18-13">Defensive tackle Kenneth Grant, a 2025 first-round pick, was designated to return from injured reserve this week after playing all 17 games as a rookie last season with 33 tackles and two sacks.</cite> Pairing a healthy Grant up front with Rodriguez at linebacker gives Miami's defense a foundation that didn't exist a month ago.

The Dolphins host Cincinnati Sunday still looking for win No. 1. The quarterback situation, the offensive line, the receiver room — all of it remains unsettled. But for a franchise trying to figure out what it actually has heading into year two of a rebuild, Rodriguez is no longer a projection. He's the answer, delivered four games into a season that's otherwise provided almost none.
