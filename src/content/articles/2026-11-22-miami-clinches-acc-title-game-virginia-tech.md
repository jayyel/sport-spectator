Miami fell behind by two touchdowns at Hard Rock Stadium on Saturday night and still walked away with more than a win. The Hurricanes' 28-20 comeback over Virginia Tech clinched a spot in the ACC Championship Game, and <cite index="37-4">Miami became the first team in any Power 4 conference this season to clinch a conference championship appearance.</cite>

That's the headline. The deeper story is what it took to get there, and what Miami is chasing when it gets to Charlotte.

## A 14-point hole and a record kick

Virginia Tech scored two quick touchdowns to open the game and led 17-14 at halftime. Miami needed its defense and its kicker to turn things around. <cite index="47-5,47-6">Weinberg connected on a school-record, 59-yard field goal to end the first half, breaking the previous mark of 57, shared by three different kickers dating back to 1981.</cite> He'd already hit from 49 yards earlier in the game, <cite index="48-10">improving his season total to 13 of 14 field goals made.</cite>

Mark Fletcher Jr. did the rest. He scored three times on the ground, <cite index="21-5">on the strength of a three-touchdown performance from veteran running back Mark Fletcher Jr., a record-breaking kick from Jake Weinberg and a defense that strengthened in the second half.</cite> His third score, an 11-yard run with just over two minutes left, finally put the game away. <cite index="21-8">That score capped a 9-play, 70-yard drive and gave the Hurricanes some much-needed breathing room.</cite>

It wasn't clean. Quarterback Darian Mensah threw two interceptions on the night — <cite index="19-10">after throwing two, total, in the season's first 10 games.</cite> Miami's defense had to bail him out, holding Virginia Tech to almost nothing after halftime. <cite index="21-8">Miami's defense held Virginia Tech to just 55 yards in the second half and forced two straight Hokies punts</cite> to set up the clinching score.

## The rematch nobody in Winston-Salem wanted

Miami's reward is a trip back to Wake Forest, which it already beat once this season. <cite index="39-6">The Hurricanes won 33-20 at Wake Forest on Sept. 18, taking a 31-7 lead before holding on in the fourth quarter.</cite> The Demon Deacons have not lost since. <cite index="41-9">Wake Forest is on an eight-game winning streak, the longest in program history, having outscored opponents 393-94 during that stretch.</cite>

Both teams enter Charlotte on Dec. 5 with one loss apiece, and both own this season's most explosive offenses. <cite index="40-9,40-10,40-11">The matchup features two of the nation's premier offenses, with Miami ranking No. 1 and Wake Forest No. 2 in FBS scoring.</cite>

For Miami, this is only the second trip to the league's title game in program history. <cite index="37-5">The Hurricanes have never won the conference title during their 22-year stay in the conference, and only appeared in the championship game once, in 2017.</cite> That trip ended badly — <cite index="43-3">Clemson won that game, 38-3.</cite> There's also a wrinkle worth knowing: realignment means the stakes are higher than just one trophy. <cite index="37-11,37-12">The Canes will play in what is likely the final ACC conference championship game, with 2026 marking the last possible chance for the Hurricanes to claim an ACC title through the title game path.</cite>

## What's actually riding on it

A win in Charlotte would be program history. A loss wouldn't necessarily end Miami's season, but it would complicate things. <cite index="37-10">Even if Miami takes on Wake Forest with both teams at 11-1, the loser of the championship game will have an uphill battle to make the playoff as an at-large team.</cite> In other words, this isn't just about the banner — it's close to an elimination game for Miami's College Football Playoff hopes.

The streak Miami protected Saturday says plenty about how dominant this group has been within the conference. <cite index="53-6,53-7">The Hurricanes have now won 12 consecutive ACC games, the longest such run in school history — and the last time Miami had an 11-game ACC winning streak, it was snapped by Virginia Tech.</cite> Not this time. Miami get to keep playing, and they get three more weeks — a regular-season finale against Boston College, then a trip to Charlotte — to find out if a team that leads the nation in scoring can finally finish the job it couldn't in 2017.
