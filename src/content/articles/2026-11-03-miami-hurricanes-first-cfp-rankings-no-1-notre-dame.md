The College Football Playoff selection committee puts out its first rankings of the season tonight, and Miami is the only team in the country with a clean argument for No. 1. The Hurricanes are 8-0, they just won on the road in conference play, and <cite index="30-6">they held onto the top spot in the AP Top 25 for a third straight week, a day after downing Bill Belichick's North Carolina</cite>. The AP poll doesn't decide anything. The CFP rankings, starting tonight, do.

None of it will matter much if Miami loses Saturday. <cite index="32-8">This season, Miami and Notre Dame don't face off until November — specifically, this upcoming Saturday, where the result will almost certainly dictate the first big shakeup in the rankings.</cite> That's the context for tonight's release: whatever seed the committee hands Miami is a placeholder, good for exactly four days.

## The North Carolina escape

Saturday's win wasn't a formality. <cite index="17-3,17-1">The Hurricanes escaped Halloween night with a win at Chapel Hill, 38-35, snapping a five-game losing streak against North Carolina and marking the program's first win there since 2017.</cite> <cite index="17-5">Miami put up 416 total yards and 38 points against a North Carolina defense that has been competitive this season.</cite>

Quarterback Darian Mensah has been the engine of it all season. Through eight games he's thrown for 2,425 yards, 25 touchdowns and just one interception, per BVM Sports' matchup preview. His top target, sophomore Malachi Toney, has turned into the best receiver in the country regardless of class — <cite index="45-2,45-3">952 receiving yards, ranked third nationally, with 11 total touchdowns tied for fifth</cite> entering the North Carolina game. Belichick, coaching against him that week, didn't bother hedging when asked how to slow him down: <cite index="47-4,47-5,47-6,47-7">"You would think it would, but not really. He's made several big plays off double coverage because he knows how to beat double coverage. At his age, that's pretty impressive. I don't see any weak points in his game."</cite> That's not spin from a Miami staffer. That's an opposing coach with six Super Bowl rings conceding there's no answer.

## Why Notre Dame is the whole season

Everything else on Miami's schedule is secondary to Saturday. <cite index="38-7">After Notre Dame, each game is a home game for the Hurricanes, who are on pace to play Wake Forest in the ACC Championship game</cite> — a rematch of their season opener. The committee's rankings tonight will reflect eight weeks of results, but the number next to Miami's name is about to be tested against the one team that beat the Irish in their own building last year and still watched the committee treat the two programs as equals for most of the fall.

<cite index="32-6,32-7">For weeks last year, the panel refused to elevate Miami ahead of Notre Dame even though both had two losses, and one of the Irish losses came against the Hurricanes on opening week. Then, when the bracket came out, the Canes were in and the Irish were out.</cite> That history is why this game carries weight beyond one Saturday in November. Notre Dame has spent a year believing the committee owed it something. Miami has spent a year proving the committee was right the first time.

The matchup itself favors chaos. <cite index="25-4,25-5,25-6">Notre Dame ranks second in the country in adjusted EPA per play, with Miami not far behind at seventh, while Miami has the No. 1 offense in adjusted EPA per play and EPA per pass while ranking second in success rate.</cite> Oddsmakers have installed Notre Dame as a home favorite, but the number has moved during the week, a sign nobody is fully confident in either side shutting the other down. Miami's defense has been the quieter story of the season — <cite index="22-7,22-9">fifth in the FBS in points allowed per game and 14th allowing just 32% of third downs converted</cite> — but it will face a Notre Dame offense that's been getting healthier, not worse, since its only loss.

<cite index="32-9,32-10">Notre Dame is ranked fourth in the AP poll, having been leapfrogged by Utah after an unconvincing win over Navy, and the rest of its schedule ranks 37th — only five spots below Miami's — meaning a second loss could drop the Irish behind Miami in several of the metrics the committee says it weighs.</cite> In other words: lose Saturday, and Notre Dame's margin for error on résumé alone disappears.

Tonight's rankings are a formality everyone already expects. Saturday in South Bend is the actual test, and it's the only game on Miami's schedule between now and December that can end the undefeated season instead of just decorating it.
