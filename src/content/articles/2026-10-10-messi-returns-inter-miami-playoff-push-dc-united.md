Lionel Messi cried on a team bus in Buenos Aires on Tuesday. By Friday he was back on a training field in Fort Lauderdale, and Saturday night he's expected to play for Inter Miami against D.C. United with a playoff seed on the line.

That turnaround is the story right now, not the tears. <cite index="59-1">Messi brought the curtain down on his 21-year international career on Tuesday, bidding farewell to adoring Argentina fans in an emotional final appearance for the former world champions in Buenos Aires.</cite> <cite index="59-6">In a perfectly scripted finale, the 39-year-old signed off with his 126th international goal in front of 85,000 supporters as Argentina cruised to a 3-0 victory over Benin.</cite> He scored a penalty, set up two more goals, and by all accounts left nothing on the field in a shirt he'll never wear again.

Then he got on a plane. <cite index="72-3,72-4">Messi has resumed first-team training with Inter Miami after bidding an emotional farewell to the Argentina national team in Buenos Aires, with the veteran forward now fully focused on steering the Florida club through the final weeks of the regular Major League Soccer campaign ahead of their anticipated postseason championship push.</cite> That's the whiplash of being 39 and still the best player on a team trying to defend an MLS Cup.

## A Coach Who Knows Exactly What He Just Watched

Inter Miami's locker room has a unique vantage point on all of this. Head coach Cristian "Kily" González didn't just watch Messi's farewell from the sideline — <cite index="87-10">his club career earned him a spot on the Argentina national team, where he played alongside current Inter Miami captain Lionel Messi in 2005, when González and Messi were teammates for two World Cup qualifiers against Peru and Uruguay.</cite>

That history showed on Friday when González addressed the moment publicly for the first time since returning to club business. <cite index="91-5,91-6,91-7,91-8,91-9">"[The farewell match] was incredibly intense," González said. "Obviously, I was very close to him, but ultimately, that moment belonged to him, it was his time. All that mattered was for him to enjoy it. I just told him to enjoy it, to soak up all the love, not just from Argentina, but from the whole world. He'll be missed in that jersey, of course, but today we have him here, and we have to enjoy having him with us now."</cite>

González has only been in the job since mid-September, the third man to run this team in 2026 after taking over for Guillermo Hoyos, who had taken over for Javier Mascherano in April. He's been handed a roster that just watched its best player pour his emotional reserves into a going-away party for a different team, and his job now is making sure that doesn't cost Miami anything over the next seven weeks. <cite index="91-12,91-13,91-14">"We're in a good place," González said. "The guys who returned from their respective national teams are doing really well. We've trained effectively, and we know we need to get back to winning, that's a given."</cite>

## The Race Hasn't Waited for Anybody

That "get back to winning" line isn't filler. Miami enters tonight coming off a loss to Columbus Crew, and the conference table has gotten crowded under Nashville. <cite index="72-9,72-10">Currently third on 46 points from 27 outings, the Herons remain level with second-placed New England while seeking to chip away at Nashville's commanding 14-point cushion, with a top-seven finish over the remaining fixtures needed to guarantee direct qualification to the Best-of-3 series and avoid a precarious wild-card game.</cite>

That's the stakes for a club that won the whole thing last December. Nobody is debating whether Inter Miami makes the playoffs. The argument is over seeding, and seeding is the difference between hosting a win-or-go-home series in front of a sold-out Nu Stadium crowd and flying across the country for one.

Tonight's opponent won't make headlines on its own — D.C. United sits buried in the middle of the conference table — but the timing matters. It's the first match back for a group of players scattered across multiple international windows, Messi's farewell chief among them, and it's a chance to answer the only question that matters in October: whether a team that just handed its talisman an emotional week can turn around and be ruthless again by Saturday night.

Nu Stadium hosts its usual pregame buildup — <cite index="34-11">Puerto Rican singer Luis Figueroa will perform the national anthem</cite> as part of the night's sponsored festivities — but none of that changes what Miami needs from the match itself. Three points keep them level with New England. Anything less and the gap to home-field advantage starts looking like the gap to Nashville: wide, and closing time is running out.
