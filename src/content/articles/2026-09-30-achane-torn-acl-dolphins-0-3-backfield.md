The Dolphins got the diagnosis they feared on Monday. De'Von Achane suffered a torn ACL in his left knee during Miami's 24-10 loss to Kansas City on September 27, and he will miss the rest of the 2026 season.

The injury happened on Achane's third carry of the day, an 11-yard gain on Miami's opening drive at Hard Rock Stadium. <cite index="32-8">Kansas City safety Alohi Gilman grasped him by the top of the helmet as he made the tackle, twisting Achane's body while his left leg was planted.</cite> Achane walked off under his own power, spent a few minutes in the medical tent, then headed to the locker room. He didn't return.

Jeff Hafley didn't wait for test results to sound alarmed. <cite index="32-4">After the game, the head coach said it "does not sound very optimistic" regarding the injury that sidelined Achane.</cite> By Monday, the fear was confirmed. Speaking to reporters after the diagnosis came back, Hafley didn't hide how personal the loss felt. <cite index="31-4,31-5">He said his heart breaks for Achane, praising how he'd grown into a great teammate with great energy.</cite>

## A Season Built Around Him, Now Without Him

The timing is brutal by any measure. <cite index="20-12,20-13">Achane was coming off the best season of his career in 2025, rushing for 1,350 yards and eight touchdowns while adding 488 receiving yards and four more scores through the air, production that earned him his first Pro Bowl selection.</cite> He finishes 2026 with almost nothing to show for it: <cite index="25-6">34 carries for 127 yards through three games, along with seven receptions for 49 yards.</cite>

Miami had just made him a centerpiece of its rebuild. According to reporting on the injury, Achane had recently signed a four-year extension worth $64 million, with $32 million guaranteed — a deal that assumed he'd be the engine of Jeff Hafley's offense for years, not weeks. Instead, the Dolphins now have to plan around his absence for the rest of a season that already started 0-3.

## Who Actually Replaces Him

There isn't a clean answer, and Hafley knows it. Ollie Gordon II took over as the lead back once Achane exited Sunday and finished with 17 carries for 41 yards and a touchdown — workmanlike production that also exposed how hard it is to simply plug in a replacement. <cite index="34-11">His 2.4 yards per carry underscored the difficulty of sustaining the running game without more opportunity automatically replacing the production Miami had gotten from its starter.</cite>

Gordon dealt with cramping late in the game, which meant Miami had to get creative. Wide receiver Malik Washington lined up at running back in a pinch. Hafley later explained the decision wasn't improvised — the staff had planned to use Washington in that role and felt comfortable enough with it that they didn't dress a third true running back. Jaylen Wright, the other option in the room, was inactive with a stinger.

Hafley insists the approach doesn't change. <cite index="33-6,33-7,33-8">He said his philosophy remains that he wants to run the football, that the offensive line is built for it, and that he has a lot of confidence in the backs on the roster.</cite> That's the coach-speak version. The honest version is that Miami now has to manufacture a running game around a rookie sixth-round pick and a converted receiver, and general manager Jon-Eric Sullivan is reportedly weighing whether the position needs outside help before Week 4.

## Why This Is the Story That Matters

Miami's 0-3 start already raised questions about whether this roster can compete in Hafley's first year. Losing Achane doesn't just cost the Dolphins their most explosive skill player — it removes the one player capable of creating something out of nothing behind a rebuilding offense. Everything about Miami's 2026 plan, from the coaching change to the receiver overhaul to the youngest roster in the league, assumed Achane would be the connective tissue holding the offense together while everything else got sorted out.

Now that assumption is gone for the year, and the Dolphins have to find out — in real time, against real opponents — who that guy is instead. Vikings defensive coordinator Brian Flores, preparing to face Miami in Week 4, has already been blunt about what he expects from a Dolphins offense that just lost its best player. The next several weeks will show whether Gordon, Washington, or a midseason addition can even approximate what Miami is missing.
