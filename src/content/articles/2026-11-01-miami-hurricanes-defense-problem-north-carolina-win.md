No. 1 Miami is 8-0. It also just needed a fourth-quarter fumble recovery to beat a North Carolina team that came in unranked and finished the night 4-4. That gap between the record and the performance is the story coming out of Chapel Hill.

The Hurricanes beat the Tar Heels 38-35 on Halloween night, <cite index="20-1">ending a five-game losing skid in the matchup and winning in Chapel Hill for the first time since the 2017 season</cite>. The box score reads like a track meet: Miami put up 416 total yards, North Carolina answered with 407. UNC quarterback <cite index="32-6">Edwards Jr. carved them up for 341 yards and four touchdowns on 28-of-34 passing</cite>. For a defense that entered the year as the supposed foundation of a playoff run, allowing a sub-.500 team to hang 35 points and drive 75 yards in under a minute late in the fourth quarter is not a one-off. It's a pattern.

## The defense keeps doing this

<cite index="32-8,32-9">Since Corey Hetherman has been Miami's defensive coordinator, his unit had not given up more than 26 points in a game — until the Hurricanes defense allowed 35 points in two of their last three games played this season.</cite> Against North Carolina specifically, <cite index="32-7">Miami's secondary and linebackers had difficulty matching up with UNC's tight ends and receivers for most of the night, and the defense struggled to get off the field on third down.</cite> PFF's grading backed that up, showing Darian Mensah grading out elite while the Miami defense dealt with soft zones, missed tackles and inconsistent pressure that let Edwards operate from a clean pocket most of the night.

Miami's saving grace Saturday was a takeaway, not a stop. With the Hurricanes clinging to a 31-28 lead, the defensive line forced and recovered a fumble that set up Mensah's game-sealing touchdown throw to Malachi Toney. North Carolina scored again with 55 seconds left anyway. This isn't the formula that wins in January. Teams that are going to beat Miami in December and beyond are going to have the ball with a chance to erase a seven- or ten-point deficit in the final two minutes, and Saturday suggested the Hurricanes' defense will let them stay in it long enough to try.

## Fletcher's record night, and why it mattered more than usual

The reason Miami survived is that its best player had the best game of his career at the moment it needed it. Running back Mark Fletcher Jr. scored three touchdowns — two rushing, one receiving — and in the process became Miami's all-time leader in career touchdowns scored, passing Edgerrin James and Stephen McGuire, who had shared the record at 35 since 1992. His 31-yard touchdown catch in the second quarter made him the sole leader with 36; his two short touchdown runs later tied him with McGuire for Miami's career rushing touchdown mark. The second of those, a fourth-and-1 conversion with under eight minutes left, put Miami ahead for good.

Mensah, for his part, was close to perfect: 22-of-29 for 274 yards and three touchdowns with no interceptions, adding 36 rushing yards on scrambles that kept drives alive on third down. That's the formula Miami has leaned on all season — an offense good enough to outscore its mistakes. It worked again. But it's worth remembering that the 2024 Hurricanes ran the same play: an explosive offense carrying a defense that couldn't get stops, and it cost them a spot in the College Football Playoff.

## What actually matters now

Miami is undefeated, ranked No. 1, and <cite index="1-9">currently boasts a 98% chance to make the CFP</cite> according to one projection cited after the win. None of that changes what Saturday exposed. The Hurricanes have allowed 35 or more points in two of their last three games, and their coordinator's track record of tighter defense has evaporated over that stretch. Miami hosts Duke and Virginia Tech before closing at home against Boston College, then travels to Notre Dame in between. Good offenses can paper over bad defensive nights in November against four-win teams. They tend to get exposed in January.
