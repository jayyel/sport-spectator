Giannis Antetokounmpo puts on a Heat uniform for the first time in a real game on Saturday, when Miami opens its preseason against Toronto at Videotron Centre in Quebec City. It's the first NBA preseason game ever played in the city, and it marks the end of a 13-year run in Milwaukee for a two-time MVP who has never worn another jersey as a pro.

The game itself won't count. But it's the first live look at a roster Miami remade almost entirely to get him.

## What Miami gave up

The trade that sent Antetokounmpo to Miami closed in late June, on the eve of the draft, after months of speculation about his future in Milwaukee. The Heat sent out Tyler Herro, Kel'el Ware, Jaime Jaquez Jr. and Kasparas Jakučionis, along with three first-round picks, a 2030 first-round swap and a 2033 second-round pick, to land Antetokounmpo and forward Bobby Portis. <cite index="41-12,41-13,41-14">Antetokounmpo and Bobby Portis headed to Miami for Wisconsin native Tyler Herro, Jaime Jaquez Jr., Kel'el Ware and Kasparas Jakučionis, plus the No. 13 pick in the 2026 NBA Draft, a first-round swap in 2030, first-round picks in 2031 and '33 and a second-round pick in 2033.</cite>

It was, by any measure, one of the steepest prices an NBA team has paid for a single player. <cite index="42-10,42-11">Antetokounmpo had been linked to the Heat for years, dating back as far as his first potential stab at unrestricted free agency in 2021.</cite> Milwaukee reportedly had a competing offer on the table from Boston built around Jaylen Brown, but <cite index="44-9">the Bucks opted to go with a package centered around youth and draft picks instead.</cite>

Miami didn't stop there. It signed Klay Thompson and Tim Hardaway Jr. over the summer to surround Antetokounmpo and Bam Adebayo with outside shooting — two moves team president Erik Spoelstra says wouldn't have happened without Adebayo's pull around the league. Spoelstra has credited Adebayo's reputation among peers as a factor in convincing free agents to choose Miami this offseason.

## Why the Heat needed this

The context matters. Miami missed the playoffs last season for the first time in seven years, and the franchise hasn't won a playoff series since 2023 despite two Finals trips in the Jimmy Butler era — both of which ended in losses. This is Pat Riley's answer to that drought: pair a two-time MVP with the center who's anchored the roster for a decade, then build shooting around them.

Antetokounmpo is entering the final season of his current contract and becomes extension-eligible with Miami six months after the trade closed, meaning the Heat could lock him into a four-year max well before free agency ever becomes a conversation. For now, the priority is getting him acclimated. At media day this week, Antetokounmpo talked about the adjustment to a new system and a new head coach, saying the goal is simple: win a championship, the way so many players who've come through Miami have. He's also been open about the growing pains of camp, describing the pace of practice as something he's still catching up to, and the daily matchup with Adebayo as players pushing each other to be sharper.

## What to watch Saturday

Don't expect heavy minutes for the stars in an exhibition opener nearly three weeks before the regular season starts. The more interesting subplot might be depth. Backup point guard Dru Smith strained his calf in camp and will miss several weeks, thinning out Miami's options behind the starters right as rotations are being settled. That's a small thing against the scale of acquiring Antetokounmpo, but it's the kind of detail that actually shapes October roster decisions while the trade is still just a transaction on paper.

Miami plays two more preseason games after Quebec City — hosting New Orleans and Minnesota at Kaseya Center — before the regular season opens October 20. Saturday won't answer whether the bet pays off. It's just the first time anyone outside a practice facility gets to watch the pieces move together.
