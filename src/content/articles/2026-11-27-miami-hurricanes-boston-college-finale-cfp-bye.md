Miami already has what it needed from this week. The Hurricanes <cite index="19-1,19-4">overcame a 14-point deficit to beat Virginia Tech 28-20 and clinch a berth in the ACC Championship Game</cite> last Saturday, locking in a trip to Charlotte before Boston College even entered the conversation. But the No. 7 Hurricanes (10-1, 8-0 ACC) still have business to finish Saturday at noon at Hard Rock Stadium, and it's not nothing.

A win over Boston College would mark something Miami has never done: <cite index="24-3">the program's first-ever sweep of the Atlantic Coast Conference regular season</cite>. It would also keep alive a path to a first-round playoff bye that looked like a long shot a month ago and now looks very real.

## The Bye Math Actually Works

Miami has sat at No. 7 in the CFP rankings for weeks despite winning, mostly because the committee keeps finding teams ahead of it that also keep winning. One analyst tracking the bracket noted <cite index="34-11,34-12,34-13,34-14">No. 7 Miami and No. 8 Wake Forest were left to play in the ACC, and the winner has a real, legitimate path to a No. 4 seed, given how fairly the committee has treated the Hurricanes</cite>. The same analysis argued that <cite index="34-15,34-16,34-17">this week's ranking should lock in a home game in the first round for Miami at worst, regardless of what happens against Wake Forest on Dec. 5</cite>.

That's the case for not looking past Saturday. Boston College is 5-6 and <cite index="24-7">sits at 5-6 and desperately needs a victory to reach bowl eligibility</cite>, which makes this exactly the kind of game that trips up teams with bigger things on the calendar. Miami was <cite index="22-9">voted the overwhelming favorite to win the 17-team ACC</cite> back in the preseason, and a stumble now against a team picked 16th would be the wrong note to end the regular season on, no matter how it affects seeding.

## Fletcher's Last Home Game, Toney's Countdown

This is Senior Day, and the headline name is running back Mark Fletcher Jr., who has spent the back half of November rewriting Miami's record book. Against Duke two weeks ago, <cite index="55-2,55-3">Fletcher scored his 36th career rushing touchdown, breaking a school record that had stood for 34 years</cite>, passing former Hurricanes great Stephen McGuire. He'd already taken the combined rushing-and-receiving touchdown record two weeks before that, <cite index="47-2,47-5">scoring his 36th total touchdown to become the sole leader in program history, passing Stephen McGuire and Edgerrin James</cite> for that mark. Fletcher turned down the NFL after a playoff run a year ago to come back for this season, and Saturday is his last home game in front of the Hard Rock crowd.

The other storyline worth watching is receiver Malachi Toney, who hasn't played since suffering a high ankle sprain before the Notre Dame game and <cite index="38-1">has missed the last three games with the injury</cite>. He's trending the right direction. Miami's initial ACC availability report this week <cite index="42-1,42-2">listed Toney as doubtful for Saturday, while offensive lineman Max Buchanan and cornerback Ja'Boree Antoine were each listed as questionable</cite>, a step up from being ruled out the previous three weeks. Cristobal said earlier in the week that <cite index="41-10,41-11">Toney was already running nearly 20 miles per hour and needed roughly a week and a half before he'd be ready to play</cite>, which lines up with a possible return for the ACC Championship Game rather than this weekend. Losing Toney for four straight games would wreck most offenses; Miami has kept winning anyway, which says as much about the supporting cast as it does about how good this team has been built to withstand injuries.

## What Saturday Actually Decides

None of this changes Miami's ACC Championship date. The Hurricanes play Wake Forest in Charlotte on Dec. 5 regardless of what happens against Boston College. What Saturday decides is smaller but still real: whether Miami finishes the regular season unbeaten in conference play for the first time, whether the committee has any excuse to drop the Hurricanes out of bye position, and whether Fletcher's final home start ends with a win instead of an afterthought. For a team that's been treated gently by the rankings all season, there's no reason to test that goodwill against a team playing for its season.
