The Dolphins are 0-2, and Sunday's loss at Levi's Stadium made clear why oddsmakers pegged this team as one of the worst in the league before the season even started. San Francisco scored touchdowns on its first five possessions and rolled to a 35-13 win that wasn't nearly that close for stretches.

Christian McCaffrey became the fourth-fastest player in the Super Bowl era to reach 100 career touchdowns, and Brock Purdy was close to perfect, <cite index="21-5">going 20 for 22 for 287 yards with two touchdown passes and one touchdown run, completing 18 straight passes at one point</cite>. The 49ers improved to 2-0 and, <cite index="21-2">after also beating the Los Angeles Rams in the season opener in Australia, began the year with back-to-back wins of at least 20 points for the first time in franchise history</cite>.

Miami actually hung around for a half. The Dolphins won time of possession in the first half and trailed only 14-6 at the break, with two field goals to show for their trouble in the red zone. Then the 49ers scored 21 unanswered points in the third quarter and turned it into a laugher.

## A Rebuild With No Cushion

This isn't the same Dolphins team longtime fans watched implode in prior seasons. Miami tore the roster down over the winter, parting with Tua Tagovailoa, Tyreek Hill and Jaylen Waddle, and handed the offense to first-year head coach Jeff Hafley and free-agent quarterback Malik Willis, who Hafley knew from their overlapping time in Green Bay. The Dolphins currently carry more than $180 million in dead salary-cap money, a direct consequence of how aggressively they cleared out the old core.

Willis' final numbers Sunday — 14-of-23 for 197 yards and a touchdown — look modest until you notice that 77 of those yards came on a single fourth-quarter touchdown pass to Ryan Miller against San Francisco's backups, with the Dolphins already down big. Take that play away and Willis managed barely 120 yards on his other 22 attempts. San Francisco's front, playing without needing to sell out against the run, kept Willis from doing the one thing that made him valuable in Green Bay: escaping with his legs. He was held to six rushing yards on two carries after piling up 39 rushing yards and a touchdown in Week 1's loss at Las Vegas.

That's the real problem for Miami right now. Willis' appeal as a stopgap starter was always tied to his mobility. When a defense as talented as San Francisco's takes that away, there isn't much of a backup plan behind him — Miami trimmed its quarterback room down to almost nothing over the summer, and the receiving corps beyond Willis' top two options is thin enough that a second-half ankle injury to rookie Caleb Douglas mattered.

## What Hafley Said, And What Comes Next

Hafley didn't try to spin it. <cite index="29-5,29-6,29-7,29-8">"Really disappointing," Dolphins coach Jeff Hafley said. "Thought we had a great week of preparation, and I felt like we were ready to play better. In the first half we did. In the second half, just not good enough in any phase."</cite>

Linebacker Jordyn Brooks tried to keep the locker room from reading too much into it, saying flatly that <cite index="29-9,29-10">it won't unravel</cite>. That's the right instinct for a team that knew going in this season was about developing pieces, not chasing wins. But the margin for error was already thin, and it just got thinner. Miami was outscored 62-26 through two weeks, a number that doesn't leave much room to argue this is simply a slow start.

The schedule doesn't offer relief. The Dolphins host Kansas City in their home opener next Sunday at Hard Rock Stadium — a Chiefs team that, even coming off a playoff miss, still has more talent up and down the roster than Miami's rebuilt shell of a roster. If Willis and this offense can't find a way to finish drives against a defense that isn't nearly as complete as San Francisco's, the conversation in South Florida will shift quickly from "what pieces can we develop" to "how bad is this actually going to get."

For now, the answer through two weeks is: bad enough that the good half of Sunday's game barely registered by the final whistle.
