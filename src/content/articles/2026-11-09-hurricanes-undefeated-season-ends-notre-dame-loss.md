Miami's perfect season lasted nine games. It ended Saturday night in South Bend, where Notre Dame beat the No. 1 Hurricanes 31-24 and did it by building a lead Miami couldn't quite erase.

The Fighting Irish jumped out early and <cite index="21-4">took a 24-3 lead in the third quarter after three touchdown passes</cite> from quarterback CJ Carr, who <cite index="20-8">had his best game of the year, passing for 319 yards and 3 touchdowns</cite>. Miami's defense held up against the run — <cite index="22-4">limiting the Irish to just 93 yards rushing on 29 carries</cite> — but couldn't contain Carr through the air, and by halftime <cite index="22-5">Notre Dame led 17-3</cite> before extending the margin further.

Darian Mensah and the offense spent the second half trying to climb out of the hole. Mensah <cite index="22-7">finished with 276 passing yards and three touchdowns, completing 24 of 43 passes — leading Miami to 21 second-half points</cite>. Without top receiver Malachi Toney, Miami found other answers: <cite index="45-7">Cooper Barkate had 10 catches for 92 yards and two touchdowns and tight end Lofton had seven catches for 113 yards and another score</cite>. It still wasn't enough. A third-quarter interception let Notre Dame restore a two-score cushion, and the Hurricanes spent the rest of the night chasing it.

## The Play Everyone's Still Talking About

With under four minutes left and Miami trailing, the game turned on a single sequence. On fourth-and-6 from the Notre Dame 18, Mensah found tight end Elija Lofton in the end zone. <cite index="49-7,49-8,49-9">The ruling came on fourth-and-6 from the Notre Dame 18 with under four minutes to play. Lofton, Miami's tight end, appeared to make a catch near the boundary of the end zone that would have put Miami one extra point from tying the game. Notre Dame safety Luke Talich dislodged the ball as Lofton stepped out of bounds, and the officials ruled the pass incomplete.</cite>

Mario Cristobal did not take it well. <cite index="54-6,54-7,54-8">"Really disappointed that they negated Elija Lofton's touchdown with two hands on the ball and two feet in bounds. But like we told our team before we left Miami, do not leave it in the hands of officials because that's what you're going to get here," Cristobal said. "Very disappointed with the way that was called with the game on the line and clear possession of the football in bounds."</cite> He said afterward that he'd gotten <cite index="51-11">"zero" explanation of the ruling</cite>.

Not everyone bought the outrage. <cite index="52-7">NBC rules analyst Terry McAulay backed the officials on the broadcast, saying Lofton never finished the catch before Talich knocked the ball out</cite>. By Sunday, Cristobal had walked back the heat of his comments, with reports indicating he later said the blame belonged to Miami itself rather than the officiating crew.

## What It Costs Miami

The loss did real damage to Miami's playoff positioning, just not to its season. The Hurricanes dropped from No. 1 to No. 5 in the AP poll, and ESPN's bracket projection slotted them at No. 6 nationally. <cite index="44-6,44-7">Without the win against the Irish, Miami's best victory was Sept. 18 at Wake Forest. The Hurricanes have four road wins against ACC teams, but now lack a true statement victory.</cite> A shot at a top-four seed and a first-round bye is probably gone.

The path to the playoff itself, though, remains entirely in Miami's hands. <cite index="38-1,38-2">Miami currently has a two-game lead over Louisville, Duke, SMU, and Clemson in the loss column in the ACC standings. If Miami wins two of its last three games, the Hurricanes clinch a berth in the ACC Championship Game</cite> — and a win there locks up an automatic playoff bid regardless of where the committee ranks them. <cite index="44-8">As devastating as the loss to Notre Dame was for Miami's chance at the top seed and a first-round bye, the Canes still have the best chance of any team in the ACC to reach the conference championship game and win it, which would lock up a CFP bid.</cite>

That march starts this weekend. Miami hosts Duke at Hard Rock Stadium, with Virginia Tech and Boston College still to come. None of those three games is unwinnable. But after nine months of being the team nobody could beat, the Hurricanes now know exactly what it feels like to be chasing instead of being chased — and they have three weeks to prove the Notre Dame loss was a blip, not the start of a pattern.
