Miami is the No. 1 team in college football for the first time since Dec. 8, 2002. That's not a typo and it's not a hot-take ranking from some podcast — it's the actual AP Top 25, released Sunday, with the Hurricanes <cite index="20-9">receiving 1,697 points with 40 first-place votes after receiving 1,594 points and two first-place votes last week</cite>.

Twenty-four years is a long time. <cite index="23-3">Nearly an entire generation of Canes have never experienced what it's like to be the top football team in the country</cite>. The last time it happened, George W. Bush was in his first term and the Hurricanes were mid-streak in a run that saw them <cite index="20-11,20-12,20-13">ranked No. 1 in the Dec. 8, 2002 poll as they spent 16 of 17 weeks that season ranked No. 1 before falling to No. 2 Ohio State in the national championship game</cite>.

## How they got there

Miami didn't just back into this. The Hurricanes earned it Saturday night by beating Florida State 43-35 in double overtime at Hard Rock Stadium, improving to 6-0 and 4-0 in the ACC. It was not clean. <cite index="35-8,35-9,35-10">Malachi Toney had a legacy game, with 11 catches for 154 yards, two touchdowns, and an all-crucial two-point conversion pass in overtime, while Javian Mallory led the ground attack with 108 yards on 13 carries and two touchdowns, and Darian Mensah was 23/34 for 326 yards with two touchdowns and an interception.</cite>

The ending was as chaotic as the ranking jump. Florida State thought it had tied the game late in the second overtime on a fourth-and-21 heave, but <cite index="38-11,38-12">Florida State quarterback Ashton Daniels connected on a fourth and 21, 36 yard heave that initially appeared to give the Seminoles a chance to make it 43-41, but a holding penalty wiped out the play</cite>. On the final snap, <cite index="36-8">the Hurricanes defense held the Seminoles on their ensuing possession, with Bryce Fitzgerald intercepting Florida State's fourth-down Hail Mary attempt on the game's final play</cite>.

What pushed Miami over the top wasn't just the win — it was everyone else losing at the same time. <cite index="23-8">Saturday marked the first time in the AP Poll's history (1936) that five of the top-seven teams lost on the same day.</cite> Georgia, Notre Dame, Indiana, Alabama and Texas all went down, clearing a runway that Miami walked through by beating an unranked FSU team in overtime rather than cruising.

## The Pitt problem

Here's the part that should worry anyone in Coral Gables who remembers 2017. Miami's next opponent, Saturday at noon at Hard Rock Stadium, is Pittsburgh — the same program that ended the Hurricanes' last great winning streak. That year, Miami carried a 15-game win streak and a No. 2 ranking into Heinz Field as a heavy favorite against an unranked Pitt team. <cite index="46-4,46-5">Freshman quarterback Kenny Pickett ran for two touchdowns and threw for another as Pittsburgh stunned the second-ranked Hurricanes 24-14</cite>, and that result turned into the headline that knocked Miami out of playoff position entirely.

Nobody in the building is pretending otherwise. Mario Cristobal, asked directly about carrying the No. 1 ranking into this stretch, <cite index="20-7,20-8">said he thinks "we have a good enough sample size of 2026 to know that's absolutely insignificant and irrelevant with the changing of the guard from week to week," referencing losses to Georgia and Notre Dame earlier in the day</cite>. The program's own fan outlets have flagged the same concern: <cite index="12-6,12-7">the Hurricanes face four emotionally challenging games against Pitt, North Carolina, Notre Dame and Duke, with last year's post-FSU collapse against Louisville serving as a warning, while offensive coordinator Shannon Dawson wants cleaner execution despite 583 yards against FSU</cite>.

That stretch doesn't get easier after Pitt. Miami goes on the road to North Carolina on Oct. 31, then to Notre Dame on Nov. 7 — a trip to South Bend against a team that's already shown this season it can beat ranked opponents — before closing with Duke, Virginia Tech and Boston College.

The ranking is real and it means something historically. But the 2002 team that Miami is being compared to also started strong before a late stumble cost it a championship. The number next to Miami's name changes nothing about Saturday's matchup against the one program with a very specific history of making the Hurricanes pay for believing their own hype.
