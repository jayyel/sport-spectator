Miami didn't blink in its first game as the No. 1 team in the country. The Hurricanes beat Pittsburgh 52-23 on Saturday at Hard Rock Stadium, and the final score undersold how comfortable it was. By halftime, Miami led 31-7 and Malachi Toney already had 159 receiving yards on 12 catches — more than Pitt's entire offense had produced to that point.

This was supposed to be the dangerous week. Three straight AP No. 1 teams had been upset by unranked opponents before Miami ever got there — Texas fell to Oklahoma, then Georgia lost at home to Auburn the very week the Hurricanes took over the top spot. <cite index="24-15,24-16">The win kept Miami atop the poll, ending a three-week run of the No. 1 team dropping after a loss.</cite> Pitt coach Pat Narduzzi put it plainly afterward: <cite index="24-5,24-6,24-7,24-8">"We ran into a buzz saw. That's a good football team. They're the No. 1 team in the country and it's no joke."</cite>

## The numbers behind the ranking

<cite index="23-9,23-10">The Canes first climbed to No. 1 following their 43-35 double-overtime victory over rival Florida State, claiming the top ranking for the first time in 24 years.</cite> Against Pitt, quarterback Darian Mensah completed 31 of 37 passes for 387 yards and four touchdowns. Three of those scores went to Toney, who finished with <cite index="26-10">194 receiving yards and three touchdown catches</cite> on a school-record 16 receptions, breaking his own mark of 13 set a week earlier. Running back Mark Fletcher Jr. also moved into the No. 2 spot on Miami's career rushing touchdown list.

The broader context matters as much as the box score. <cite index="23-5,23-8">The Hurricanes remain atop the Associated Press Top 25 for a second consecutive week, one of just three undefeated teams left in college football, and 1977 marked the last time this few unbeaten teams remained this late in a season.</cite> Cristobal has tried to wave off the significance all month, saying before the FSU game that rankings are <cite index="29-17">"absolutely insignificant and irrelevant with the changing of the guard from week to week."</cite> His team keeps winning anyway, and it's hard to ignore that <cite index="31-12,31-13,31-14,31-15">the last time Miami was ranked No. 1 was the Dec. 8, 2002 poll, a season it spent 16 of 17 weeks on top before losing to Ohio State in the national championship.</cite>

## Belichick's biggest test yet walks in

None of that history matters if Miami stumbles on the road Saturday. The Hurricanes travel to Chapel Hill to face a North Carolina team that looks nothing like the one that finished 4-8 a year ago under Bill Belichick. The Tar Heels are 4-3, and two weeks ago they beat a ranked Pittsburgh team 21-17 — Belichick's first win over a ranked opponent as a college coach, and North Carolina's first win over a ranked team since 2023.

That win came off a fourth-quarter comeback, with quarterback Billy Edwards Jr. throwing a 75-yard touchdown to tight end Shamar Easter to take the lead for good. It's the kind of result that suggests this UNC team, while still unranked and still carrying a losing conference record at 2-2, is capable of playing one good half against anybody. Hosting the nation's No. 1 team with a signature win already on the resume is a different kind of occasion than facing Clemson or Pitt.

Miami has a clear statistical edge in almost every category — Narduzzi noted after facing the Hurricanes that <cite index="24-9,24-10,24-11,24-12,24-13">they're averaging 45 points a game and rank No. 1, No. 2 or No. 3 in just about every category</cite> nationally. But the Hurricanes also have one ranked opponent left on the regular-season schedule in Notre Dame on Nov. 7, and a trip to South Bend looms right behind this one. North Carolina isn't that game. It's the one that decides whether Miami gets there unbeaten.

The pressure, as the Hurricanes' campus paper put it this week, is real in a way this program hasn't felt in a generation. Every game from here is a measuring stick, and Saturday is the first one on the road since the ranking changed everything.
