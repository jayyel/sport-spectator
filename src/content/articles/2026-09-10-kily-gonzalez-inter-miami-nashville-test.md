Inter Miami has not lost since Cristian "Kily" González took the bench, but "not lost" is doing a lot of work in that sentence. The Herons drew Atlanta United 2-2 at home on Sept. 5. They drew Chicago Fire 1-1 on the road Wednesday night. Two games, two results salvaged by second-half goals, zero wins. Saturday, at Nu Stadium, they get a chance to find out whether the calm has actually translated into results — against a Nashville SC team that has given them almost nothing to work with all year.

González was hired Aug. 28 to clean up a mess. Miami had lost four of five across all competitions, interim coach Guillermo Hoyos was serving a suspension for violating the league's kickoff policy, and the defending MLS Cup champions looked nothing like a team defending anything. <cite index="33-4,33-5">Despite the presence of Lionel Messi and a host of established players, results had continued to disappoint, with the club losing four of its last five matches and sitting 10 points behind Nashville.</cite> González had to wait on a work permit before he could even take charge, so his predecessor's staff got one more game first — and Lionel Messi turned it into a party.

## The Bridge Game, Then the Real Job

<cite index="72-1">Inter Miami snapped its five-game winless streak in emphatic fashion, dismantling CF Montréal 7-1 behind a four-goal, one-assist performance from Lionel Messi</cite> on Aug. 30, with assistant Rafael Perez running the bench while González's paperwork cleared. It was the largest margin of victory in club history, and it meant González inherited a team playing its best soccer of the summer rather than one in outright crisis.

What he's gotten since is trickier. Against Atlanta, Miami built control before halftime, then <cite index="63-4">struggled to maintain that level after the break, allowing Atlanta to control the second half and eventually fight back for a point</cite> via a late penalty. Against Chicago on Wednesday, Robert Lewandowski beat Miami's back line inside 10 minutes, and it took a Messi corner and a Casemiro header to salvage a point on the road. Both results were fine. Neither was the identity reset a five-game winless run was supposed to demand.

There's a wrinkle to Wednesday's game worth noting: it was Noah Allen's first trip back to a Miami lineup as an opponent. The academy product and club's all-time appearance leader was <cite index="82-4">loaned to Chicago through the 2026 MLS season with a permanent trade option</cite> just six days earlier, closing out a run that included three trophies and more than 130 appearances for the only club he'd ever played for. He started at left back for the Fire and helped hold Miami to a draw against his former team.

## What Saturday Actually Tests

Nashville is the reason none of this is academic. <cite index="92-13,92-14">González has done a great job with his team this season; they are currently in second place in the Eastern Conference standings, with 12 wins, four losses and seven draws for 43 points.</cite> Nashville sits comfortably ahead of them, and the gap has been real all year — Miami trailed by 10 points as far back as late August, and BJ Callaghan's team has given up almost nothing at home. Their defense has been the league's stingiest for stretches of the season, and they've been the only unbeaten home side in MLS this year heading into their recent slate.

That's the actual test for González. Two draws against playoff-caliber opponents is a defensible transition. A third one, at home, against the team sitting on top of the conference, starts to look like a pattern rather than an adjustment period. Miami's attack isn't the question — Messi, Suárez and Casemiro have scored enough goals to win most weeks. The problem all season has been control after the lead, the same issue that turned a 2-1 advantage over Atlanta into a draw and let Chicago pull level early before Miami had to chase the game.

There's also a calendar squeeze building. Miami hosts Cruz Azul on Sept. 16 in the Campeones Cup, then San Diego FC four days after that, with the Supporters' Shield and playoff seeding both still in reach if they can string results together. None of that gets easier if Saturday is draw No. 3.

González didn't create this team's problems — he inherited a roster that had a historic rout in it one week and a five-game winless skid the week before. But two months from now, nobody will remember the transition period. They'll remember whether he found a way to beat the team that's been the Eastern Conference's best all season. Saturday is the first real look at an answer.
