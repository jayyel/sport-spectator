Miami hosts Florida State Saturday night at Hard Rock Stadium with the Hurricanes ranked No. 3, sitting at 5-0, and getting healthier at exactly the right time. The Seminoles arrive at 3-3. On paper, this isn't close. In the sport's oldest in-state grudge match, that has never mattered much.

The Florida Cup trophy is on the line again, and Miami has won the last two meetings. FSU head coach Mike Norvell is in his seventh season in Tallahassee, and his team showed signs of life by beating Virginia for the Jefferson-Eppes Trophy, with quarterback <cite index="31-5">Ashton Daniels scoring five rushing touchdowns, setting a single-game school record for a quarterback.</cite> But FSU gives the ball away. Miami ranks 13th nationally in turnover margin at plus-1.0 per game, while <cite index="24-6,24-7">turnover margin per game is a huge predictor of success, and FSU sits 87th at -0.4.</cite>

## Mensah Is Playing Quarterback Better Than Anyone Else in the Country

The reason Miami is favored by whatever margin oddsmakers settle on isn't the defense. It's Darian Mensah, the Duke transfer who has turned the Hurricanes' offense into the most efficient operation in the country. Two games ago at Wake Forest, <cite index="38-1,38-2">Mensah set an Atlantic Coast Conference record with 24 consecutive completions and threw for 220 yards and three touchdowns</cite> in a 33-20 road win. The streak didn't start that night. <cite index="38-6">His 24 straight completions broke the ACC single-game record of 15, and his streak reached 35 consecutive completions dating to Miami's previous game against Florida A&M.</cite>

For context on how absurd that is, the previous ACC multi-game record belonged to Mitch Trubisky. Mensah blew past it by five completions, and he still came up short of the FBS record. <cite index="38-7,38-8">Mensah came within two passes of tying the FBS record for most consecutive completions in a game, a mark East Carolina's Dominique Davis set at 26 in 2011.</cite>

The volume stats back up the streak. <cite index="42-6">The kid has more touchdowns on the season than he does incompletions.</cite> That sentence was true two games ago and it's still true now. The only real critique anyone has found is that Miami's non-conference slate was light, which matters for Heisman voters and means nothing for Saturday.

## Cristobal Gets Bodies Back for the Biggest Game So Far

Miami used its bye week to find health rather than rest. Head coach Mario Cristobal told reporters this week that two defensive starters are fully cleared, saying <cite index="50-6,50-7,50-8,50-9">Ezekiel Marcelin is "all the way back," Lightfoot is all the way back, and Lucas is all the way back.</cite> Cornerback Xavier Lucas and edge rusher Marquise Lightfoot returning to full strength matters against an FSU offense that, turnovers aside, can still move the ball with Daniels at quarterback and a physical line in front of him.

Cristobal has made a point this week of refusing to treat the game as a formality, telling reporters that <cite index="52-7,52-9,52-10,52-11">an unranked team playing the number one team in the country doesn't mean anything if anyone assumes they're entitled to winning — "they're going to get beat."</cite> That's coach-speak, but it's also true of this rivalry specifically. FSU has beaten Miami as a significant underdog before, and the Seminoles have had two weeks since their last game to prepare for this one.

## What Actually Matters Saturday

Strip away the records and the rivalry flavor text, and the game comes down to whether Florida State can protect the ball against a defense that forces turnovers and whether its secondary can slow down a quarterback who hasn't been forced to punt much all season. Miami controls time of possession when Mensah is clicking, which starves the other team's defense of possessions and its offense of opportunities to erase a deficit.

A Miami win keeps the Hurricanes on pace for an unbeaten run into November, when a trip to Notre Dame looms as the real test of this season. A Florida State upset would be one of the bigger results in the rivalry's recent history, given where each program sits. Either way, Hard Rock Stadium will be full, the trophy will change hands or stay put, and the answer will be clearer by 10 p.m. Saturday than anything written this week.
