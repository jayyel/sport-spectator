Columbus didn't need a win over West Boca Raton on Friday. The Explorers had already locked up a spot in the semifinals of Florida's first-ever Open Division. They needed it anyway, for the top seed, for the home crowd at Tropical Park, and — if you believe what West Boca's coach said on the radio a week earlier — for the satisfaction of proving a guarantee wrong.

They got it. <cite index="63-10">Despite turning the ball over four times in the opening half, the Explorers overcame them as running back Cecil Smith scored the go-ahead touchdown in a 17-16 victory at Tropical Park on Black Friday.</cite> West Boca answered late, with Trey Moran scoring on a short run to cut it to one. <cite index="63-4">The ensuing point after was no good, securing the win for the Explorers</cite> — blocked by A'Mir Sears, who turned a nervy final minute into a celebration.

## A Guarantee That Backfired

The buildup had an edge to it. <cite index="48-9">West Boca coach Dylan Potts had gone on WESP-FM 106.3 and guaranteed a Bulls win against Columbus</cite>, even though <cite index="48-10">the game was only for seeding since both teams had already advanced to the semifinals</cite>. That talk gave an otherwise academic matchup real stakes. <cite index="42-15,42-16">Only Aquinas and Bishop Gorman had scored more than 13 points against Columbus all season, and West Boca's 17 points allowed were a season-high for the Explorers.</cite> Columbus made sure the guarantee meant nothing. <cite index="46-5">The Explorers secured the top spot with a 17-16 victory, completing a 3-0 run through pool play and leaving West Boca Raton in second place at 2-1.</cite>

That result matters more than the seeding line suggests. This is the inaugural year of the FHSAA's Open Division, an eight-team bracket built to pull the state's best teams — regardless of school size or classification — into one pool-play gauntlet and settle the argument about who's actually best on the field instead of in a poll. Columbus spent all season building a case to belong in that group. The Explorers debuted in the statewide rankings in September after <cite index="59-9,59-10">stunning nationally ranked Bishop Gorman of Nevada, 33-28</cite>, then lost a tight one on the road to St. Thomas Aquinas before reeling off a long string of blowouts that carried them into the national conversation. By mid-November, Columbus was a national top-20 team entering a playoff bracket designed exactly for a team like this.

## Chaminade-Madonna, and the Streak That Ended

Columbus now gets Chaminade-Madonna in the semifinal, and the Lions arrive having just done something bigger than anything that happened at Tropical Park. <cite index="51-7,51-9">Chaminade-Madonna earned its spot by defeating previously undefeated and nationally-ranked St. Thomas Aquinas 21-18</cite>, a program that had won seven straight state titles. <cite index="51-10,51-11,51-13">Trailing 18-14 with 1:44 left, quarterback Brady Quinn drove the Lions 80 yards on seven plays and found Peter Pierre for a 34-yard touchdown with 22 seconds remaining.</cite> That's the standard Columbus now has to match.

<cite index="46-4,47-4,47-5">The semifinal is set for Saturday, Dec. 5, at The Range at H.G. Morse Stadium in The Villages</cite>, with Lakeland facing West Boca Raton in the other half of the bracket. Whichever two teams win that weekend will play for the first Open Division state championship in Florida history — in Miami, at Pitbull Stadium on the FIU campus, the kind of symmetry a Miami program would notice.

## Why This One Is Different

Miami-Dade has produced playoff winners all fall, but most of that noise has come from public powerhouses like Miami Central and Miami Northwestern. Columbus, a Catholic school in West Miami-Dade that went 4-8 just two seasons ago under the same coach, Dave Dunn, climbing into the final four of a division built specifically to find the state's best team is the rarer story. The Explorers were mathematically through before Friday's game even kicked off. They played it like they weren't, and that's the difference between surviving a bracket and actually being built for one. Chaminade-Madonna just proved a seven-year dynasty can end in 22 seconds. Columbus is about to find out if it can finish what a blocked kick started.
