Miami kicks off at noon today against Pittsburgh at Hard Rock Stadium as the No. 1 team in both major polls, something the program hasn't been able to say since December 2002. The last time the Hurricanes held the top spot, the Orange Bowl was still standing and the Big East was still their conference. An entire generation of Miami fans has grown up hearing about that run without ever seeing the program actually hold it.

The timing isn't an accident. Athletic department officials scheduled Saturday's game to double as the 25-year reunion of the 2001 national championship team, and that group — regarded as one of the most talented rosters ever assembled, with a record 38 players eventually drafted into the NFL — will be honored in a halftime ceremony. The current Hurricanes are wearing uniforms modeled after that team's look for the occasion.

## How Miami got back here

The ascent to No. 1 came less through dominance than survival. Miami needed a second-overtime touchdown and an interception to hold off rival Florida State 43-35 last weekend, a game in which the Hurricanes allowed a season-high 35 points and 443 total yards. <cite index="24-14,24-15,24-16">After a 43-35 double-overtime escape against Florida State at Hard Rock Stadium on Saturday night — on a day when five of the AP's top seven teams lost — Miami is the No. 1 team in college football.</cite> <cite index="21-9">The wildest and wackiest week of the college football season so far saw five of the top seven teams lose — a first in the AP poll's 90-year history.</cite>

That chaos, as much as anything Miami did on the field, is why the Hurricanes are sitting at the top. <cite index="22-30,22-31">Miami received 1,697 points with 40 first-place votes after receiving 1,594 points and two first-place votes the previous week.</cite> It's a fragile perch. Mario Cristobal downplayed the symbolism of the ranking after beating the Seminoles, <cite index="22-11">saying "I think we have a good enough sample size of 2026 to know that's absolutely insignificant and irrelevant with the changing of the guard from week to week."</cite>

## Pitt's history against No. 1

Historically, this matchup hasn't been competitive when Miami has carried the top ranking. <cite index="70-6,70-7">The Hurricanes are 42-6 all-time as the nation's No. 1 team, including 3-0 when facing Pitt as the top-ranked squad — with wins in 1986, 2001 and 2002.</cite> The Panthers' broader history against No. 1 teams is even starker: <cite index="70-11,70-12">Pitt is 0-13-1 all-time when facing teams that were holding the No. 1 ranking at kickoff.</cite> <cite index="70-13,70-14">They haven't played a top-ranked team since facing Miami in 2002, and Pitt has never scored more than 21 points against a team ranked No. 1.</cite>

There is one wrinkle worth noting for anyone tempted to assume this is automatic: Pitt coach Pat Narduzzi has done this before. <cite index="70-16,70-17">Narduzzi does have a win over a No. 2-ranked team during his Pitt tenure — that win coming against Miami in 2017.</cite> Pitt arrives banged up this time, though — the Panthers have dropped back-to-back games and are <cite index="52-13">coming off a 27-21 setback at Boston College and have lost back-to-back games since losing quarterback Mason Heintschel for the season due to a torn ACL.</cite>

## What Miami brings into it

The engine behind Miami's unbeaten start is quarterback Darian Mensah, a Duke transfer who arrived in January and has been nearly turnover-proof. <cite index="61-8,61-9">Mensah will make his seventh start under center vs. Pitt, and he has 18 touchdowns this year compared to just one interception.</cite> Wide receiver Malachi Toney has been his primary weapon, <cite index="66-5,66-6">completing 23 of 34 passes for 326 yards against Florida State while Toney caught 11 passes for 154 yards and two touchdowns.</cite>

None of this settles anything by itself. The first College Football Playoff rankings come out November 3, and Miami still has to get through North Carolina on the road and a trip to Notre Dame in the coming weeks. But for one Saturday, the fifth-year seniors on this roster get to share a stadium with the team that set the standard they've spent two decades chasing — and they get to do it from the top spot.
