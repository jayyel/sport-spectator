Miami's defense played all of Friday night's game at Wake Forest without two starting cornerbacks and a third reserve. For three quarters, it didn't matter. For one, it was the only thing that mattered.

The No. 5 Hurricanes survived a late Wake Forest rally to win 33-20, improving to 3-0 overall and 2-0 in the ACC. The story of the night was supposed to be Darian Mensah, who <cite index="20-14,20-15">opened the game by completing 24 consecutive passes, shattering both the school record and the ACC record while coming within two completions of the FBS mark</cite>. He finished <cite index="21-14">30-of-34 passing for 220 yards with three touchdowns</cite>, and Miami raced to a 31-7 lead <cite index="19-7">midway through the third quarter</cite>.

Then the offense that had looked unstoppable simply stopped. Miami's next four possessions ended in three-and-outs, and Wake Forest quarterback Gio Lopez, who <cite index="24-11,24-12">completed 29 of 46 passes for 312 yards, three touchdowns and one interception, with scoring throws to Kamrean Johnson, Jack Foley and Carlos Hernandez</cite>, dragged the Demon Deacons back within 11 points early in the fourth quarter. That's when the game actually got decided, and it wasn't Mensah who decided it.

## A secondary held together by tape

Miami took the field missing three defensive backs. <cite index="20-5">The Hurricanes defense was down three key players in its secondary — starting cornerbacks Xavier Lucas and OJ Frederique Jr. plus reserve cornerback Ja'Boree Antoine — and still gave up 312 passing yards to Gio Lopez, who completed 29 of 46 passes</cite>. That included <cite index="20-6,20-7">eight passing plays of at least 15 yards, four of at least 20 yards, with nearly half the yardage coming after the catch</cite>.

Those numbers should worry a program that plays Florida State, Clemson and Notre Dame before the season is out. But the response in the fourth quarter is the part worth remembering. With the lead cut to 31-20, <cite index="20-1,20-2">Miami's defense shut down Wake Forest over four full drives in the fourth quarter, with the possessions ending in a safety, a turnover on downs, another turnover on downs, and an interception</cite>. The Hurricanes also <cite index="21-10">got a game-sealing interception from Ethan O'Connor with 2:24 left, after Wake Forest had driven to the Miami 22-yard line</cite>.

Mario Cristobal didn't dress it up afterward. <cite index="19-4">"I think a ton of credit goes to the defense for just bowing up and executing at a high level," Cristobal said.</cite> Mensah, whose own night will get most of the attention, said the same thing without being asked. <cite index="19-9,19-10,19-11">"I have a lot of faith in our defense, they give us some trouble in practice," Mensah said. "So for them to go out there, it's no surprise. Them dudes over there are all ballers."</cite>

## The offense that vanished

What's harder to explain away is how completely Miami's attack disappeared once it had the game in hand. <cite index="24-9,24-10">Miami managed just 13 yards of total offense in the fourth quarter, with seven passing yards and six rushing yards, punting three times and failing to score an offensive point after its touchdown midway through the third quarter.</cite> A team that had been moving the ball at will suddenly couldn't get a first down against a Wake Forest defense that had been overwhelmed for two and a half quarters.

Some of that is injury attrition — Miami dealt with players getting banged up as the game wore on, and depth got tested on both sides of the ball. Some of it is simply what happens when a team builds a 24-point lead and starts playing not to lose instead of playing to finish. Either way, it's the kind of stretch that gets overlooked in a win and dissected in a loss. Miami avoided the loss because its defense, undermanned as it was, refused to let Wake Forest complete the math.

That's the actual lesson from Winston-Salem. The record-setting start matters for what it says about Mensah and an offense that leads the conference in explosive plays. But the game was decided by a defensive front that had every excuse to fold and didn't. Miami hosts Central Michigan next Saturday at Hard Rock Stadium before the schedule turns unforgiving — a trip to Clemson, then Florida State at home in the Florida Cup. If the offense has another quarter like the fourth against Wake Forest in either of those games, the margin for a shorthanded secondary to bail it out again gets a lot thinner.
