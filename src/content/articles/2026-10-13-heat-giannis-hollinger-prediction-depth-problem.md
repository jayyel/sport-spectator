The Miami Heat spent the summer landing the best player to wear their uniform since Dwyane Wade. On Tuesday, one of the most respected analysts in basketball said it's not going to matter.

The Athletic's John Hollinger <cite index="61-10,61-11">predicted the Heat would finish with a record of 40-42, calling it a stunning projection because in head coach Erik Spoelstra's 19 seasons in South Beach, the team has finished under .500 just three times.</cite> That would put Miami outside the top eight in the East, back in the play-in at best, in the same season they acquired a two-time MVP.

The reasoning isn't hand-waving. <cite index="63-9,63-10">Hollinger believes the Heat don't have a two-way wing player who can really help the team, and a lack of playmaking in the backcourt remains a concern as well.</cite> His core complaint is depth: <cite index="63-8">"I don't think the rest of the roster beyond Giannis and Bam Adebayo is anywhere near good enough, and those easy wins in the East will be much harder to come by this season."</cite>

## The Same Problem Pat Riley Didn't Solve

This isn't a team that was fine and got better. <cite index="71-5">The Heat lost in the first stage of the play-in tournament to the Charlotte Hornets in overtime, missing the playoffs for the first time since 2019.</cite> Pat Riley wasn't shy about his reaction. He made clear afterward that he wasn't stepping aside and wasn't interested in a rebuild, and the Giannis trade was the aggressive response.

But the trade came with a cost. <cite index="60-4,60-5,60-6">The Heat did acquire Milwaukee Bucks icon Giannis Antetokounmpo, but in that trade they sacrificed a lot of their depth — to replace players like Jaime Jaquez Jr. and Tyler Herro, they signed veterans Tim Hardaway Jr. and Klay Thompson.</cite> That's a swap of two rotation pieces still in their 20s for two players well past 30. It upgrades the ceiling. It doesn't obviously upgrade the minutes 15 through 25.

## The Spacing Question Nobody's Solved Yet

The more interesting problem is on the floor, not the depth chart. Giannis has spent his career next to centers who stretch defenses out of the paint. Bam Adebayo isn't that player. <cite index="62-10,62-11,62-12">Adebayo has started relying on the long ball more as his career has progressed, attempting a career-high 400 threes last season but making just 31.8 percent of them.</cite> That's not the gravity a 7-foot screener needs to create driving lanes for the league's most dominant slasher.

It's a fixable problem in theory — shooting the ball at a league-average clip instead of a well-below-average one — but it's exactly the kind of structural fit question that doesn't get solved by talent alone, and it's the same question evaluators have been asking since the trade happened in July.

## Preseason Looks Good. That's Not the Test.

None of this has shown up in exhibition games, which is the point — exhibition games don't test it. Giannis has looked comfortable, not forced. <cite index="49-1,49-4">He gave Miami an encouraging first look at its revamped roster in the preseason opener, finishing with nine points, four rebounds and seven assists</cite> against Toronto, then followed with an 18-point half in his home debut against New Orleans. The Heat have also lost a preseason game to Minnesota, the team they'll face to open the regular season on October 21.

LeBron James, who knows exactly what it looks like when a star joins a contender with the pieces already in place, likes what Miami built around its new frontcourt. On his podcast, he predicted Miami's revamped defense would be "damn good," pointing to Wiggins, Adebayo, Giannis and Davion Mitchell as reasons to believe.

That's the bet Riley made: that two historically great two-way players at the top of the roster paper over everything else. Hollinger's projection is a direct challenge to that bet — not that Giannis won't be great, but that everyone below him and Adebayo won't be good enough to turn greatness into wins. The preseason has been encouraging theater. The answer starts October 21, and it won't be settled by one shooting slump or one hot week. It'll be settled by whether the ninth or tenth man on this roster can hit an open three when the defense collapses on the two stars who are actually worth the hype.
