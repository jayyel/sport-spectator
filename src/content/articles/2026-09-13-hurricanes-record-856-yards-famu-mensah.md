No. 7 Miami beat Florida A&M 77-7 on Thursday night at Hard Rock Stadium, and by the time it was over, the box score didn't look like a real football game. The Hurricanes finished with 856 total yards, which broke the previous school record of 750 set against Ball State in 2024. They also tied Miami's all-time scoring mark of 77 points, a number the program had only hit twice before, both times against Savannah State.

The game was so lopsided that both teams agreed to shorten the fourth quarter to 10 minutes instead of 15. By the time that clock started, Miami already led 77-0. Florida A&M's only points came on a 72-yard fumble return with 6:40 left, meaning the Hurricanes' defense didn't allow a single offensive touchdown all night.

## The Record Book Gets Rewritten

<cite index="43-4,43-8">The Hurricanes finished with 856 yards, breaking the mark of 750 against Ball State in 2024, and the team finished with 42 first downs — another school record.</cite> <cite index="51-11,51-12">Miami scored on 11 of its first 12 possessions while the defense held FAMU to 107 total yards and did not surrender an offensive touchdown, extending the program's streak to 20 consecutive home-opening wins.</cite>

Quarterbacks Darian Mensah and Luke Nickel <cite index="43-1,43-3">each threw three touchdown passes in the win, a game so imbalanced that Florida A&M finished 1-2 while Miami improved to 2-0.</cite> The production came from all over the field. <cite index="48-13,48-14">Miami added touchdowns on a 21-yard catch from Vance Spafford and a 52-yard rushing score from Javian Mallory, a freshman from West Boca High School who led the team with 142 rushing yards on the night.</cite>

Cristobal, who picked up his 99th career win as Miami's coach, wasn't interested in framing the night as anything more than a step. <cite index="43-14,43-15,43-16">"Certainly, just wanted to see our guys play fast and free and improve," Cristobal said. "That was the most important thing; going 1-0 is always the goal, but in college football every single week you better get better. In some respects, I think we did that tonight."</cite> Players echoed that after the game, with right tackle Matthew McCoy telling reporters the group wasn't satisfied despite the record-setting margin.

## Mensah's Two-Game Line Is Barely Believable

The headline attraction remains the transfer quarterback Miami pried away from Duke in January. <cite index="49-11,49-12">Mensah completed 14 of 15 passes for 252 yards and three touchdowns in the first half before giving way to reserves, pushing his season total to eight touchdowns against just four incompletions through two games.</cite> He'd opened the season at Stanford by going <cite index="47-12">27-for-30 passing for 401 yards, five touchdowns and no interceptions</cite> in a 45-6 win.

Add it up and Mensah is <cite index="52-6">41-of-45 for 653 yards and 8 touchdowns through two games, without an interception, while having sat out multiple quarters already.</cite> He's done it while spreading the ball around rather than leaning on one target — <cite index="47-9,47-10">he threw passes to eight different receivers against Stanford alone.</cite> Wide receiver Malachi Toney has been the primary beneficiary, catching a school-record 234 yards worth of passes in the opener before adding more production against FAMU.

Asked to explain the numbers, Mensah kept it simple. <cite index="54-3,54-8">"Pretty ridiculous," he said about Miami's offense. "It's just a testament to the guys up front."</cite>

## Wake Forest Is the Actual Test

None of this came against real competition. Florida A&M is an FCS program, and Stanford went 3-9 a year ago. That changes next week. <cite index="51-3,51-4,51-5">Cristobal's team now turns its attention to conference play, hosting Wake Forest on Sept. 18, and the opposition will become more demanding with the margins eventually shrinking.</cite> The game moves to Winston-Salem, where <cite index="55-7,55-8">Miami and Wake Forest kick off at 7:30 p.m. ET on ESPN in what's being billed as the first true litmus test</cite> of how much this Miami team has actually improved from the group that lost the national championship game to Indiana last January.

That's the real value of a night like Thursday. It doesn't tell you much about how good Miami is against teams that belong on the same field. But it does tell you the pieces are there — a clean, efficient quarterback, a receiver room that can do damage after the catch, and a run game deep enough that a true freshman can go for 142 yards without anyone blinking. Whether that translates against an ACC front will start to show up on tape starting next Friday.
