Miami needed double overtime and two overturned touchdowns to beat a 3-3 Florida State team Saturday night. That's the part that should worry the Hurricanes more than the final score suggests.

No. 3 Miami held off the Seminoles 43-35 at Hard Rock Stadium, a back-and-forth rivalry game that <cite index="22-5">featured multiple lead changes, late touchdowns and a game-sealing interception.</cite> The Hurricanes are 6-0 and 4-0 in the ACC. Florida State, which came in unranked and under pressure, leaves Miami Gardens with its 12th straight road loss and nothing to show for a game it was good enough to win.

## A Lead That Kept Changing Hands

Miami looked like it would cruise early. Mark Fletcher Jr. capped <cite index="21-15">an 11-play, 86-yard touchdown drive by scampering in from seven yards for a relatively quick 7-0 lead.</cite> But Florida State answered and then took control in the second quarter, when defensive back Ashlynd Barker <cite index="22-9">intercepted Mensah in the end zone, handing the Miami quarterback his first interception of the season.</cite> The Seminoles turned that into a 21-14 lead behind quarterback Ashton Daniels, who threw for 298 yards and four touchdowns on the night.

Florida State's best player down the stretch was receiver Duce Robinson, who <cite index="25-11">had a pair of TD catches late, including a 75-yarder that tied the game with 2:49 left in regulation</cite> and another to open the first overtime that put FSU ahead 35-28. Miami answered both times. Javian Mallory, who finished with <cite index="25-9">108 yards and two touchdowns, including one in the first OT,</cite> broke a 25-yard touchdown run to force a second overtime. In the second OT, Mensah found Malachi Toney for a short touchdown, and Toney threw a two-point conversion pass to Cooper Barkate for the final margin.

Toney was the best player on the field. He finished with <cite index="25-6">11 catches for 154 yards and two scores, plus threw a 2-point conversion pass to Cooper Barkate</cite> after his own overtime score. Mensah completed <cite index="25-3,25-4">23 of 34 passes for Miami, which has now won seven of its last 10 games against the Seminoles.</cite>

## Two Calls Decided the Finish

The ending turned chaotic. Facing fourth-and-21 in the second overtime, Daniels found a receiver in the end zone for what looked like a game-tying touchdown, but <cite index="26-9">the play was overturned due to an offensive holding call.</cite> On the next snap, facing fourth-and-31, Daniels heaved another jump ball into the end zone that Robinson appeared to catch, but <cite index="26-10,26-11">it was ruled an interception on the field, and the play was reviewed and upheld.</cite> Bryce Fitzgerald came down with the ball, and Miami stormed the field. It was <cite index="28-5">the first overtime game between Miami and FSU since 2004.</cite>

Florida State's frustration afterward was obvious. Daniels said of the sequence, <cite index="27-4">"I thought it was a touchdown ... but we're not the ones out there calling the shots."</cite>

## A Chaotic Weekend Puts Miami in the No. 1 Conversation

The result matters beyond the rivalry trophy. This was one of the wildest Saturdays of the season nationally — Georgia lost to Auburn, Notre Dame lost at BYU, Ohio State ended Indiana's 22-game winning streak, and Alabama and Texas both took damaging losses. With that much carnage at the top, Miami's survival act counts for a lot. One national outlet's early Week 8 projection had Miami jumping to No. 1 with Ohio State at No. 2, while others had Ohio State or BYU ahead of the Hurricanes — but every version has Miami in the top three.

That's the case for this team right now: it is undefeated, it has a Heisman-caliber quarterback in Mensah and a go-to receiver in Toney, and it just proved it can win an ugly game against a bad opponent. The case against it is exactly what happened for three quarters Saturday — a defense that let Florida State hang 35 points and two deep touchdowns in the final minutes, and a team that needed a disputed fourth-and-31 interception to escape at home. Miami carries both of those truths into Pittsburgh next week, still unbeaten, still unproven against anyone with real quality depth at receiver.
