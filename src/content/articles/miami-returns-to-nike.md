The University of Miami is returning to Nike. According to a report from the Miami Herald's Barry Jackson on Wednesday, later confirmed by the Associated Press through a source, the Hurricanes have agreed to a 10-year apparel deal worth more than $200 million that takes effect with the 2027 football season. It ends a twelve-year run with adidas and reunites Miami with the brand that outfitted the program for nearly three decades before.

It is a genuinely big deal. It is also, for one very specific reason involving a 2001 throwback jersey, about to create one of the stranger uniform situations college football has seen in a while. We'll get to that.

## The Numbers

The Nike deal is worth roughly $20 million a year, with about two-thirds of that in cash — around $13 million annually — and the rest in apparel and product. That's a steep jump from the adidas agreement it replaces, which paid Miami about $6.5 million a year in cash. Call it close to a threefold raise.

adidas didn't walk away quietly. Multiple reports say the company made a counteroffer and simply couldn't match Nike's terms. That's a notable reversal from 2015, when adidas pried Miami away from Nike with what was, at the time, the richest deal the athletic department had ever signed.

Here's where the new deal sits among the biggest apparel contracts in college sports:

| School | Brand | Total | Length | Per Year |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Penn State | adidas | $300M | 10 yrs | $30.0M |
| **Miami** | **Nike** | **$200M+** | **10 yrs** | **$20.0M** |
| Texas | Nike | $250M | 15 yrs | $16.7M |
| Ohio State | Nike | $250M | 15 yrs | $16.6M |
| Tennessee | adidas | $100M | 10 yrs | $10.0M |
| Oregon | Nike | $88M | 11 yrs | $8.0M |

A quick note on that table, because it's worth being precise: this is not the biggest apparel deal in college sports. Penn State signed a 10-year, $300 million deal with adidas earlier this year, and on a per-year basis it clears Miami comfortably. But among current deals, Miami's annual value trails only Penn State — and it lands the Hurricanes back in a Nike stable that already includes Texas, Ohio State, Clemson, Georgia, Oregon, Alabama, and LSU. In raw yearly dollars, Miami is right at the top of that group.

The money matters beyond the jerseys. Miami's NIL spending is reportedly approaching $50 million a year, with new facilities on the horizon, and an apparel deal at this scale helps underwrite the arms race that Mario Cristobal's rebuild has committed the program to.

## A Reunion, Not a First Date

It's easy to frame this as Miami landing a shiny new partner. It's closer to the truth to call it a homecoming.

Nike first signed Miami in 1987, in what was then a groundbreaking arrangement — the Hurricanes became the first school in the country outfitted head to toe by a single brand across every sport. Nobody had done it before. That deal, and the swagger of the Miami teams that wore it through the late '80s and beyond, helped shape the entire modern model of how schools and apparel companies do business.

That partnership ran until 2015, when Miami left for adidas. Now, after a decade as the adidas flagship program in college athletics, the Hurricanes are heading back to where they started. When the deal kicks in next year, it'll be Miami's 28th year wearing the swoosh across the program's history.

- **1987** — Nike signs Miami, the first all-sport deal of its kind
- **1987–2015** — Twenty-eight seasons in the swoosh
- **2015** — Miami leaves for adidas, then its richest deal ever
- **2026** — The final season in three stripes
- **2027** — Nike returns, at roughly triple the money

## Now, About Those Throwbacks

Here's the part that makes this more than a business story.

Rumors started circulating earlier this week that Miami plans to wear throwback uniforms this season honoring the 2001 national championship team — one of the most beloved squads in program history, celebrating its 25th anniversary. The 2001 Canes are iconic, and so is what they wore.

What they wore was Nike.

The problem is the calendar. The Nike deal doesn't begin until July 1, 2027. The adidas contract runs through June 30, 2027. So for the entire 2026 season — the season of the 25th-anniversary tribute — Miami is still an adidas school. Which means the homage to the most iconic Nike-era uniform in Hurricanes history would be manufactured by adidas, three stripes and all.

| 2001 | 2026 |
|---|---|
| The original kit | The throwback |
| Made by Nike | Made by adidas |

It's the kind of detail that's almost too on-the-nose. A tribute to a Nike era, produced by the brand Miami is about to leave, in the same news cycle that the Nike reunion leaks. If the throwbacks do appear this fall, they'll be a small, strange monument to a transition already in motion.

Of course, there's an obvious fix, and it's the fun one: wait. Nike takes over in 2027 with ten years and every sport on campus to play with. The 2001 look will finally be theirs to reissue properly — the real thing, from the brand that made the original. A 25th-anniversary nod in adidas this year is awkward. A 26th-anniversary "we did it right this time" drop in Nike next year might be the better story anyway.

## What It Signals

Strip away the jersey intrigue and this is another data point in the same trend line: Miami is spending, winning, and being treated like a blue blood again. A national championship appearance put the program in a position of leverage it hasn't held in apparel negotiations for a long time, and Nike — a company that has taken its share of stock-market and brand lumps lately — clearly saw Miami as worth paying up for.

For the fans, the takeaway is simpler. The swoosh is coming home. It's just going to take until 2027 to see it on the field — and there's a very specific throwback we'll all be watching for whenever Nike finally gets its hands on it.
