Miami joined the ACC in 2004 and has never won the conference. Twenty-two years, nine Big East trophies gathering dust, and exactly one trip to the title game, back in 2017, a loss to Clemson. Tonight that drought gets a real chance to end — not with a championship yet, just a ticket to play for one, but after two decades of near-misses, that ticket matters.

No. 7 Miami (9-1, 7-0 ACC) hosts Virginia Tech (6-4, 3-4) at Hard Rock Stadium at 7 p.m. A win clinches Miami's spot in the Dec. 5 ACC Championship Game. So would a Louisville loss to Pittsburgh, since <cite index="24-2">wins by Miami and Wake Forest, or a Louisville loss, will clinch ACC Championship Game berths for the Hurricanes and Demon Deacons</cite>. Either way, Miami controls it. They just have to beat a team they've owned for years — Miami has won six straight in the series, including a 34-17 win in Blacksburg last season, with Virginia Tech's last victory in the matchup coming back in 2019.

## Why GameDay Showed Up for an Unranked Opponent

There's no other ranked-versus-ranked game on the Week 12 board, which is exactly why ESPN's traveling pregame show picked Coral Gables anyway. This is only the third time College GameDay has broadcast from the actual University of Miami campus — after 2017 and again last year for the Florida game — rather than from Hard Rock Stadium itself. Dwayne "The Rock" Johnson, a former Miami defensive tackle, served as guest picker. Pat McAfee and Desmond Howard opened the show by jumping from Miami's 33-foot diving platform into the team pool, a callback to McAfee's solo leap during last year's visit.

The spectacle is real, but it's attached to something concrete: Miami at 9-1 is alone atop the conference, and a victory tonight locks in the second ACC title game appearance in program history. <cite index="26-7">The Hurricanes maintained their position in Tuesday's College Football Playoff rankings after a 36-25 victory over Duke</cite>, and they enter tonight ranked seventh in the latest committee rankings, with Wake Forest expected to be waiting in Charlotte if both programs hold up their ends.

## The Toney Question and the Fletcher Workload

Miami's only loss this season came at Notre Dame, a game receiver Malachi Toney missed entirely. <cite index="25-11">Head coach Mario Cristobal's team's only loss came on the road against Notre Dame without superstar receiver Malachi Toney, who could be back for the ACC Championship if Miami is partaking in the contest.</cite> That's the subtext of tonight: beat Virginia Tech, and Miami doesn't just get a trophy chase, it potentially gets its best offensive weapon back for it.

In the meantime, quarterback Darian Mensah has kept the offense humming without him. Running back Mark Fletcher Jr. has carried the load all year, piling up 537 yards and double-digit touchdowns on the ground through nine games, and he's coming off a three-touchdown performance against Duke even while banged up in the second half of that game.

Virginia Tech arrives on a two-game losing streak under first-year head coach James Franklin, and the Hokies' defense has been allowing yardage at a rate that should let Miami's offense operate the way it has all season. The worry for Miami isn't talent, it's complacency — a trap game dressed up as a coronation, with a laid-back Hokies program that already has nothing left to play for except spoiling a party.

## What Actually Changes Tonight

A win doesn't deliver the ACC championship. It delivers a chance at one, against Wake Forest or whoever else survives, on Dec. 5 in Charlotte. But for a program that has spent every year since 2004 explaining why this particular trophy has eluded it despite five national championships in the case, a chance is no small thing. <cite index="51-17,51-18">The Hurricanes spent nearly 20 years in rebuilding mode before having a breakthrough in 2024. Last year they made it to the CFP title game, losing to the Indiana Hoosiers.</cite> They didn't even make the ACC title game that year. Tonight is about not letting that happen twice.
