The Heat needed four games to find their first win of the season. Bam Adebayo needed one rebound more than seven to find his name at the top of a list that had belonged to Udonis Haslem since 2012.

Both happened at the same moment, in the same building, on the same stat sheet. Miami beat Atlanta 129-117 at State Farm Arena on Wednesday night, and <cite index="43-2,43-3">Bam Adebayo became the franchise's career leader in rebounds during the win, while Giannis Antetokounmpo recorded 24 points and 18 rebounds in his first win with the team.</cite> It was the Heat's first victory since trading for Antetokounmpo over the summer, and it arrived after <cite index="56-5">starting 0-4 for the first time since the 2007-08 season.</cite>

## A record nobody saw coming this early

Haslem spent 20 seasons in Miami building a reputation as a locker-room constant more than a stat-sheet force, and <cite index="42-7,42-8">it took Haslem 20 seasons to get to his total, but he essentially spent the final eight seasons of his career as a designated locker room leader who rarely got minutes.</cite> Adebayo got there in his tenth year. He grabbed nine boards against the Hawks, pushing his career total past Haslem's mark. <cite index="43-5">He surpassed former captain Udonis Haslem, who recorded 5,791 rebounds over a 20-season career.</cite>

The two have a real relationship, not just a shared locker room. Haslem was around for Adebayo's entire rise, from a rookie out of Kentucky to a two-time All-Star anchoring Miami's defense. <cite index="41-6,41-7">"The person I'm passing has been so close to me throughout my 10 years in this career, from the time I walked in until now," Adebayo said</cite> in his postgame interview, as a Heat staffer set aside the game ball for him.

Spoelstra, who has coached both men, didn't treat it as a footnote. <cite index="41-8,41-9,41-10,41-11">"It was a really special moment in the locker room," Heat coach Erik Spoelstra said. "He's done it with an iron will, a work ethic that's unmatched. That's a lot of rebounds in traffic, getting hit, banged in there. To be able to do it and break the record of his brother, UD, that's a really special moment."</cite>

Context matters here. Adebayo is not some undersized guard racking up garbage boards. He's spent a decade as Miami's most reliable low-post presence, and the number now attached to his name puts him in rare company. <cite index="41-12,41-13,41-14,41-15,41-16,41-17,41-18">Adebayo is No. 2 on Miami's scoring list behind Hall of Famer Dwyane Wade, No. 3 in assists, No. 3 in steals and No. 5 in blocked shots. Only nine players have grabbed more rebounds since 2017-18, Adebayo's first season in the NBA after the Heat took him with the No. 14 pick out of Kentucky in the 2017 draft.</cite>

## The actual fix was simpler than anyone expected

The record was the emotional centerpiece, but the basketball reason Miami finally won a game was more mundane: Spoelstra stopped trying to force two ball-dominant bigs to share the same space at the same time. The pick-and-roll returned, with Antetokounmpo embracing life as a roll man and settling into the dunker spot when he wasn't the one with the ball. That adjustment paid off on the glass immediately. Antetokounmpo grabbed a season-high 18 rebounds, five of them offensive, and Adebayo found his footing on offense for the first time all year, finishing with 24 points on 8-of-16 shooting.

The box score reflected a team playing complementary basketball rather than two stars circling each other. <cite index="54-12">Andrew Wiggins scored 18 points and Pelle Larsson added 17 points, eight assists and six rebounds for the Heat, who shot 53% from the field and had a whopping 70 points in the paint.</cite> Miami built its lead early and never let the Hawks seriously threaten it, opening with a hot shooting quarter and controlling the glass by a wide margin for the night.

Even Atlanta's coaching staff noticed the difference in size and physicality. Hawks coach Quin Snyder acknowledged after the game that <cite index="57-2,57-3">Miami out-rebounded the Hawks in their win, dominating the battle of the glass 52-40, with Giannis Antetokounmpo leading the way with an impressive 18 boards.</cite>

## One win, same questions

Nobody in the building was pretending this solves everything. Miami is now 1-4, which is still one of the worst starts in franchise history, and the roster adjustment to pairing two players who each need the ball in their hands remains a work in progress. Spoelstra himself has said publicly that the shooters around Antetokounmpo need to find pockets where they can stay aggressive without disappearing when the ball goes to him. Friday brings a quick turnaround against Indiana in NBA Cup group play, a chance to find out whether Wednesday's formula was a real fix or a one-night correction against a shorthanded opponent.

But for a franchise that has spent two decades defined by continuity between its great players, watching one era's defining rebounder hand the torch to the next felt like more than a footnote to an ugly start. Haslem's number stood for 14 years. It took Adebayo exactly the kind of game Miami has needed all season to finally get past it.
