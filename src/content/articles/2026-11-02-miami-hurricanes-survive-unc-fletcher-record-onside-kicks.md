Miami is still undefeated. That's the headline. But if you watched the final 98 seconds in Chapel Hill on Saturday night, the undefeated part felt almost incidental to what actually happened at the end.

The No. 1 Hurricanes beat North Carolina 38-35 on Halloween night, a game that <cite index="23-7">stayed a back-and-forth shootout before the undefeated 8-0 season survived</cite>. Miami is 6-0 in conference play. North Carolina dropped to 4-4. Those numbers will go in the record book. What people are actually arguing about is a pair of onside kicks that UNC recovered — twice — only to have both wiped out by penalties.

## A finish that split the sport

Down three points with under two minutes left, <cite index="30-5,30-6">North Carolina recovered two consecutive onside kicks with 98 seconds remaining, and officials erased both recoveries with penalties</cite>. The first flag came on Shamar Easter for lining up offside before the kick. UNC had to retry from five yards back, recovered that one too, and then a second flag landed — an illegal block on linebacker Peyton Seelmann. On the third attempt, Miami finally came up with the ball and ran out the clock.

Both calls were defensible on replay. <cite index="32-11,32-12,32-13">On the first kick, officials flagged Easter for being offside, which NCAA rules require kicking-team players to avoid before the ball is touched.</cite> Seelmann himself didn't dispute the second one afterward, admitting he'd made contact before the ball had traveled the required ten yards. But the optics — a top-ranked team needing two separate bailouts in under two minutes to protect a national championship push — were enough to reignite an officiating debate that had already followed Miami out of its double-overtime escape against Florida State two weeks earlier, when <cite index="30-7,30-8">a holding penalty on Xavier Chaplin wiped out a 36-yard touchdown pass on fourth-and-21 in that game's second overtime</cite>.

Belichick, for his part, didn't go scorched earth on the officials. Asked directly about the calls, he said simply that <cite index="34-4,34-5,34-6">they were "probably about right," adding that both looked close to him</cite>. Seelmann was more candid about his own penalty, admitting <cite index="34-13,34-14">he went out to hit the Miami player before the ball had gone ten yards, calling it "a little too early."</cite> That's about as honest a postgame admission as you'll get from a player who just lost a shot at the biggest win of his season.

What actually set up the chaos was a UNC turnover that nobody's arguing about. With Miami up 31-28 and under six minutes left, <cite index="36-11">defensive lineman Marquise Lightfoot stripped quarterback Billy Edwards Jr. from behind, and Justin Scott recovered at the North Carolina 37-yard line</cite>. Miami converted the takeaway into the touchdown that turned a one-score game into a two-score cushion — the only reason UNC needed two onside kicks instead of one.

## Fletcher's name is alone at the top now

Buried under the finish was a legitimate piece of program history. Senior running back Mark Fletcher Jr. caught a 31-yard touchdown pass in the second quarter — the longest reception of his career — and in doing so moved past a tie he'd shared with two Miami legends. <cite index="26-4,26-7">Fletcher's career total touched 36, passing Stephen McGuire and Edgerrin James, who had shared the mark at 35 apiece since McGuire's run from 1989-92 and James's from 1996-98.</cite> He added a short touchdown run later that also pulled him level with McGuire atop Miami's all-time rushing touchdown list.

Fletcher didn't need a huge rushing night to do it — <cite index="21-10">he finished with 43 yards on 13 carries to go with his three total touchdowns</cite>. A four-star recruit out of American Heritage who stuck with the program through every season of his career rather than transfer, Fletcher's record now sits entirely on his own, more than three decades after it was set.

Darian Mensah did his part too, going <cite index="23-1">22 of 29 for 274 yards and three touchdowns with no interceptions</cite>, steering an offense that put up 416 total yards against a North Carolina defense that had actually given Miami real trouble for most of the night.

## Why this one matters more than the score suggests

Miami remains No. 1 heading into a November that includes a trip to Notre Dame, and this game is exactly the kind of result that keeps committee members nervous rather than reassured. A team that needs a defensive takeaway and two separately-penalized onside kicks to survive an unranked opponent on the road isn't playing like a team that's pulling away from the field. The record book moment for Fletcher is real and worth celebrating. But the tape from Chapel Hill is going to get rewatched for the flags, not the touchdowns, and that's the version of this win Miami actually has to answer for against tougher competition down the stretch.
