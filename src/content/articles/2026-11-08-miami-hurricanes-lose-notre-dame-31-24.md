Miami's unbeaten season ended Saturday night in South Bend, and it ended the way these things usually do — not with one bad play, but with a pile of them that the Hurricanes couldn't out-talent their way through. Notre Dame beat No. 1 Miami 31-24, dropping the Hurricanes to 8-1 and ending a run that had them sitting atop both the polls and the first College Football Playoff rankings of the season.

This was supposed to be Miami's signature road win, the one that validated a Heisman-caliber quarterback and a defense that had shut down everything in front of it. Instead, the Hurricanes found themselves down 17-3 at halftime after scoring at least 14 points and averaging 27 first-half points through their first eight games. <cite index="20-12">The Hurricanes wound up trailing 17-3 at halftime, after scoring at least 14 points and averaging 27 first-half points in their first eight contests this season.</cite>

## A Game Miami Couldn't Have Scripted Worse

The absence hanging over everything was Malachi Toney, Miami's star receiver and a Heisman hopeful in his own right, who didn't play. Without him, the offense that had been the best scoring unit in the country looked lost for most of the first half. <cite index="24-7,24-8">The win came down to the final minute because of a late surge by the Hurricanes' explosive offense, which entered No. 1 in scoring but looked anemic in the first half without injured star wideout Malachi Toney.</cite>

Notre Dame, meanwhile, got exactly the game it needed. Quarterback CJ Carr threw for 319 yards and three touchdowns, and the Irish built a 24-3 cushion early in the third quarter before Miami's offense finally woke up. <cite index="24-3,24-4">Quarterback CJ Carr completed 29 of 40 passes for 319 yards for the Fighting Irish, who hung on after jumping out to a 24-3 lead. Micah Gilbert caught nine passes for 98 yards and two touchdowns, and Jordan Faison added six catches for 77 yards.</cite>

The turning point of the night, at least in Mario Cristobal's mind, came well before any of that. With first-and-goal from the Notre Dame 1-yard line in the second quarter, Miami ran it three straight times — two Darian Mensah keepers sandwiched around a Mark Fletcher Jr. carry — and came away with nothing but a field goal. <cite index="20-11">Miami had first-and-goal from the Notre Dame 1 in the second quarter and wound up settling for a field goal, after three runs — one by Fletcher and two keepers by Mensah — went nowhere.</cite> That's four points left on the field in a game Miami lost by seven.

## Mensah's Human Night

Darian Mensah had entered the week near the top of every Heisman list, completing passes at a rate nobody else in the country could match. Saturday was the first time all season he looked ordinary. He finished 24 of 43 for 302 yards and three touchdowns, but he also threw his first interception since the opener, a pick to Notre Dame's Luke Talich that killed a drive when Miami was trying to tie the game in the third quarter. <cite index="24-10">The Hurricanes stayed close the rest of the way behind Heisman Trophy favorite Darian Mensah, who completed 22 of 40 passes for 285 yards and three touchdowns (but also threw only his second interception of the season).</cite>

Even that wasn't the backbreaker. Miami actually pulled within a touchdown twice in the second half, the last time at 31-24 with under ten minutes left on a Mensah-to-Cooper Barkate score. But the final gut punch came on what looked like a go-ahead touchdown to tight end Eli Lofton, who caught a ball in the corner of the end zone on fourth down late in the fourth quarter — only for officials to rule he didn't maintain possession. <cite index="3-5,3-6,3-1">Miami faced a fourth-and-six at the Notre Dame 18-yard-line. Mensah found tight end Eli Lofton in the corner of the end zone, but the tight end couldn't add to his seven-catch, 113-yard and one-touchdown day. Lofton couldn't maintain possession of the ball and Miami turned the ball over on downs with 3:36 to play.</cite> Cristobal said afterward he was "beyond upset" the play wasn't ruled a touchdown, and it's hard to blame him — it was the last real look Miami got. <cite index="20-10">Miami coach Mario Cristobal said he was "beyond upset" that Lofton's play wasn't ruled a score.</cite>

## What This Actually Costs Miami

Losing the No. 1 ranking stings less than losing the head-to-head cushion. Miami now joins the pack of one-loss contenders jockeying for playoff position, while just two FBS teams — Utah and UCLA — remain unbeaten. <cite index="31-14">The loss knocks Miami from the unbeaten ranks, leaving just Utah and UCLA with zeros in the loss column.</cite> Notre Dame, for its part, got the signature win it needed and is now positioned to climb back into the playoff conversation it fell out of a year ago.

None of this is fatal for Miami. A 12-team field still has room for a one-loss ACC contender, especially one that just took the eventual No. 10 or 11 seed down to the final minute on the road. But the margin for error that came with being undefeated is gone. Miami still has Duke, Virginia Tech and Boston College left, all at home, and likely needs to win out to control its own playoff fate. The red-zone discipline that failed them at the Notre Dame 1-yard line can't fail them again.
