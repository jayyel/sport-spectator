The Miami Dolphins have played two NFL games this season and recorded zero sacks. Not two, not one. Zero. They are the only team in the league that can say that after Week 2, and on Sunday they host a Kansas City Chiefs team that's off to a 2-0 start behind Patrick Mahomes.

That's the matchup at Hard Rock Stadium for Miami's home opener, delayed to Week 3 by a schedule that sent the Dolphins to Las Vegas and San Francisco to start the year. They return 0-2, having been outscored by an average of 18 points a game, and their most glaring problem isn't hard to find.

## The Numbers Are Ugly

<cite index="39-1,39-2">Miami's pass rush has struggled to get going through two games, ranking 26th in pass rush win rate and last in pressures while remaining the only team in the NFL without a sack.</cite> The Dolphins managed just seven total pressures across the two games, a number that would be alarming for a single half against most offenses, let alone two full games.

Jeff Hafley, in his first season as head coach and doubling as the team's defensive play-caller, isn't hiding from it. <cite index="40-10,40-11">"It hasn't worked in the first two games," Hafley said of the pass rush, adding, "I've coached a lot of defense and done a lot of different things."</cite> He's said he intends to keep tinkering with fronts, blitz packages and personnel until something clicks, because right now nothing has.

Some context makes the number less absurd but no less concerning. <cite index="40-8,40-9">The NFL record for fewest sacks in a 16- or 17-game season is 10, set by the Chiefs back in 2008, while the Dolphins' own franchise low is 16, set by the expansion 1966 team.</cite> Miami isn't walking away with either record. But two games of nothing is not how you get to double digits by January, and the schedule isn't offering a get-well opponent this week.

## Robinson's Back, Mahomes Isn't Waiting

The Dolphins' best hope for jump-starting the rush returns from the concussion protocol. Chop Robinson, the 2023 first-round pick who's been in and out of concussion protocol multiple times over the past year, missed the San Francisco game and is now expected to play Sunday. <cite index="37-9">He's produced 10 sacks in 33 games for his career</cite>, but his season opener against Las Vegas was quiet — <cite index="37-9">just one tackle in 42 defensive snaps.</cite>

Whatever Miami cooks up schematically, the opponent makes it harder. Kansas City brings a rebuilt identity this year, not just Mahomes slinging it. <cite index="48-11,48-12">Hafley acknowledged the challenge, noting that Kansas City's offensive line is solid and that Kenneth Walker III, one of the Chiefs' newest backs, is already leading the league in rushing.</cite> Load the box to slow Walker and Mahomes picks you apart; drop extra bodies into coverage and there's no pressure to make him uncomfortable. It's the exact bind a pass rush with zero sacks is built to lose.

Linebacker Jordyn Brooks framed the challenge in blunter terms, saying the priority is simply tackling Walker before he breaks free, calling him someone who is <cite index="48-13,48-14">"breaking a bunch of tackles and very shifty."</cite> Rookie linebacker Jacob Rodriguez was even more direct about what's owed to the fans in the building for the first time this season: <cite index="48-16,48-17">the team knows it must play better, saying, "We got to play a lot better, and we got to give them something to cheer for."</cite>

## Why This Matters More Than the Record

An 0-2 start during a stated rebuild isn't itself a crisis — Miami tore down its roster this offseason and everyone from the front office to the fan base understood there would be losses. But a defense with no ability to affect the quarterback caps how competitive this team can be no matter who's healthy on offense. Chris Bell is dealing with knee soreness but plans to play Sunday, and the receiving corps has its own questions with Caleb Douglas banged up. None of that matters much if Mahomes has all day.

Sunday isn't really about the win-loss column for a team already bracing for a long year. It's about whether Hafley's scheme changes produce a single sack against a quarterback who's historically excelled at avoiding exactly that. If Miami can't get home against Kansas City's line, the zero has real staying power — and so does the conversation about just how far this defense has to go.
