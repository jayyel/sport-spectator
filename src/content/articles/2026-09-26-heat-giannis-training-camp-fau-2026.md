The Heat are heading back to Boca Raton. The team announced this week that training camp will run September 29 through October 2 at Florida Atlantic University, closed to the public, before the group breaks camp and opens the preseason October 3 against Toronto in Quebec City. It's the seventh time in franchise history Miami has used FAU for camp, and the most consequential by a wide margin, because this is the first time Giannis Antetokounmpo puts on a Heat jersey in front of a coaching staff that has to figure out what to do with him.

Everything before this — the trade in June, the introductory press conference in July, the parade of transactions since — was theory. Camp is where it becomes a basketball team.

## A roster nobody would recognize from last spring

Start with what's gone. Miami's only All-Star from a year ago, Norman Powell, left in free agency. So did Tyler Herro, Jaime Jaquez Jr., Kel'el Ware and Kasparas Jakučionis, all shipped to Milwaukee in the deal that brought back Antetokounmpo and Bobby Portis. That's five rotation players out the door in a single offseason, which is not how contending teams usually operate, but Pat Riley wasn't building for continuity. He was building around one guy.

In their place: Portis, Klay Thompson on a buyout deal after his release in Dallas, Tim Hardaway Jr. on a one-year, $6 million contract after finishing as a Sixth Man of the Year finalist in Denver, Nick Richards, and rookie Ryan Conwell. <cite index="58-13,58-14,58-15,58-16">Miami let Norman Powell, its only All-Star last season, sign with Chicago then filled in its roster with veterans on short money. Tim Hardaway Jr., a Sixth Man of the Year finalist in Denver last season, took one year and $6 million. Klay Thompson arrived in late August after a buyout in Dallas. Nick Richards signed a few days later.</cite> The Heat also quietly locked up their own building block before he hit restricted free agency: <cite index="65-2,65-3">guard Pelle Larsson agreed to a new four-year, $60 million contract extension, with a mutual option for the 2030-31 season, avoiding restricted free agency next summer.</cite>

The fifteenth roster spot remains open on purpose. The buyout market in February tends to be more useful than what's left in free agency in September, and holding the spot costs Miami nothing.

## The fit question nobody can answer until pads are on

Every projection of Miami's starting five lands in roughly the same place: Davion Mitchell, Thompson, Andrew Wiggins, Antetokounmpo and Bam Adebayo. That's a group built to switch everything and protect the rim — <cite index="55-12,55-13">the Heat has already finished with one of the NBA's top-10 defensive ratings in nine of the past 11 regular seasons, and Antetokounmpo has been named to five All-NBA Defensive teams during his career and won Defensive Player of the Year for 2019-20.</cite> Nobody around the team doubts that end of the floor will translate immediately.

The other end is the actual experiment. <cite index="55-9,55-10">Adebayo and Antetokounmpo do most of their damage offensively inside the three-point line, which raises real questions about their frontcourt fit.</cite> Spoelstra has spent his career solving spacing problems with shooting on the perimeter, and this roster is thinner there than it looks on paper — Thompson is 36, Hardaway is 34, and beyond Antetokounmpo there isn't another player on the roster who reliably creates a shot from nothing. That's the tension camp exists to work through: how much you stagger the two bigs so one is always playing center, and how much shooting you can surround them with before the offense clogs.

There's also Antetokounmpo's own timeline to track. He's eligible to sign an extension starting October 1, but the more likely date is January, when he becomes eligible for a four-year max worth up to $275 million. Camp won't resolve that, but every practice between now and then is effectively an audition for both sides — Miami proving it built something worth staying for, Antetokounmpo deciding whether it has.

## Media day comes first

Before any of the on-court work, the Heat hold their traditional media day Monday at Kaseya Center — the first extended chance for Antetokounmpo, Adebayo, Riley and Spoelstra to sit in front of cameras together and describe what they think this is. Then it's straight to Boca Raton, where a two-time MVP will run through his first practices in a Miami uniform. The Heat haven't been favorites for anything in years. This is the closest thing they've had to a fresh start since 2010, and it starts on a college campus, not in front of a home crowd.
