Miami's first offensive snap of the 2026 season was a 48-yard catch and run from Darian Mensah to Malachi Toney. Four plays later the Hurricanes were in the end zone, and the shape of the night was already set.

The seventh-ranked Hurricanes beat Stanford 45-6 on Friday at Stanford Stadium, opening ACC play with a result that surprised nobody and a performance that still managed to. Mensah, making his Miami debut, finished 27 of 30 for 401 yards and five touchdowns with no interceptions — more scoring passes than incompletions, and career highs in both yardage and touchdowns.

Toney was the other half of it. He caught passes for 234 yards, a Miami single-game record, and scored on the first two drives before a 47-yard connection in the third quarter pushed him past the mark. Stanford spent much of the evening in man coverage against him, which is a defensible plan against most receivers and was not one against this one.

## What the night actually established

Mario Cristobal said in the week leading up that the game had held the program's attention since January, and the reference point is obvious enough: Miami lost the national championship to Indiana eight months ago and came into this season the preseason favorite in the ACC. Openers against teams picked last in the conference don't resolve much. This one resolved the passing game.

The margins tell the story of a team doing what it should. Miami led 14-3 after the first quarter and 28-6 at halftime, built on a methodical ten-play, 75-yard drive and an eight-play, 89-yard drive capped by a five-yard Charmar Brown touchdown run. New kicker Jake Weinberg added a 46-yarder late. Stanford quarterback Davis Warren threw for 214 yards on 21 of 41 and never got the Cardinal into the end zone, a week after leading them to a win over Hawaii.

## The part worth worrying about

The run blocking was not good. Stanford committed to stopping it, which is the correct response to a team that just lost a national title game and returned this much at receiver, but the offensive line did not consistently win those reps. Against Wake Forest and Clemson and Florida State, that becomes a problem rather than a footnote.

The defense was fine and also unexamined. Miami lost Rueben Bain Jr. and Akheem Mesidor to the NFL from a 13-3 team, and what returns — Ahmad Moten Sr. inside, Damon Wilson II off the edge, Xavier Lucas and Bryce Fitzgerald in the back end — is talented enough that the expectation is another top-tier unit. Stanford was not the test of that. Neither is Florida A&M this Thursday at Hard Rock.

The real measuring sticks are further out. What Friday bought Miami was the least complicated version of the season's first question: the quarterback is going to be fine.
